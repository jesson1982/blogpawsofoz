The Build-Up Season Test: Humidity, Red Dust and a Back Seat That Cleans Up

By Tom RileySep 1, 2026Dog Car Seat Covers6 min read

Short answer: the build-up kills covers slowly rather than tearing them, separating a cover from its foam backing and growing smells no detailer will promise to remove. What survives is a waterproof-backed cover you can pull out and wash, plus a fifteen-minute weekly routine.

Every Territory local knows the build-up. September goes tense, October goes green, and by November the humidity sits on the town like a wet dog, which is appropriate, because my back seat is where the whole phenomenon gets audited. Heat plus moisture plus a dog that swims at every creek crossing is a laboratory of rot, and the build-up has eaten more car gear for me than corrugations ever did. This is what survives it, what dies in it, and the fifteen-minute routine that keeps a dog back seat from turning into a terrarium.

What the build-up actually does to a cabin

Humidity is the enemy and the heat is its accomplice. Everything absorbent in the cabin, foam, carpet, quilted covers, pulls moisture from the air overnight, then the sun cooks it, and that cycle grows smells the way Territory lawns grow grass. Add wet-dog delivery runs from the creek and you have a microbiology project with a headrest. The failure I see most is not tearing; it is the slow divorce between a cover and its foam backing as the adhesive gives up in the damp, followed by the smell that no detailer will promise to remove.

The fabrics that handled it and the ones that didn't

Quilted foam covers were the first casualties, for the reason above: foam absorbs, adhesive fails, smell moves in and never pays rent. Canvas, ironically, thrives, as long as it dries, because tight weave sheds moisture and holds structure. The mesh-and-textile hybrid covers surprised me: they hold less water than quilted foam and dry in an hour of air con, though they sacrifice toughness against nails. Waterproof shells went exactly as they do every October, turning the back seat into a soup pot from the dog's underside down, and they were retired for the season before Christmas.

The routine: fifteen minutes, three times a week

The system is boring and it is the whole answer. After every swim run, towels on the seat and the dog towelled before it loads, which sounds fussy until you price the alternative. Every second evening, cover off and hung in the carport where the afternoon breeze actually moves; a cover drying flat on the seat is a cover fermenting. Sunday, the full reset: vacuum the bench and floor, wipe the hard plastics, hose the cover if the week was muddy, and leave everything open to the breeze until bone dry. Then the tell I check weekly: the sniff test with the windows closed for an hour. If a closed cabin smells like dog in an hour, the routine has slipped, because a dry cabin closed for an hour should smell like nothing at all.

  • Towel the dog and the seat after every swim run.
  • Hang the cover in the carport every second evening.
  • Vacuum, wipe plastics and hose the cover on Sundays.
  • Run the closed-cabin sniff test once each week.

Gear decisions the climate forces on you

Three upgrades earn their keep up here. A raised platform or extenders that hold the dog off the bench seat, because airflow under the dog beats any fabric choice, and a wet bench under a dry dog rots upward. Rubber floor mats, since carpet is the build-up's favourite victim and mud's favourite canvas. And the humble stack of cheap cotton towels that lives in the tub with the spare wheel, because the difference between a manageable cabin and a swamp is whether the towel is within reach when the dog exits the creek three metres ahead of schedule. None of this is glamorous. The build-up does not reward glamour; it rewards drainage.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I get wet dog smell out of a car cabin for good?

Find the sponge first, because the smell is never the air, it is a surface holding moisture: foam cover, carpet, headliner edge. Strip the cabin, dry everything properly for a full day, then run the closed-cabin sniff test to find what still offends. Enzyme cleaners on fabrics, never perfume sprays, and fix the routine that let it soak in the first place, or you are just seasoning the next round.

Should I leave windows cracked when parked in the build-up?

A crack helps airflow and does little for heat, so it is worth doing for dryness, not for temperature. In the dry season I leave a decent gap; in the wet I watch the sky, because a cracked window and an afternoon storm equals a swamp with a head start. Security matters too: nothing valuable visible, and if the parking spot is dicey, close up and accept the hour of air con on the drive home as the drying cycle.

Is canvas or mesh better for a dog that swims daily?

Mesh for the daily swimmer, canvas for the tourer. Mesh dries in about an hour and does not hold the swamp, which is the daily battle won; canvas is tougher under claws and corrugations but needs half a day of shade to dry. My own setup is canvas through the tourist season and the mesh hybrid swapped in from October, because the seasons demand different gear and the swap takes ten minutes.

Do I need a platform for the dog, or is that overkill?

In the build-up it is the single best upgrade, and it is not close. Lifting the dog off the bench keeps air moving under and around it, keeps it off the wettest surface in the cabin, and turns mud management from a cushion problem into a wipe-down problem. For big dogs the comfort gain on long corrugated legs is real too. The only cost is losing the footwell for a second dog or your groceries.

What about mould in the car's own carpet and headliner?

Attack it early because mould owns fabric permanently once established. Strip and dry the cabin with doors open in full shade for a day, vacuum with a brush head, wipe hard surfaces with a diluted vinegar solution, and treat any visible colonies on carpet with an enzyme or mould spray used sparingly. If the smell returns within a week of all that, the colony is under the carpet or in the foam, and that is an upholstery-out job no spray will fake its way through.
