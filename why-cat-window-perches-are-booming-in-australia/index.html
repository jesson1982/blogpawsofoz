Why Cat Window Perches Are Booming in Australia

By Marcus HaleSep 2, 2026Cat Window Perches5 min read

Cat window perches have moved from a niche import stocked by a few online sellers to one of the most visible categories in Australian cat gear, and the growth is structural rather than a fad. Industry estimates over the last few years point to cat ownership rising while renting and apartment living expand, and window perches sit exactly where those trends intersect: cheap, space-free, no-drill enrichment for cats in homes where a cat tree won't fit and a wall bracket isn't allowed. Here's what's actually driving the category, and what the signals say about where it's heading.

What the category looked like five years ago

Half a decade back, the Australian market offered essentially one product shape: a basic suction hammock, mostly through marketplace imports, with limited weight ratings and no local warranty story. Today the same shelf space holds rigid-frame designs, sill-mounted boards, tethered safety models and budget-to-premium tiers from local retailers and chains. That widening of the range is itself a growth signal; retailers don't expand their range in categories that are flat. Search interest and marketplace listing counts have risen steadily too, though I'd treat any precise figure with caution, because the category is young and definitions vary between data sources.

The drivers behind the growth

Four forces are doing the work:

  • More renters: a large share of Australian households rent, and renters can't drill walls, which pushes them straight to no-drill window gear
  • Smaller homes: apartments and townhouses leave little floor space for cat trees, and vertical glass becomes the enrichment real estate
  • More cats: cat ownership has grown over recent years, and cats overwhelmingly live indoors in units, where window watching is their main entertainment
  • Indoor-cat norms: the shift towards keeping cats contained, driven by wildlife concerns and council rules, makes indoor enrichment purchases feel necessary rather than indulgent

Notice that none of these reverse easily. A renter who buys a $40 hammock isn't a fad customer; they're the market.

Reading the marketplace signals

When I assess a category's health, I look at listing density, review velocity and the shape of the price curve. Window perches check all three: listings have multiplied across Australian marketplaces and pet retailers, reviews accumulate quickly on even mid-tier products, and the price band has fanned out from a single cheap tier to a genuine budget-through-premium ladder. That ladder matters most, because it shows buyers at different budgets entering rather than one cohort churning. I'd flag the usual caveat: some of the volume is the same generic product under different names, so growth in listings overstates growth in distinct products. The direction is still unambiguous.

Who's buying

The buyer profile I'd sketch from the category's shape is a renter or apartment dweller, often with one or two cats, buying their first perch at the $30 to $50 mark and often adding a second perch or an upgrade within a year. Multi-cat households buy multiples, which is where the category quietly compounds: one cat might need one bed, but two cats in a rental need two window spots to keep the peace. The other cohort is senior-cat owners buying lower, softer, more stable designs, a smaller but stickier segment with less price sensitivity.

What it means for owners, and where it heads

For owners, a maturing category means better options without paying more: locking suction cups, washable covers, honest weight ratings and local sellers who handle returns. Expect the next phase to be less about new shapes and more about refinement, tougher cup materials suited to Australian heat, better fabric for summer, and mounting hardware that survives the move from one rental to the next. The category grew because it solved a genuinely Australian problem, a cat in a rented apartment with one sunny window, and that problem isn't going anywhere. Frequently Asked Questions

Are cat window perches really that popular in Australia?

All the observable signals point that way: listing counts, review volumes and the spread of price tiers have all grown over recent years, and major pet retailers now stock multiple designs. Exact market size figures vary between sources, so I'd trust the direction over any precise number. The category has clearly moved mainstream.

Why are window perches so suited to Australian renters?

Because they need no drilling, no wall fixings and minimal space, which is exactly the constraint set of a rental. Suction and sill-mounted designs install in minutes and remove without a trace, protecting the bond. With a large share of Australians renting and cat-friendly tenancies expanding, that fit makes the category almost a default purchase for renting cat owners.

Is the growth just cheap imports flooding marketplaces?

Some of the listing growth is duplicated generic products, so raw counts overstate it. But the more telling signal is the spread of price tiers and the arrival of better-engineered designs at mid-range prices, which indicates real demand, not just supply dumping. Categories driven purely by cheap imports tend to stay single-tier, and this one hasn't.

Do cat owners buy more than one perch?

Commonly, yes. Multi-cat households need multiple window spots to avoid resource guarding, and seasonal heat pushes owners to set up a summer perch and a winter one on different orientations. That repeat-purchase behaviour is a big part of why retailers keep investing in the category despite the low entry price of a first hammock.

What should I look for given how fast the market is changing?

Buy on the engineering, not the marketing: locking suction cups, wide box-stitched straps, washable covers and a rating well above your cat's weight. A category this competitive improves fast, so mid-priced products from the last year or two often beat older premium ones. Check recent reviews rather than listings, since designs get revised quietly.
