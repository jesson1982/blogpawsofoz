Moving House With a Window Perch: A Renter's Pack-Down and Reset

By Ellie NguyenSep 2, 2026Cat Window Perches8 min read

Short answer: A suction window perch packs down cleanly if you release the cups with a credit card, store the brackets flat, and remount only on clean glass at the new place. The real work is the reset, not the carry, because window sizes and your lease rules change with the address.

Last month I moved from a Carlton one-bedroom to a Brunswick flat, still with Juno and Pim (my two cats) and still answering to a landlord who books inspections. The window perch was the one thing I worried about most, because it is the one item that touches the glass. Packing a cat bed is easy. Packing a suction-mounted perch so it arrives straight, and then putting it back without scuffing a freshly painted sill, is a different job. I had an afternoon between the removalists and the bond check, so I treated the perch as the fragile thing it is.

Releasing the suction cups without a trace

The mistake that costs bonds is yanking the cups off the glass. Suction holds by a thin seal of air, and pulling the bracket sideways can drag the rubber across the pane and leave a smear that reads as a scuff to a fussy inspector. I slide a flat plastic card, an old ID or a loyalty card, slowly between the cup and the glass to break the seal. It takes a minute per cup but the glass comes away clean. Any residue from the original mounting wipes off with a damp cloth and a drop of dish soap. I never use a scraper or anything metal near the sill, because a single scratch on a painted surface is harder to explain than a missing hook.

Packing the brackets so they travel straight

A perch that arrives bent is a perch you cannot trust to hold, so the packing is about keeping the frame true rather than keeping it small. I wrap the metal or timber bracket in a tea towel, then slide it between two pieces of cardboard cut to the same shape, taped so it cannot shift in the box. I press the suction cups flat on baking paper and fold it over, which stops them curling or sticking to other things. Loose screws go in a snap-lock bag taped to the bracket, because the one thing guaranteed to vanish in a move is the tiny fitting that holds the whole thing together. If the perch has a fabric cover, I wash it on a cold cycle first so the new flat does not smell of the old one.

Checking the new windows before you mount

Not every window in the new flat is a perch window, and the difference matters more in a rental than it did in the last one. I check the glass for double glazing first, because suction needs a single, sealed, non-porous surface and some newer units have a soft coating that cups will not grip. I avoid windows with a crack or a deep scratch, since adding weight there is asking for a problem I cannot fix. I measure the sill depth with a ruler, since a narrow aluminium frame gives less landing ledge than wide timber. The range I lean on when I am stuck on a model is the cat window perch range, which shows what fits what kind of glass.

  • Break each suction seal with a flat card, never by pulling the bracket.
  • Pack the frame flat between cardboard so it travels without bending.
  • Check the new glass is single, sealed and free of cracks first.
  • Keep all screws in one bag taped to the bracket, not loose.
  • Remount only after the sill is clean, dry and fully cured.

Reading the new lease and the light

The lease at the new address is not the lease at the old one, and perch rules live in the fine print more often than people expect. Some agreements say nothing about fixtures but ask that you return the place in the same condition, which is where no-drill mounting earns its keep. I read the inspection clause before I mount anything, because a landlord who visits every three months wants evidence the walls are untouched, not a debate about whether a perch counts as a fixture. Morning light matters too. My Carlton kitchen window faced east; the Brunswick lounge faces west and bakes late, so I gave the cats a cooler window and kept the sunny one for the plants.

Remounting on day one or day five

No rule says the perch goes up the moment boxes arrive, and a quiet reset suits skittish cats. I normally wait until the lounge room is roughly in order so the perch lines up with where the cats will actually spend the day, not where it happened to fit on moving day. When I do mount, I clean the pane with warm water and a cloth, dry it fully, press each cup for a slow count of ten, then tug-test it before the cats are near. If a cup will not bite, I move to the next window rather than force it, because a perch that slips is worse than no perch for a day.

Settling the cats into the new view

The cats do not care the perch is the same model; they care it sits by a window with something moving outside. Juno took to the Brunswick perch within a day because it looks onto a footpath where dogs walk past, and Pim needed the familiar cover before she would hop up. I kept the washed cover on and left a cardigan I had worn near the mount so the scent was hers, not the previous tenant's. For the first week I checked the cups each morning, because temperature swings in a new flat can loosen a seal that held fine in the old one. After that it is just the usual glance, the same as any other perch, and the bond stays safe because nothing was drilled and nothing was scratched.

Frequently Asked Questions

Will a window perch leave marks when I take it down at the end of a lease?

Taken off the right way, no. The seal releases cleanly if you slide a flat card behind each cup instead of pulling, and any dust ring wipes away with a damp cloth. The damage comes from yanking or from using a scraper on the glass. In two moves I have handed back both places with the windows exactly as I found them.

Can I move a suction perch to a different window in the same flat?

Yes, and it is worth doing if the light or the view changes through the day. Clean the new pane, dry it, and press each cup firmly, then give it a gentle tug test before the cats use it. If a cup will not bite on the new glass, pick another window rather than force it, because a slip hurts more than waiting a day for a better spot.

How do I know the new window will actually hold the perch?

Check three things first. The glass should be a single sealed pane, not a soft-coated double unit the cups cannot grip. The surface must be clean, dry and free of cracks. And the sill needs enough depth for the cats to land. If all three hold, a firm press and a tug test tell you the mount is safe before any weight goes on it.

Should I pack the perch with the removalists or carry it myself?

Carry the brackets yourself. They are light but bend easily under a stack of boxes, and a warped frame is the one fault you cannot see until mount time. I wrap the frame flat between cardboard and keep it in the front seat. The fabric cover can go in the removalists' box after a cold wash, since it takes up no room and survives the trip fine.

My cats hid during the move, will they use the perch again?

They will, but give them the familiar cues first. I keep the washed cover from the old flat on the new mount and leave a worn cardigan nearby so the scent is theirs, not the stranger's who lived there before. Most cats explore within a day or two once the room is quiet. If one stays hidden and off food, that is the moment to ask your vet rather than wait it out.
