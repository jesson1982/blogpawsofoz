Train a puppy to love their bed and stop the 2am wandering

By Daniel WhitlockSep 4, 2026Beds & Mats5 min read

Short answer: a puppy learns to love their bed when the bed predicts good things, like chews, calm and your company, and when wandering is never rewarded. The fix is not a stricter bed but a boring one with a routine: feed chews there, reward settling, and ignore the 2am patrol so it pays nothing. Most puppies take two to three weeks of consistent nights to stop wandering, so start the habit on night one. The owners who struggle most are the ones who accidentally make the midnight prowl the most exciting part of the puppy's day.

Why puppies wander at 2am

A young puppy is used to the warmth of the litter and wakes looking for company, heat and something to do. If the 2am wander earns you out of bed, a play, or even a telling off, the trip just got rewarded. The bed has to become the most comfortable, most rewarding place in the room, not the launch pad for a prowl, and that means the rest of the house needs to be dull by comparison at night.

Step one: make the bed the treat spot

For the first week, all the good quiet chews happen on the bed. A stuffed frozen Kong, a long lasting chew, a scatter of kibble, all given there while you sit nearby. The puppy learns the bed equals the best stuff, and the bed scent builds a calm association fast. Keep the chew calm and long, not a frantic tug toy, because we want rest, not a second wind before bed.

  • Give the calming chew only on the bed.
  • Sit close and read, no eye contact games.
  • Let the puppy leave when finished, no forcing.

Step two: capture the settle

When the puppy flops on the bed on their own, mark it with a soft word and a quiet treat tossed beside them, not a fussy pat. Over several days they connect lying down there with good things. Keep the marker calm so the bed stays a rest cue, not a hype cue, and resist the urge to celebrate, because a puppy wired up by praise is a puppy awake for another hour.

Step three: handle the 2am wake

If they wake and wander, first rule out a toilet need for very young puppies, then keep it dull. A quick silent potty trip, no play, straight back to the bed with the chewer. If it is not toilet time, ignore the patrol from your bed. Boring nights make the wandering fade within a fortnight for most, and the boring part is the hard part for owners who want to soothe.

Step four: build the routine

Same bed, same spot, same order each night. Puppies love a script, and a predictable wind down tells their body sleep is coming. Move the bed only when needed, because a familiar spot anchors the habit more than the fancy shape does, and a bed dragged room to room loses the scent that makes it feel like home to the puppy in the dark.

See how bed shapes affect settling in our Beds and Mats hub, and pick one your puppy can call their own rather than a hand me down from the old dog.

One last note for apartment dwellers: a bed near the front door also pays off when neighbours visit, because the puppy learns the mat is the calm spot while the hallway fills with shoes. The same bed that ends the 2am wander often ends the doorbell chaos, which is a bonus most owners did not expect from one simple habit built over a few patient weeks.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long until the 2am wandering stops?

For most puppies, two to three weeks of consistent, boring nights does it, assuming toilet needs are met. The key is that wandering pays nothing, while the bed pays well. If it goes past a month with no change, check the bed is comfortable and that no one is accidentally rewarding the patrol, because a slipped routine resets the clock fast.

Should I lock the puppy on the bed?

No. Forcing or confining a reluctant puppy can make the bed a trap they fear. Let them choose it by making it rewarding, and use a pen around the bed for safety rather than as a punishment. A puppy who opts in settles faster and stays settled than one who was pinned there, and the fear is hard to undo later.

My puppy cries the moment I leave the room?

Build distance slowly. Sit beside the bed, then a metre away, then just outside the door, rewarding quiet at each step before moving on. Rushing distance creates the cry, so shrink the steps. A frozen chew helps bridge the first alone moments while you are still in view, and patience here pays off for months.

Is a covered bed better for settling?

Some puppies love the den feel of a covered bed and settle quicker; others feel trapped. Try both if you can return one, and watch the body language. A curled, sighing puppy in a cave bed is happy; a scratching-to-escape one is not, so let the puppy tell you which shape wins rather than guessing from a photo.

What if my puppy chews the bed itself?

Redirect to a proper chew and remove the bed from the pen if it is being destroyed, since eating filling is a vet risk. Young puppies mouth everything, so supervise early nights and offer a legal chew instead. A bed with a tough cover helps, but nothing replaces watching a teething pup, and a chewed bed is a cost and a hazard at once.
