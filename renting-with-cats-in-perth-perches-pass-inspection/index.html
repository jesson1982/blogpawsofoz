Renting With Cats in Perth: Perches That Pass Inspection

By Maddie SorensenSep 2, 2026Cat Window Perches5 min read

In a Perth rental, a no-drill window perch is one of the safest cat upgrades you can make, and it passes routine inspections as long as the glass stays clean, the frames stay unmarked and you can show it comes off in ten minutes. I've run suction hammocks and sill pads through two tenancies in Perth's northern suburbs, inspections included, and the formula is simple: nothing permanent, nothing sticky, everything documented. Here's how to set it up so your property manager never blinks.

What WA tenancy rules mean for perches

Western Australia's rental rules are friendlier to pets than they used to be, with consent reasonably considered rather than refused by default, but the bond still hinges on the property coming back as it was found. That's what shapes the perch decision: anything that drills, glues or stains is a bond risk, while anything that clamps or suctions to glass and wipes off clean generally isn't. The inspection itself is usually quick; what gets noticed is staining, scratches on frames, and window film that's been peeled or bubbled.

You don't need to ask permission for a removable perch in most tenancies, but reading your lease's clause on alterations and affixing is worth ten minutes. If it bans adhesive products outright, that rules out any perch mounted with sticky pads, and fair enough.

The setups that pass

These are the configurations I've used or fitted for other renters around Perth:

  • Suction-cup hammock on plain glass, kept well away from any tint film or coating
  • Padded sill cushion, which just sits on the ledge and lifts off with no contact damage at all
  • A freestanding tall cat tree angled at the window, the zero-contact option if your lease is strict
  • A folding window seat that braces against the frame with rubber feet rather than fixings

The sill cushion is the sleeper pick. It's the least exciting perch on the market and the one with literally nothing to fail, mark or explain.

Glass, frames and the marks that matter

Inspectors look at window frames closely, because that's where cat damage usually shows. Keep cups on the glass itself rather than the frame, and keep any clamping pads off painted or powder-coated surfaces. Clean the glass under the cups properly before fitting, because grit under a suction cup causes both slipping and the faint ring that looks like staining at inspection time. A microfibre cloth and a spray of glass cleaner after you reposition the perch covers it. Perth's hard water leaves mineral spots on north-facing glass too, so a quick wipe where the perch sits, before the inspection rather than after, keeps everything looking deliberate rather than neglected.

Preparing for inspection day

The night before, I take the hammock down entirely, wipe the glass and the sill, and stash it in the cupboard with the packaging. Total effort: fifteen minutes. Some renters leave everything in place and it's fine, but I've had inspections where the agent photographed windows for the owner, and I'd rather the photos show clean glass. Take your own dated photos each time too; if there's ever a dispute about a mark, you'll have proof of condition. And keep the receipt or listing page in the same folder as your lease, because showing the perch is a consumer product designed to remove cleanly settles conversations fast.

At the end of the lease

Full teardown is genuinely quick if you've kept to no-drill gear: cups peel off slowly at an angle, any faint ring polishes out with glass cleaner, and the sill cushion just lifts away. The one thing that catches renters out is adhesive residue from products that promised no residue, so if you've used anything sticky, budget real time for removing it. I do a final clean of every window the perch touched, photograph each one, and put the photos in the exit folder. Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need my landlord's permission for a suction window perch?

Usually no, because it's removable and causes no alteration, but check your lease for clauses on affixing items or adhesive products. If the lease is silent, a removable perch generally falls under normal living use, the same as furniture. When in doubt, a short message to the property manager costs nothing and gives you it in writing.

Will suction cups leave marks on the window?

On clean glass, properly applied and slowly removed, they leave nothing a wipe won't clear. Marks happen when grit sits under a cup or when someone yanks the cup off fast. Clean the pane before fitting, peel cups off at a shallow angle, and polish any faint ring with glass cleaner before inspection day.

Can my cat's claws scratch the window frames?

They can if the perch lets the cat launch from the frame or dangle claws over painted surfaces. Choose a perch that positions the platform clear of the frame, add a felt or rubber buffer where anything touches, and trim claws regularly. Claws on glass are harmless; claws on powder-coated or painted frames are what inspectors notice.

What's the most inspection-proof perch option?

A freestanding cat tree positioned at the window, followed by a padded sill cushion. Neither attaches to anything, so there's nothing to explain, nothing to clean off and nothing that can fail. They give up some of the elevated view a suction hammock offers, but for a strict lease or a nervous tenant they're the safe choice.

Should I remove the perch before a routine inspection?

You don't have to, but it's a fifteen-minute job that removes any question. Take it down, wipe the glass, and photograph the clean state. Over multiple inspections this habit keeps the window in documented good condition, which is exactly the evidence that protects your bond if a mark is ever blamed on the perch.
