Connecting Two Playpens for a Bigger Enclosure

By Sophie BennettAug 4, 2026Pet Playpens8 min read

Short answer: link two eight-panel pens into an L-shape when a single square no longer holds a growing Cavoodle and a curious kitten. No tools are needed, since the connectors clip the open ends by hand and the whole thing stays reversible when the lease ends. Here's the trade-off that sold me: Wally flops at one end while Miso keeps her litter tray at the other, and the mesh between them lets both see the room without sharing a corner.

When Wally the Cavoodle puppy first came home to my little Perth flat, I set up a single 8 panel dog pen in the lounge room and thought I had the whole situation sorted. At the time it felt like more than enough room. Miso the rescue kitten could hop in and out through the low front panel, Wally had space to flop on his bed, and I could close the door section whenever I needed to vacuum or cook dinner without a puppy wrapped around my ankles. What I did not expect was how quickly a growing puppy would outgrow that neat little square of floor. This is the story of how I linked two pens together into one bigger enclosure, why it made sense as a renter on a budget, and what I learned along the way so you can decide whether the same approach fits your own home.

Why I started with one 8 panel dog pen

I chose an eight-panel design in the first place because it folds completely flat and slides behind the wardrobe when visitors come over. In a rental you cannot bolt anything to the wall, and you certainly cannot leave a bulky metal crate sitting in the middle of the living room for the length of a lease. The panel system meant I could reshape the pen around the coffee table on wet days and tuck it beside the balcony door when the afternoon sun came around. For the first six weeks it was the right call: a contained, cosy space that kept Wally out of mischief while I worked at the kitchen table, and a safe lookout post for Miso.

When more space became necessary

By the time Wally hit four months old, the single enclosure started to feel cramped in a way I had not planned for. He wanted to chase a ball, spin in circles, and stretch out after a walk on the footpath by the coast, while Miso wanted a sunny patch by the window that happened to sit inside the pen. I realised I had two sensible options: buy one larger single unit, or connect a second set to the one I already owned. Because I was renting and watching every dollar, doubling what I already had made far more sense than throwing the first pen away and starting over with something bigger and more expensive.

How connecting two playpens actually works

Most modular pens use a straightforward panel-to-panel clip or hinge at the top and bottom. I lined up the open ends of the first 8 panel dog pen with the second set, then secured the joining panels with the connectors that came in the box. The result was an L-shape that wrapped neatly around the corner of the room and used space I was otherwise wasting. There were no tools involved, no drilling, and nothing the property manager would ever notice when they popped in for a routine inspection. If your two sets are the same brand and batch, the clips line up without a second thought.

What I gained by linking the pens

The biggest win was having two zones inside one connected enclosure. Wally now has a play area with his bed, water bowl, and toys, while Miso has a quieter corner with her litter tray and a soft cushion. They can see and smell each other through the mesh, which keeps them both settled and stops the kitten from feeling shut away. On our camping weekends I can still pack the whole thing down into the boot of the car, so the renters pet pen that grew with me also comes along to the campsite and keeps Wally contained on unfamiliar ground.

Things to check before you double up

Measure your room before you commit to a second set, because two linked pens take up a surprising amount of floor. Check that the connectors line up if your two sets came from different batches, since a slightly different clip style can leave a gap. Keep at least one door reachable so you are not climbing over panels to reach a water bowl, and make sure the link point sits on flat ground rather than a rug edge where it could rock. A quick evening with the tape measure saves a frustrating return later.

A renter's view on floor space and flexibility

Renting near the coast means smaller rooms and a premium on every square metre, so a setup you can undo in five minutes is worth its weight. The linked pens give me flexibility without any damage to the property, and when the lease ends I take the entire system with me to the next place. For a first-time pet parent who is still figuring out what works, that kind of reversible, low-cost option is exactly what takes the pressure off. I never feel locked into one layout, and that freedom matters more than I expected when I started.

Could one big pen have been better?

People sometimes ask whether I should have just bought a single large pen instead of connecting two. For a renter the answer is not straightforward. A single oversized unit is heavier, harder to store, and tricky to move between rooms, whereas two smaller sets can be used apart on ordinary days and joined only when I want the bigger space. That split-use flexibility is what sold me on the connected approach, and it has saved me more than once when a friend visited and I needed the lounge room back. One pen by the balcony, one in the bedroom, or both linked — the choice is mine each morning.

Tips for a calm connected setup

A few small habits made the bigger enclosure work better. I rotate the toys between the two zones so neither animal gets bored, I keep the food and litter at opposite ends so smells do not mix, and I always do a quick check of the joining clips each morning. None of it takes long, and it keeps the whole system feeling like one calm space rather than two pens awkwardly shoved together. Wally now associates the linked pen with playtime, and Miso treats her corner as a sunny retreat, which is exactly the balance I was hoping for.

Wrapping up

If you are starting out like I was, begin with one good 8 panel dog pen and see how your routine settles. When you need more room, a second set that connects to the first is a budget-friendly way to grow without starting over, and it keeps your home rental-safe the whole time. The connected enclosure turned out to be one of the better calls I made as a new pet parent, and my renters pet pen setup has quietly become the backbone of how Miso and Wally share our small coastal flat.

Frequently Asked Questions

Frequently asked questions

Can I connect two different brands of pen?

It is possible only if the panel heights and the clip style match, which is rare between brands. For a clean, gap-free join, I would stick to the same brand and ideally the same batch so the connectors line up without fuss.

Will linking two pens make them less stable?

Not if the joining clips are seated properly and the whole shape sits on flat floor. The link point is actually a braced corner, so a well-joined L-shape can feel sturdier than a single freestanding set.

How much floor space do two connected sets need?

Roughly 180 by 100 centimetres for an L-shape in the sizes I use, though it depends on the exact panel dimensions. Measure your room and the pen footprint before ordering a second set.

Is a connected pen a good choice for a renter?

Yes, for the same reason a single panel pen suits a rental: nothing is fixed to the wall, it folds away, and you take it with you at the end of the lease. The connected version just adds room without adding damage.

Can my kitten and puppy share the linked space?

Mine do, with separate zones inside the one enclosure. The mesh lets them see each other, and giving each animal their own corner keeps mealtimes and the litter tray apart.

Do I need any tools to join the panels?

No tools at all. The connectors clip the open ends of the two sets together by hand, which is part of why the whole thing stays rental-friendly and reversible.
