What Suction Mounts Leave Behind: Paint, Glass and Flyscreens

By Ellie NguyenSep 2, 2026Cat Window Perches7 min read

Short answer: Suction cups usually leave a grey ring of compressed rubber, a faint greasy film, and sometimes a patch of lifted paint on low-sheen walls. Clean glass with warm soapy water and a microfibre cloth and most of it disappears. Cheap cups on soft paint are the ones that take a souvenir.

I moved into this flat with two cats and a bond I could not afford to lose. In the second week I stuck a suction perch on the lounge room window, watched Miso sleep in it all afternoon, and then spent an hour that night staring at a grey circle on the glass. The next cup went on the bedroom wall, which is painted in a low-sheen acrylic the landlord clearly bought by the bucket, and it took a coin-sized patch of paint with it. I have tested surfaces before mounting anything ever since.

What actually comes off with the perch

Four things can be left behind, and they are not equally serious. The most common is a ring of compressed rubber: the cup flattens against the surface over weeks, dust sticks around the edge, and when you pull it off you get a grey circle that reads as dirt but will not wipe away with a dry cloth. The second is a film of oil that leaches out of cheaper cups, which is invisible on glass until sunlight hits it at an angle. The third is an imprint in the paint itself, a shallow ring where the cup has pressed into a soft finish. The fourth, and the only one that costs money, is paint that lifts away with the cup because the surface underneath was never sealed properly. The first three are cleaning jobs. The last one is a repair.

How I test a spot before committing

Before anything goes on a window or a wall, I run a two-day test. I take the smallest cup in the kit, wet it, press it onto the spot I actually want to use, and leave it for 48 hours with nothing hanging off it. Then I remove it and look at the surface in daylight, from the side. If there is a ring left after a wipe with warm soapy water and a microfibre cloth, that spot does not get a perch. On glass I test low down near the frame, where a mark would sit behind a pot plant. On painted walls I test behind a curtain or a piece of furniture, never in the middle of a blank wall. Everything I mount comes from the no-drill gear in my window perch gear series, and every piece of it goes through the same test.

Which surfaces mark and which forgive

Glass is the most forgiving surface in the flat. It is non-porous, it does not absorb oil, and warm water with a drop of dish liquid takes almost anything off it. Gloss enamel paint is nearly as good, because the finish is hard and sealed. The trouble starts with low-sheen and matt acrylics, which are soft enough for a cup to press into, and with anything painted over a surface that was not primed. Timber sills and window frames vary enormously. A sealed, varnished sill is fine. A waxed or raw one will take a mark and keep it. Flyscreen mesh is its own category, and it is the one surface I will not put a cup on at all.

  • A grey ring of compressed rubber is normal and lifts with warm soapy water.
  • Low-sheen and matt paint marks far more than glass or gloss enamel.
  • Test every new spot for 48 hours before anything hangs off it.
  • Flyscreen mesh stretches under load and does not go back.
  • Keep a microfibre cloth and dish liquid with the perch kit.

The removal routine I use at the end

Never pull a cup straight off the glass. Slide a fingernail under the tab, break the seal slowly, and let air in before you apply any force. If the cup has been up for months, warm it first with your hand or a cloth wrung out in hot water, because a cold cup on cold glass tends to let go suddenly and take paint with it. Once it is off, wipe the surface with warm water and a drop of dish liquid, then dry it with a clean microfibre cloth. For a stubborn rubber ring on glass, a paste of bicarb and water on a cloth lifts it without scratching. On painted walls, do not scrub. Wipe once, gently, and if the mark stays, leave it. Scrubbing a soft finish turns a faint ring into a bald patch.

Glass, flyscreens and a hot window

The west-facing window in my flat gets hot enough that a suction cup softens and creeps. Heat does two things. It weakens the seal, so the perch drops, and it bakes whatever film sits under the cup onto the glass. I clean the glass before mounting, always, because dust trapped under a cup is what turns a clean lift into a gritty scratch. I also check the cup every few days through January and February and re-seat it if it has moved. Flyscreens stay off limits. Even a light perch pulls the mesh and bows the frame, and the mesh does not go back. If the only bright window has a screen, I mount on the glass beside the frame rather than over the mesh.

What I tell the agent before inspection

I list the perch on the condition report when I move in, even though it is a removable item, because it means the window and the wall behind it are documented as they were. I photograph the spot before I mount anything and again after I take it down, on the same phone, in the same light. That is not about hiding anything. It is about having an answer ready if someone asks why a mark is there. My last two inspections did not mention the windows at all. I take the perch down the morning of the inspection, clean the glass, drop the cups in a drawer, and leave the room looking like a room.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do suction cups damage glass?

Not the glass itself. Glass is hard and non-porous, so a cup sits on the surface rather than pressing into it. What you get is a film of oil from the rubber and a ring of trapped dust, and both come off with warm water and a drop of dish liquid. The parts of a window that suffer are the frame and the flyscreen, not the pane.

Can a suction cup mark be removed from painted plaster?

Sometimes. A dry ring usually lifts with a warm, barely damp microfibre cloth and no pressure at all. An imprint in the paint will not come out, because the surface itself has changed shape rather than collected dirt. If paint has lifted, that is filler and a touch-up, which is a conversation for your property manager rather than a job for the night before an inspection.

What if the only bright window has a flyscreen?

Mount on the glass beside the frame, or use a perch that rests on the sill without sticking to anything. Flyscreen mesh stretches under load and stays stretched, and the corners of an aluminium frame bow. It is the one surface in my flat I will not put a cup on, no matter how light the perch is.

How long can a suction mount stay up?

Longer than most people think, but the longer it stays, the more time the rubber has to press into a soft finish and the more heat cycles it goes through. I re-seat mine every couple of weeks so I can look at the surface underneath. Anything that has been up through a full summer gets a proper clean before it goes back on.

Will a mark cost me part of my bond?

It depends on what the mark is and what your condition report says. A clean window with no residue is not a cost. Lifted paint or a stretched flyscreen can be, and the amounts vary by state and by lease. Check your own condition report and ask your property manager or a tenant advice service.
