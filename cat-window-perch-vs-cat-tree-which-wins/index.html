Cat Window Perch vs Cat Tree: Which Wins?

By Sophie BennettJul 13, 2026Cat Window Perches5 min read

Sophie & the Fur Babies — suction-cup cat window hammocks for renters & sun-loving cats

Before Miso arrived, I assumed every cat needed a tall carpeted cat tree, the kind that takes over a corner and collects fluff. Then I rented a smaller Perth flat, ran out of floor, and realised a window perch might suit us better. I am not a vet and this is not medical advice, just the practical maths of a first-time pet parent weighing two very different bits of gear. The short answer is that neither "wins" outright. It depends on your space, your cat, and your lease. Here is how I think it through, and why Miso ended up with a perch and a small tree both.

The case for a window perch

A window-mounted cat bed does three things a tree cannot. It uses no floor space, it puts your cat in the sun, and it gives them a view. In a flat, that last point is huge. Miso would rather watch the footpath than climb carpet, and the perch lets her do it while lounging. Because it sticks to the glass with suction, it is also the most renter-friendly option going. No screws, no marks, bond-safe. For a small home and a sun-loving cat, the perch is hard to beat.

The case for a cat tree

A cat tree still earns its place. It gives vertical climbing, multiple platforms, and usually a scratching post, which a window perch does not. If your cat needs to scratch furniture or burn energy indoors, the tree pulls that job. It also holds more weight, so for a large cat a sturdy tree is the safer structure. The downside is the footprint and the price, and in a one-bedroom rental that footprint is a real cost in itself.

What Miso actually prefers

Miso is a watcher, not a climber. She uses the perch for hours and the tree for a quick scratch. Wally the Cavoodle, being a puppy, mostly tries to steal the tree's dangly toy. So for our home the perch won on daily use, and the tree stays as backup. If your cat is a Bengal who climbs the curtains, flip that priority and spend on the tree. The right answer follows the cat's personality, not the trend.

Weight and safety honesty

I will not fudge the limits. Our twin-suction window perch suits small and medium cats up to about 15 kg. It is not a large-breed holder. If your cat is big, a reinforced four-suction steel-cable perch or a solid cat tree is the honest pick. A tree will generally out-hold any suction product, which is worth knowing before you choose.

Can you have both?

Absolutely, and many renters do. A window perch near the best light plus a compact tree in a corner covers climbing, scratching, and sunbathing without crowding a small flat. If budget is tight, start with the perch, because it is cheaper and uses no space, then add a tree later if your cat shows they want to climb. That is the path I took, and it spared me buying a giant tree Miso might have ignored.

Frequently asked questions

Frequently asked questions

Is a window perch better than a cat tree?

For renters with little floor space and a sun-loving cat, the perch usually wins on daily use. A cat tree wins for climbers and scratchers who need a post. Many homes keep both.

Which is more renter-friendly?

The window-mounted perch, clearly. It sticks to glass with suction, needs no drill, and leaves no marks, so your bond is safe. A cat tree is bulky and awkward to move at lease end.

Which costs less in Australia?

A suction perch is cheaper, with our value pick at $24.99–$49.99. Cat trees commonly run $80 to $250 depending on height and materials.

Can a large cat use a window perch?

A cat over about 15 kg should use a reinforced four-suction steel-cable perch or a sturdy tree. We do not claim our standard twin-suction hammock holds heavy large breeds.

My flat is tiny. What should I buy first?

Start with the window perch. It costs less and uses zero floor, then add a small tree later only if your cat wants to climb and scratch.

Will a perch stop my cat scratching furniture?

Not really. A perch is for lounging and watching, not scratching. If furniture scratching is the problem, a cat tree with a post or a separate scratcher is the better fix.
