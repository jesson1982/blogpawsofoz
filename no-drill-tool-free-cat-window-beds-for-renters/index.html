No-Drill, Tool-Free Cat Window Beds for Renters

By Sophie BennettJul 7, 2026Cat Window Perches6 min read

Short answer: no-drill suction beds give a renter a happy cat and untouched glass, so your bond stays safe and the deposit comes home with you. In my Perth flat I wanted Miso a sunny lookout but drilling was never on the table, and the tool-free stick-on version left the window mark-free when I shifted it with the seasons. In short, clean dry glass and a weekly tug-check are the whole habit, and there is nothing for a property manager to flag at inspection.

Renting in Perth taught me one rule fast: if I put a hole in something, I either fix it perfectly or lose part of my bond. So when I wanted to give Miso a sunny lookout, drilling brackets into the window was never on the table. What I found instead was a whole category of no-drill, tool-free cat window beds that stick with suction and come away clean. For a budget-conscious renter, that is the dream combo: happy cat, untouched glass, and a deposit I get to keep.

Why renters need a no-drill option

Most leases say you cannot alter the property without permission, and a window is exactly the kind of fixture a property manager inspects. A screw or a bracket leaves a mark that has to be explained, patched, or paid for. A suction hammock leaves nothing behind because it never touches the glass with anything but rubber. I have taken Miso's perch down to deep-clean the window and put it back in the same spot, and the pane looked identical before and after. That reversibility is what makes it renter-safe.

There is a practical side too. As a first-time pet parent I did not want to buy a power drill or learn to use one just for a cat bed. Tool-free means I opened the box, washed the window, and pressed the cups on. The whole setup took me longer to read than to actually do, and Wally supervised from the rug like he was judging a cooking show.

The bond-friendly promise

My bond is a chunk of money I would rather keep than hand over for avoidable damage. A no-drill perch protects that in two ways. First, no holes. Second, no adhesive residue, because a good suction cup needs bare glass and nothing sticky. I have seen window film and double-sided tape ruin a rental surface, and I wanted none of that. The suction design is the rare bit of pet gear that is genuinely reversible, which is why I recommend it to every renting friend with a cat.

I also like that it moves with me. When my lease ends and I shift to a new flat near the coast, the hammock comes with me in a tote bag. No uninstall cost, no patching, no awkward conversation at the final inspection. It is the kind of small, sensible choice that makes renting with pets feel less temporary and more like home.

Sizing and the honest weight note

Our standard twin-suction hammock is made for small and medium cats up to about 15 kg. Miso is well under that, so she has room to stretch. I want to be straight with you: I would not use our standard hammock for a large cat of 15 kg or more. For a heavy breed, the right tool is a reinforced four-suction or steel-cable model that spreads the weight and stays put. Saying otherwise would be careless, and renters have enough to worry about without a failed perch.

Setting it up without tools

The method is simple and the same every time. Clean the glass with warm water and a little dish soap, rinse, dry with a lint-free cloth, then press each cup from the centre out to push the air free. If the cup has a lock tab, flip it. Lift your cat on gently the first time so she connects the bed with a good experience. I did this with Miso on a Saturday morning, and by the afternoon she was treating the perch like it had been there forever.

Because there is no drilling, you can also tweak the position as the seasons turn. In winter the low sun suits one window; in summer the morning light favours another. I shift the perch between two windows without a single tool, which a screwed-in bracket would never let me do. That flexibility is part of why it suits rental life so well.

Keeping the glass and the cat happy

Tool-free does not mean care-free. I wipe the cups and the glass every week and re-seat if needed, especially after a hot day when the pane warms up. Wally is taught that the window is Miso's calm space, so he watches from a polite distance. The result is a small, sunny lounge that costs me almost nothing to maintain and nothing to undo when I move.

For anyone renting with a cat, my honest take is this: start with a no-drill suction bed, size it to your cat, and respect the weight limit. You get a happier pet, a brighter flat, and a bond inspection that stays boring in the best possible way. Miso may not understand the lease, but she understands the sun, and that is the part that matters to her.

Frequently Asked Questions

Frequently asked questions

Will a no-drill window bed damage my rental?

No. A suction hammock uses rubber cups on bare glass with no screws, no adhesive and no holes. It comes away clean, which keeps your bond safe at the final inspection.

Do I need any tools to set it up?

None. You clean the window, press the cups on, and lock the tab if there is one. I managed it with Miso weaving between my feet and Wally judging from the rug.

Is the standard hammock okay for a large cat?

Our standard twin-suction model suits small and medium cats up to about 15 kg. For a large cat of 15 kg or more, choose a reinforced four-suction or steel-cable perch. We do not claim the standard one holds heavy large breeds.

Can I move it to a different window?

Yes, and that is the joy of a tool-free design. Pop it off, clean the new pane, and re-seat. I shift Miso's perch with the sun across the seasons with no marks left behind.

What if my property manager inspects?

There is nothing to flag. The perch leaves no alteration, so you can leave it up or remove it in a minute. I keep mine up because it is clearly reversible and causes no damage.

How do I make sure it stays stuck?

Clean, dry, smooth glass and a weekly tug-check on the cups. Avoid window film or conditioner residues. Mount on a cool pane rather than a sun-baked one for the best initial seal.
