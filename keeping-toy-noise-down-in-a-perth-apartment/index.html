How We Keep Toy Noise Down in a Perth Apartment (Without Boring the Pets)

By Sophie BennettSep 5, 2026Cat & Dog Toys5 min read

Direct answer: the trick to quiet apartment play is choosing soft, floor friendly toys and setting a few house rules, not banning play altogether. Jingle balls and hard plastic squeakers are the usual culprits behind neighbour complaints, while felt, fabric and treat based toys keep both Miso and Wally busy with almost no sound. With a rug down and a toy curfew after nine, we have stayed on good terms with the people next door for over a year.

Why apartment toy noise becomes a problem

In a unit, sound travels through shared walls and floors more than you expect. A squeaker at seven in the morning or a ball bouncing on floorboards at night reads as carelessness to someone trying to sleep. Our first month here earned us a polite note, and that was the wake up call.

The fix was not less play, it was smarter play. Pets need stimulation, but they do not need a marching band to get it.

The quiet toys that actually worked

Felt mice, fabric knots, and a cat wand with a soft ribbon are nearly silent. For Wally, a plush he can squeak gently plus a lick mat keeps him settled with no racket. We swapped jingle balls for the crinkle free version and kept hard toys for the park.

  • Felt and fabric toys for indoor fetch and carry.
  • Wands with cloth ribbons instead of plastic attachments.
  • Lick mats and snuffle play for calm, quiet engagement.
  • Anything noisy stays in the car or at the off leash park.

Simple habits that kept the neighbours happy

We put a rug under the play area to deaden paws, and we stop rough play by half past eight. Toys that roll are kept in a shallow basket, not on the floor where they scatter at midnight. None of it was hard, and the building has been quiet since.

Miso still gets her zoomie bursts, but we steer them to the hallway runner rather than the tiled kitchen where sound rings out.

When to take loud play outside

Some dogs need to squeak and chase, and that is fine at the park or beach. We keep one loud rope and a squeaky ball in the car so Wally gets that outlet without it costing us goodwill at home. Perth has enough green space that this is easy most of the year.

We also learned that quiet is a habit the pets pick up from us. When we stop reacting to every squeak and redirect to a soft toy, Wally reads the room and chooses the plush on his own. Miso never needed the lesson, she prefers silence anyway, but the consistency helped the whole apartment stay calm.

Strata living adds its own rules. Our building asks for no noise after ten, and a simple toy curfew keeps us within that without a word from the committee. I keep a small basket by the door with the indoor set, and the louder things live in the car for park days. That physical split means there is never a late night temptation to grab the wrong toy.

For renters especially, good relations with neighbours are worth more than any single toy. A five minute conversation about our routine, plus a quiet play habit, turned an early note into a friendly wave. Enrichment and courtesy are not opposites, they just need a little planning.

If you are moving into a new block, introduce the quiet set first. Let the neighbours meet a calm pet before they ever hear one, and the goodwill carries you through the noisy puppy phase that might come later.

Frequently Asked Questions

What toys are quietest for an apartment?

Felt, fabric and soft plush toys make the least noise, along with wand toys that use cloth ribbons. Food based toys like lick mats are nearly silent. Avoid jingle balls, hard plastic squeakers and anything that bounces on floorboards late at night.

Can I stop my dog squeaking toys indoors?

Keep the squeaky ones for outside and offer a soft plush or lick mat at home. Most dogs adapt quickly when the loud toy simply is not available indoors. A short play session at the park covers their need to make noise.

Will a rug really reduce toy noise?

Yes, noticeably. A rug or mat under the play zone softens paws and stops toys rolling into walls. It will not silence everything, but combined with a toy curfew it takes the edge off enough that neighbours rarely notice.

Is it unfair to limit play to keep neighbours happy?

Not at all, as long as the pet still gets daily enrichment. Quiet toys and outdoor rough play cover both needs. Limiting noise is about timing and surface, not about denying your cat or dog the chance to play.

What time should indoor play stop?

A reasonable cut off is around eight thirty or nine in the evening, the same quiet hours you would expect from a neighbour. Save energetic chase for daytime and offer calm lick mat or wand time in the evening instead.
