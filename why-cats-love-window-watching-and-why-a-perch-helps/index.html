Why Cats Love Window Watching (and Why a Perch Helps)

By Sophie BennettJul 4, 2026Cat Window Perches6 min read

Sophie & the Fur Babies — suction-cup cat window hammocks for renters & sun-loving cats

There is a reason almost every cat I have ever met ends up parked at a window. Miso, my little rescue kitten, is no different. Give her a sunny pane and she will sit there with her tail tip twitching, watching birds, leaves, and the occasional startled pigeon with a focus I can only envy. I am not a vet and this is not clinical advice, but as a first-time pet parent I have become fascinated by the simple joy a window brings her. A perch just makes that joy easier to reach.

When I first set up Miso's suction hammock, I expected her to use it now and then. Instead it became her favourite spot in the flat, ahead of the cosy bed on my desk and the warm spot by the heater. The window, it turns out, offers three things a floor cannot: light, height, and a live show that never repeats.

The pull of the sunny patch

Cats are heat-seekers. Left to their own devices they will find the warmest square of floor in any room and claim it. A window in a Perth flat collects the sun like a battery, and Miso stretches out flat on her mesh perch to soak it up. The elevated bed breathes, so she stays warm without getting sticky, and the slight give of the hammock seems to suit her better than a hard sill.

I noticed the change in her behaviour within a week. She stopped flopping in the hallway where the sun happened to land, and started waiting by the lounge window each morning. Watching that little routine form was one of the small pleasures of bringing her home.

Why height matters to a cat

Even a confident cat likes to be up where it can see the room. In the wild, a higher vantage meant safety and a clear view of dinner. Miso may be a pampered apartment kitten, but the instinct is unchanged. Her window perch lifts her to the top of the glass, above Wally the puppy's curious nose, and she plainly feels settled there.

For a renter with a dog and a cat, that vertical separation is gold. Miso has a retreat that is hers alone, and Wally has learned that the window is her lookout, not a toy. Their truce is built, in part, on a suction cup and a bit of mesh.

The endless entertainment of the outside world

What surprises new cat parents is how absorbing a window can be. Miso will track a magpie across the lawn, freeze at the buzz of a bee on the glass, and chatter softly at sparrows she cannot reach. This is the famous cat bird watching behaviour, and it is genuinely good enrichment. She is exercising her senses, practising the stillness of a hunter, and burning a little energy without tearing the couch.

I started calling it her telly. On rainy days the show is quieter, just drops sliding down the pane, but she still sits with her paws tucked, calm and watchful. An elevated cat window bed turns dead glass into a front-row seat, and the cost is a fraction of any electronic toy I considered buying.

How a perch changes the experience

A cat can watch from a sill, of course, but a proper perch changes the comfort. Miso's hammock cups her body, keeps her off the cold frame in winter, and lets her dangle a paw into the warm air. Because it is mounted rather than balanced on the ledge, there is no risk of it sliding when she leaps up, which was my worry with a loose cushion.

The mesh also means she stays cool through a Perth summer. I have seen window beds with thick padding that must get clammy; the open weave of ours seems to suit her coat, and she grooms herself up there without fuss.

The honest note carries here too. Our standard perch is for small and medium cats up to about 15 kg. A big cat deserves the same window joy, but on a reinforced model built for the load. Nobody should miss out on bird watching, whatever their size.

Making the window a happy place

I keep Miso's perch near a window with a decent garden view, not a blank wall. If your outlook is bare, a pot plant on the sill outside or a bird feeder further out can bring the show to life. I also leave the blinds open through the day so the light reaches her, which is the whole point of renting a bright coastal flat.

Wally, my puppy, loves to lie underneath and watch her watch, which is its own quiet comedy. The two of them, one above and one below, remind me why I bothered with the perch in the first place. It is not just furniture; it is a small improvement to the daily life of a rescue kitten who, I suspect, appreciates the view as much as I appreciate her enjoying it.

Frequently asked questions

Frequently asked questions

Why does my cat stare out the window for so long?

Cats are natural hunters and the moving world outside — birds, leaves, insects — holds their attention. It is a form of enrichment that lets them watch, track, and practise stillness without leaving the room.

Is window watching good mental stimulation for a cat?

In my experience, yes. Miso stays calmer and more settled when she has a daily view. It is not a replacement for play, but it keeps her senses engaged while I work.

Do I need a perch, or is a windowsill enough?

A sill works, but it is hard, cold, and easy to slide off. A perch adds comfort, safety, and a better sightline, which is why Miso chose hers over the bare ledge.

My cat chatters at birds — is that normal?

It is very common and nothing to worry about. That soft chatter is a harmless hunting response. I am not a vet, but it is normal feline behaviour I see in Miso often.

Can a window perch suit a kitten and a puppy in one flat?

Yes. I mount Miso's perch high enough that Wally cannot reach it, giving the cat a safe retreat and the dog a floor-level show of his own.

What if my cat is too large for your standard perch?

Our standard hammock suits small and medium cats up to about 15 kg. For a larger cat, use a reinforced 4-suction or steel-cable model so they can enjoy the window safely.
