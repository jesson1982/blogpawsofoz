Where the Carrier Sleeps: Camp Setups for Overnight Trips

By Tom RileySep 4, 2026Pet Carriers & Backpacks8 min read

Short answer: the carrier comes into the tent or swag with you, off the ground, zipped or latched and out of the breeze line, and it stays there until morning. A carrier left outside a tent overnight is a goanna magnet in the Top End and a wind risk anywhere. Treat it like camp furniture with a resident, not a bag.

Scout is a Border Collie, so he has never fitted in a carrier in his life. Most of what I know comes from field-testing travel carriers on trips with mates' dogs and cats, including a jack russell who has done Darwin to Kununurra and back in a soft crate. On those trips the same lesson landed every time: the carrier stops being transport the moment you arrive. From dusk to pack-up it is a bedroom, and where you put it decides whether everyone sleeps. Get it wrong and you spend the night listening to zips, claws and complaints, and so does the site next door.

The carrier as camp furniture

The moment camp goes up, the carrier changes jobs. On the road it is transport, latched and strapped into the car. At camp it is a den, and I set it up like one. It comes inside the tent, door open, with whatever bedding the animal already knows, placed where it can see the door and the cooking area. A mate's jack russell, my usual test passenger, spends the evening shuttling between the carrier and my camp chair, and that is exactly the point. The carrier gives a small animal one spot in camp that smells right and stays put while everything else moves. It also keeps fur and sand out of the swag, which matters more on night three than night one. Among the travel carriers I have tested, the ones that work at camp are the ones an animal already naps in at home.

Where the carrier goes overnight

Ground rules that have settled out of a few dozen nights. Inside the tent or swag, always, never on the ground outside. Flat ground, away from the door where the draught and the mozzies come in, and not against a canvas wall, which holds heat and blocks the mesh. In a tent, the foot end works well, with the mesh panels of the carrier facing the mesh of the tent so air actually moves through. Swags are tighter. If the animal sleeps in the swag, the carrier usually stays in the car or the vestibule with the door open toward the swag. The one placement I refuse is on the ground sheet under a drip line. Condensation plus a carrier floor pad equals a damp dog by midnight, and damp becomes smell very quickly in this climate.

Securing it against wind and wildlife

A carrier is light, which is the whole problem at camp. Wind will walk one across a campsite, so in any breeze I strap it down or sit the esky against the wind side. Wildlife is the other half. Goannas work by smell and will flip an unzipped flap looking for food, dingoes test anything new left in camp overnight, and birds pull at zip tags for the fun of it. So the food never lives in the carrier, treats get wiped off after meals and every zip is closed by dark unless I am sitting right there. Hard crates latch, which is their quiet advantage at camp. Soft carriers rely on zips and on me. Neither survives a food smell left inside, so that habit matters more than any latch.

Airflow in a tent or swag

Airflow decides whether the animal sleeps or paces all night, and a carrier can block it as easily as provide it. The mistakes I see are the same every trip: carrier pushed against the tent wall, mesh side blanketed by a towel for privacy, or zipped shut out of habit while the tent itself is open. My setup is simple. Mesh faces mesh, the door stays open whenever the tent is zipped, and nothing drapes over the carrier except in wind or rain, and then only on the wind side. In the car overnight, the carrier rides in the footwell or on the seat, never sealed in a boot, with the windows cracked and socked the same as any night. Warm nights change all of it. If the evening stays sticky past bedtime, the plan changes with it.

  • Bring the carrier inside the tent before dark, every single night.
  • Keep it off the ground where condensation and crawlers travel.
  • Face the mesh side to airflow, never a canvas wall.
  • Never store food or treats inside the carrier at camp.
  • Latch it when you cannot supervise, not automatically all night.

What changes in the wet season

The build-up and the wet rewrite the whole setup. Afternoon storms arrive fast, so the carrier moves to the driest corner of the tent and, if the forecast is ugly, onto the esky or a chair, off the floor where water creeps. Humidity is the slower problem. A soft carrier that got soaked in the afternoon is still damp at lights out, so I dry it every morning with the panels open and the sun on the mesh, before it goes anywhere near bedding. Ants are the third change. A carrier left with a biscuit crumb in the floor pad becomes an ant highway overnight, and the animal will let you know at two in the morning. Wipe the inside each evening and stand the base on a block, off the ground.

Packing the carrier away each morning

Pack-up starts with the carrier, not the tent. Everything comes out, sand gets shaken off the base and the mesh goes in the sun while breakfast happens. I run a thumb along both zip lines, because grit in a zip at camp becomes a broken zip by the third day, and a broken zip turns transport back into a problem. Bedding goes back in only if it is properly dry, otherwise it rides in a separate bag. Last, the carrier gets strapped down where it will travel, not where it rested. A carrier that has spent the night as furniture still needs to survive the drive home, and corrugations do not care how cosy it was at midnight.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can a cat stay in a carrier all night at camp?

Not zipped in all night, in my experience. A cat that has settled does better with the carrier as an open den inside the zipped tent, coming and going as it likes. On a first night, or at a busy site, keep the door shut until things are quiet, then open it. The carrier is the retreat, not the cell.

How do I stop wildlife getting into the carrier overnight?

Bring it inside the tent with you, which removes most of the problem. Goannas and dingoes follow smell, so I never store food or treats in the carrier and I wipe it out after meals. If it must stay outside, latch every zip, keep it off the ground and weight or strap the wind side before dark.

Should the carrier go in the car or the tent overnight?

It depends on the animal and the night. In the dry season, a mesh tent is usually the better bedroom. On a sticky build-up night, a sealed tent can feel stuffier than the car with windows cracked and socked, so I move with the weather. Watch how your animal handled the day, and ask your vet if heat is a concern.

How do I dry a carrier after a wet season storm?

Open every zip and panel first thing and stand it in the sun with the mesh facing out while you make breakfast. Towel the base before it goes back in the car, or it carries a damp smell to the next camp. Soft carriers hold water in the floor pad, so lift that out if the design allows and dry it flat.

My dog cries in the carrier at night. What helps?

On my trips it has usually been company or heat. Move the carrier within sight of where you sleep, leave the door open once the tent is zipped and put something inside that smells like home. Check the spot is not catching a cold draught or holding the day's warmth. If crying runs on night after night, mention it to your vet before the next trip.
