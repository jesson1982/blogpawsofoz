Dog Car Hammock vs Seat Cover: Which Protects Your Car Better?

By Mick O'DonnellAug 18, 2026Dog Car Seat Covers6 min read

Mick from the Bush — dog car-travel & ute gear

The question I get asked more than any other at the servo and the show is a simple one: do I want a dog car hammock or just a plain seat cover? Banjo and I have run both, and after fifteen years with a working Heeler I've got a straight answer. But it isn't the same answer for everyone, because the dog, the car and the trip are different from one property to the next.

So let's lay it out. There are really three forms people mean when they say pet car seat cover: the hammock that clips to the front headrests, the bench cover that just lies across the seat, and the hammock-extender that adds a hard floor over the footwell. Each protects the car a bit differently, and each asks something different of you and the dog.

Protection — what actually gets saved

This is where the dog car hammock vs seat cover argument is won or lost. A bench cover sits on the seat base and that's it. The back of the seat, the footwell and the doors are still fair game for mud, salt and claw marks. Banjo learnt to lean on the door the first week I owned a bench cover, and the panel copped it.

A hammock hangs from the front headrests, so it wraps the back of the seat and bridges the footwell. Most come with door flaps too, so the panels are covered. That's a big step up for a dog that shakes or scratches.

The extender takes it further. Because it builds a hard floor out over the footwell, there's no gap at all, and the dog can't drop between the seats. If protection is your only question, the extender wins, hands down. For most everyday travel, the hammock is the sweet spot.

Fit — will it stay put

Fit is the quiet part people skip. A bench cover with two straps over the rear headrests will walk forward every time the dog sits up, and on a corrugated road it'll bunch. The hammock's four straps hold it square. The extender is the widest of the lot and needs the rigid board seated properly, so it pays to measure your seat first — some are built for SUVs and run wide in a dual-cab ute.

Dog comfort — and a calmer ride

Banjo reckons comfort matters more than I give him credit for. On a bench cover he slides into the footwell the moment I brake, and a dog that's sliding is a dog that's stressed. The hammock holds him level. The extender lets a big dog actually lie down and stretch, which is why on the long drive into town or down to the coast I run the extender. A flat floor means he's settled before we hit the highway, and a settled dog is a safer dog.

Install effort — the honest bit

I won't pretend the extender is a thirty-second job. You clip the straps, seat the board, and check it's flat. Call it two minutes. The hammock is four straps and done. The bench cover is the easiest, but you pay for that ease in protection. If you're in and out of the car all day with the dog, the quick bench might suit; if it's one or two trips, spend the two minutes on the hammock.

My pick

For most folks reading this, the hammock is the answer. It covers the bits that cop the damage, it holds the dog, and it's not a drama to fit. If you've got a big dog or you do beach runs where sand gets everywhere, step up to the extender. The plain bench cover I'd keep for the small, calm dog on short trips — and even then I'd rather see a hammock.

One more thing: whichever you pick, get the waterproof one with a non-slip backing. Out here the dog is wet more often than not, and a cover that slides or soaks through defeats the purpose. Banjo doesn't care which form it is. He just wants the window down and the seat there. The car's my problem, and the hammock or extender keeps it my problem, not the upholsterer's.

Frequently asked questions

Is a dog car hammock better than a seat cover?

For most dogs, yes. The hammock covers the seat back and bridges the footwell, and most include door flaps, so more of the car is protected and the dog can't slide into the floor on a corner. A bench cover only saves the seat base.

Do hammocks stop the dog falling into the footwell?

They do. Clipped to the front headrests, the hammock forms a wall between the front and back seats. A hammock-extender goes further with a hard floor over the footwell so even a big dog can lie flat.

Will a pet car seat cover fit my ute?

The hammock and bench forms clip to the headrests and suit most dual-cab utes. The wide extender is built with SUVs in mind, so measure your rear seat before you order one.

Are hammocks hard to fit?

No. Four straps, a minute or two. The extender takes a little longer because you seat a rigid board, but it's still a quick job. The bench cover is the easiest of the three.

Which is best for a large dog?

The hammock-extender. A big dog needs the flat floor to lie down, and the rigid board won't bow under his weight. The plain hammock leaves the footwell open and a heavy dog will still slide.

Can I use a hammock with the seat folded?

Some extenders and bench covers work with the seat folded for more cargo room, but a hammock needs the rear seat up to clip to the headrests. Check the maker's notes for your model before you rely on it.
