Mesh vs Plush Cat Window Hammock: Which Fabric?

By Sophie BennettJul 9, 2026Cat Window Perches8 min read

Short answer: mesh wins for a Perth summer and plush wins for winter, so the smart move is one breathable hammock with a removable warm cover rather than two full beds. Three hot weeks and a sweaty Miso taught me the fabric matters as much as the suction, and the mesh kept her cool while the plush liner came out once the glass went cold. The truth is most first-time renters are better off starting with mesh, then adding plush for the cooler months.

When I first started looking for a cat window hammock, I thought the shape and the suction cups were the only things that mattered. It took about three hot weeks of Perth summer and one very sweaty kitten to realise the fabric on the bed is just as important as the mount itself. Miso, my rescue kitten, spends most of her day chasing patches of sunlight across the lounge room floor, and the window perch became her favourite spot within a day of me putting it up. The question I kept coming back to was simple: do I want mesh or plush?

I am not a vet and I do not pretend to be one. I am just a renter who wanted a cosy, no-drill spot for my cat to watch the world go by. So everything below is based on living with the thing, washing it, and watching how Miso behaves — not on any clinical knowledge. I hope it helps you pick the right fabric for your own flat and your own fur baby.

Why the fabric matters more than you'd think

The bed of a window hammock sits right against the glass, and glass does two very different things depending on the season. In summer it radiates heat like a radiator, and in winter it turns cold and a little damp to the touch. The fabric sitting between your cat and that pane decides whether your cat stays comfortable or ends up restless. A mesh cat window hammock lets air move through, which is a quiet lifesaver on a forty-degree day. A plush cat window perch with cover, on the other hand, gives that padded, nestled feeling that cats seem to adore when the nights get chilly.

I learned this the hard way. My first perch had a thin plush cover, and during a February heatwave Miso would hop up, realise it was warm, and immediately hop back down. She wanted the window, not the warmth. Swapping to a breathable mesh bed changed everything — suddenly she was happy to stay put for hours.

What I actually noticed with Miso

Miso is a small cat, still growing, and her behaviour is a pretty good weather gauge. On warm mornings she picks the mesh bed every time, stretches out flat, and lets the sea breeze from the coast drift across her belly. On cold evenings she gravitates to the plush version, curls into a tight little donut, and presses her chin into the padding. Watching her taught me that there is no single "best" fabric — there is only the best fabric for the day you are having.

If you live somewhere with a long, relentless summer, mesh is the safer default. If your place gets properly cold and your cat is the cuddly type, plush earns its keep. Plenty of people, myself included, end up rotating two covers depending on the month.

Breathability — the big one for Perth summers

This is where mesh genuinely wins. The open weave means warm air does not get trapped under your cat, and any breeze coming through a cracked window actually reaches them. A cat window perch with cover made of plush foam holds heat, which feels lovely for about ten minutes and then becomes a warm sandwich. For a climate like ours on the west coast, breathability is not a luxury — it is the difference between a perch your cat uses and one that becomes a decoration.

There is also a comfort angle. Cats do not sweat the way we do; they mostly cool through their paw pads and by finding cooler surfaces. A mesh bed gives them that cooler surface, and I have watched Miso flop onto hers like she is melting into the mesh on a hot afternoon.

Warmth when the glass goes cold

Come July, the story flips. The window gets cold, sometimes with a faint condensation film, and a bare mesh bed starts to feel like lying on a chilly rack. That is when the plush cover shines. The padding lifts Miso off the cold glass and holds a little of her own body heat, so she stays curled and sleepy instead of hopping up and wandering off. If your cat window bed for sleeping is going to be used year-round, a plush option for winter is worth keeping on hand.

I keep both beds and just swap the cover over the same twin-suction frame. It takes me less than a minute, and it means Miso always has the right surface without me buying a whole second hammock.

Washability and keeping it hygienic

Let us be honest — cats shed, and a window perch collects fur, the odd whisker, and the fine coastal dust that drifts in around here. Washability was a big factor for me. Mesh covers tend to rinse clean quickly and dry fast, which suits our humid summer air. Plush covers hold more fur in the pile and take longer to dry, but a gentle cold wash brings them back to soft and fresh. I wash Miso's covers every couple of weeks, or sooner if Wally the Cavoodle decides the empty perch looks like a dog bed.

Whatever you choose, check the care label and avoid harsh heat in the dryer, which can shrink the fabric and loosen the way it sits on the frame. A line dry in the shade has worked perfectly for me.

Sizing and which cats it suits

Before you buy, it is worth being honest about your cat's size. Our standard twin-suction hammock is sourced through 1688 and is suited to small and medium cats up to about 15 kg. It is a brilliant everyday lounge for the average cat, but I would never tell you it is built for a heavy large breed. For a big cat over 15 kg, please look at a reinforced four-suction or steel-cable model instead — that is the honest, safe choice, and I would rather point you the right way than oversell our own product.

Which one did I keep?

I kept both, honestly. The mesh cat window hammock is on the go year-round because Perth summers are long, and the plush cat window perch with cover comes out as soon as the evenings turn crisp. If I could only have one, I would pick mesh for the climate and add a thin blanket on cold nights. That gives me breathability in the heat and a little extra warmth when I need it, without owning two full frames.

The best part, for a renter like me, is that none of this needs a drill or a permanent fixture. The suction cups come off cleanly when I move out, and the fabric covers roll up small in a drawer. Practical, budget-friendly, and Miso-approved — which is the only review that really counts in my flat.

Frequently Asked Questions

Frequently asked questions

Is a mesh or plush cat window hammock better for hot weather?

For hot weather, mesh is the better choice because air moves through the open weave and any breeze reaches your cat. A plush cover holds heat and can feel warm within minutes, so in a Perth summer I keep the mesh bed on the window.

Can I use a plush cover in winter on the same frame?

Yes. Most twin-suction frames let you swap the bed, so you can use a plush cover through the cold months and a mesh one in summer. I keep both and change them over in under a minute.

How do I wash a cat window perch cover?

Mesh covers rinse clean and dry quickly; plush covers do best with a gentle cold wash and a shade line dry. Avoid high dryer heat, which can shrink the fabric and affect how it sits on the frame.

Will your standard hammock hold a large cat?

Our 1688-sourced twin-suction hammock is suited to small and medium cats up to about 15 kg. For large cats over 15 kg we recommend a reinforced four-suction or steel-cable model rather than our standard bed.

Does the fabric affect how secure the suction holds?

The fabric itself does not change the suction; a clean, flat glass surface and firm cups do. Keep the window pane clean and press each cup firmly, and the mount stays put regardless of mesh or plush.

Which fabric should a first-time renter buy?

If you are in a warm climate, start with mesh for breathability, then add a plush cover for winter. It is the most flexible, budget-friendly way to cover every season without buying two full hammocks.
