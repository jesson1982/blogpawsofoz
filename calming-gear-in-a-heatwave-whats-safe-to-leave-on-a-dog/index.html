Calming Gear in a Heatwave: What's Safe to Leave on a Dog

By Rebecca HalloranSep 1, 2026Calming & Anxiety8 min read

Short answer: on a hot day, take the collar, wrap or body suit off. Calming gear sits against the coat and holds warmth, so in a heatwave the honest default is to remove it, cool the room and keep the gear for the cooler ends of the day. If you cannot tell heat from anxiety, ring your vet.

I ran a Saturday class in Southport in January and three people brought me the same question: could the dog keep the collar on through a 38 degree week? One woman had a nine year old staffy who wore a fabric wrap from morning until bedtime, and the wrap was damp when she took it off that evening. Calming gear is designed around behaviour, and nothing in the design brief was ever about a Queensland heatwave. What follows is the checking routine I teach in class: what to look at on the gear itself, which formats survive a hot day, and when the right answer is to take it off and deal with the heat first.

Heat changes what the gear is doing

When the temperature climbs, anything sitting tight against a coat stops doing the job you bought it for. A wrap is meant to give steady pressure. Add a hot room, a dog with a heavy double coat and fabric that does not breathe, and the same wrap becomes a layer you cannot justify. The gear does three physical things in summer: it covers skin, it traps a layer of still air, and it stays damp for longer than you expect. A dry 34 degree day with airflow is a different situation from a humid 28 degree day in a closed room. Buy a cheap thermometer and put it where the dog actually sleeps.

Fit checks that matter in summer

A wrap or collar that fits in July can be too tight in February, because a summer coat is thinner and a hot dog carries less padding under the band. Run two fingers under every band, every time you put it on. Check that the seams sit flat against the skin, and that no fabric has bunched under the front legs, where a body suit creases when the dog lies down. Look at the skin under the band twice a day: behind the ears, under the armpits and along the chest are the places where thin hair and friction meet. If the skin looks different from the day before, stop using the gear and ask your vet.

The other check people forget is the removal test. You should be able to undo the whole thing with one hand, in the dark, in under five seconds, because a hot car park is not the place to learn how a new buckle works.

Materials that hold heat against the coat

Some calming formats are simply warmer than others. A thin flat collar with a pheromone strip is the coolest thing you can put on a dog, because it covers almost nothing. A lined fabric wrap or a full body suit is the warmest, because it covers the chest and back, and that is exactly the surface a dog uses to lose heat. In between sit bandanas, which are easy to soak and easy to remove, and diffusers, which never touch the coat at all. If you want to compare the shapes side by side before you buy, the calming gear listings show what each format actually covers.

  • Keep summer gear thin, open weave and easy to undo with one hand.
  • Check under every band twice a day for damp fabric and rubbed skin.
  • Take gear off for naps, meals, crates, car trips and any time the dog is alone.
  • If the room sits above the mid twenties, use gear only in short supervised blocks.
  • Never layer a wrap, a collar and a shirt on the same dog on a hot day.

Cool the room before you calm the dog

I would rather see an owner spend twenty minutes on airflow than twenty dollars on a new collar. Shade, a fan on the floor, a tiled hallway and a door left open between two rooms all do more for a hot dog than any piece of fabric. If the house is shut up while you are at work, the gear should be off for that stretch. If you genuinely cannot tell whether your dog is uncomfortable from the heat or unsettled by something else, that is a question for your vet. Take the gear off, move the dog to the coolest spot in the house, and make the call.

Two hours on, then a break

I hand out a simple rotation in class. Gear goes on for the event, not for the day. A storm build up at four in the afternoon is a good reason for a collar. A nine hour stretch in a warm house is not. Wrapped formats I would keep to two hours at a time with a check underneath, and always off for naps, meals, crates and car trips. Overnight is the part people get wrong, because the house is often warmer at ten at night than it was at four in the afternoon. Read the overnight forecast, not the daytime one. If the minimum is high, leave the gear off and run a fan instead.

Wash and dry gear properly in summer

Summer gear gets damp, and damp gear is why a lot of collars and wraps end up in the bin early. Follow the care label, wash at the coolest setting it allows, and skip the dryer. Dry everything flat in the shade, because a hot line in full sun will harden elastic and warp a plastic buckle. Keep a spare band or a second collar so one can be washed and dried while the other is in use. Velcro is the other summer problem: hair and sand pack into the hooks and the strap stops holding, so pick it out with a comb once a week. Never put gear back on a dog while it is still damp.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can a calming collar stay on overnight in summer?

Only if the room itself is cool, and even then I would rather see it off. Overnight is when owners stop watching, the house often sits at its warmest and a fabric band can stay damp for hours. Take it off at bedtime, leave a fan running, and put the collar back on in the morning if the day calls for it.

How do I tell whether my dog is hot or anxious?

That is a vet question, and I do not answer it in class. What I teach is the practical step: if you cannot tell the two apart, remove the gear, move the dog to the coolest spot you have and ring your vet for advice. Guessing between two very different problems is how owners lose time.

Should the collar come off before a swim or a bath?

Yes. Water ruins the strip on most pheromone collars, and a soaking wet fabric band sits against the neck and holds moisture. Take the gear off before any water, dry it flat in the shade, and keep a spare collar or band so your dog is not left without one while the first one dries.

Does a wet bandana help in humid weather?

It helps while it is wet and there is air moving over it. In a still, humid room the same cloth warms up with the dog and holds that warmth instead. Check it with your hand every half hour, rewet it or take it off, and never leave a damp bandana on a dog lying still in a closed room.

What if I come home and the gear has been on in a hot house?

Take the gear off straight away, move the dog to the coolest floor in the house and put fresh water down. Then watch him, and ring your vet if anything about him worries you, because I am not going to diagnose that in a blog post. Leave the gear off until the room cools down.
