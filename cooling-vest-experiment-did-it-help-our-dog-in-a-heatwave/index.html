The Cooling Vest Experiment: Did It Help Our Dog in a Heatwave?

By Sophie BennettSep 3, 2026Pet Apparel5 min read

Straight answer: the cooling vest helped, but only as one part of keeping Wally safe, not as a magic fix. During a 41°C Perth day we soaked the vest, wrung it out, and put it on him before a short, shaded walk, and he panted less and slowed down later than on a previous hot walk without it. The vest bought us maybe fifteen calmer minutes, not a free pass to be outside at noon. The real win was timing, water, and shade, with the vest as a helpful extra.

Why we bothered testing it at all

After a scary hot afternoon the summer before, when Wally flopped on the tiles and refused to move, I wanted something beyond a bowl of water and crossed fingers. Cooling vests kept showing up in searches, so when a friend lent us one I decided to actually use it in a proper heatwave rather than trust the marketing.

The vest we tried was the evaporative kind: you soak it, squeeze out the excess, and the slow evaporation is meant to pull heat off the dog. Simple idea, and it matched what I'd read about water-activated cooling.

How the heatwave test went

We picked the worst day on the forecast, soaked the vest in cool tap water, and fitted it snugly but not tight. Wally tolerated it well, which surprised me, and the fabric stayed damp for the whole short walk. His panting was lighter than I expected for that temperature.

The catch is that once the vest dried out, it stopped helping, and a dried vest can actually trap heat. So it is a timed tool, not an all-day one. We re-soaked it after the walk and let him lie on the tiles in front of a fan.

  • Soak and wring the vest just before you head out.
  • Keep walks short, shaded, and early or late.
  • Re-wet the vest the moment it feels dry to the touch.
  • Stop at any sign of heavy panting or wobbliness.

What the vest didn't do

It did not let us skip the basics. No vest replaces fresh water, a cool floor, and staying indoors through the worst of the day. On a 41°C afternoon the smartest thing we did was cancel the walk entirely and just play inside, with the damp vest on for comfort.

I also would not trust a vest on a dog left in a yard, because once it dries the benefit is gone and the dog is still hot. Supervision matters more than the gear.

Would I buy one now?

Yes, but with realistic expectations. For early-morning summer walks and camping trips it is genuinely useful, and it pairs well with the broader set in our pet apparel range. It is not a substitute for common sense on a brutal day.

Tips I'd pass to other Perth owners

If you try a cooling vest, treat it like a wet towel with a job, not a gadget. Soak it properly rather than sprinkling it, and wring it so it sits against the skin instead of dripping down the legs. I keep a second one in the esky on camping trips so there is always a cold backup once the first dries out.

The bigger lesson was about me. I used to stretch our walks because Wally seemed fine, then regretted it when he crashed at home. A vest buys comfort, not extra time in the sun, so I now set a phone timer and turn back before he shows any strain.

For flat-faced dogs, seniors, or any pet with a health condition, have a word with your vet before relying on one, because some issues make heat riskier. The vest is support, not a green light, and the owners who do best pair it with shade, water, and plain good judgement on the worst days of summer.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do dog cooling vests actually work?

The evaporative type does take heat off the dog while it is wet, which can ease panting on a warm walk. The effect is real but temporary, lasting only as long as the fabric stays damp, so treat it as support rather than a cure for heat.

Can a cooling vest be dangerous?

A dried-out vest can trap warmth, and a too-tight one can rub. Never leave a dog in a vest unsupervised in heat, and remove it the moment it feels dry. The vest is a tool, not a replacement for shade and water.

How long does a soaked vest stay cool?

On a very hot, dry day ours went from damp to dry in roughly twenty minutes, which is when we re-wet it. Humidity slows drying, so in stickier weather it may last longer, but check it by touch rather than guessing.

What age or breed benefits most?

Flat-faced breeds, older dogs, and small pups with thin coats feel heat fastest, so they get the most from a vest on warm walks. Still, every dog is different, and a vet can advise if your pet has health issues that make heat riskier.

Should I use it for indoor cooling too?

A freshly soaked vest can comfort a restless dog on a hot night, paired with a fan and tiles. Just don't leave it on once dry, and make sure your dog can move away from it if he wants to.
