Small Dog and Front-Seat Covers: Safe Ways to Travel Little Pets

By Mick O'DonnellAug 9, 2026Dog Car Seat Covers6 min read

Short answer: the airbag matters more than the cover, so a little dog goes in the back unless the passenger airbag is switched off. The missus's Jack Russell cross reckons the front seat is its throne, and the setup that keeps it there is a booster with sides, tethered to a harness rather than a collar. If you only fix one thing, make it the harness, because a small dog's trachea will not take a sudden load.

Banjo is a Heeler, all muscle, so the front seat is not his scene. But the missus has a little Jack Russell cross that thinks the front passenger seat is its throne. Small dogs are a different problem to big ones. They can slip between the seats, they get lost in the footwell, and — this is the big one — they sit right next to the airbag. A small dog car seat cover is only half the conversation. The other half is keeping that little mate alive if the airbag goes off.

I will say the quiet part loud: a dog in the front seat next to a live airbag is a real risk. The airbag is built for a human adult, not a five-kilo dog. If it deploys, it does so with force that can badly hurt or kill a small pet. So before we talk covers and boosters, the rule is simple — if the dog is up front, the passenger airbag must be off, or the dog should not be up front at all.

The front seat cover itself

A front seat cover for dog use is a smaller version of the back-seat one. It protects the seat from claws and dirt, and the good ones have a non-slip backing so the little fella does not slide when you brake. For a small dog, you want a cover that stays put, because a sliding cover slides the dog with it, and that is how they end up under your feet on the footpath merge.

Look for the same build we trust in the boot: 600D Oxford with a PP cotton fill and an EVA backing. It will not be chewed through by a tiny set of teeth, and it wipes clean after a trip to the beach. The PU leather versions are smart and wipe even easier, but they warm up, so again, never leave a dog in a parked car in our summer.

Harness and seat-belt tether

The safe way to have a small dog up front is a harness clipped to the seat-belt. The tether is short, the dog stays in the seat, and it cannot launch at your face when a roo bolts across the road. The harness spreads the load across the chest, which matters even more for a tiny dog whose neck is fragile. Never use a collar with a tether — a small dog's trachea is delicate and a sharp stop can do real harm.

Booster seats for the little ones

A booster seat is a small basket that clips to the seat and lifts the dog up so it can see out the window. Dogs seem to love them; Banjo would never fit, but the Jack Russell thinks it is the captain's chair. The booster still needs a tether to the harness, and it still needs the airbag off. What it adds is security — the sides stop the dog bouncing out, and the height keeps it calm because it can watch the world go by.

What is never safe

An unrestrained dog in the front is the worst option. It becomes a projectile in a crash, it distracts the driver, and it is right in the airbag's path. A dog on a driver's lap is asking for trouble — you cannot brake properly and the airbag will get the dog first. We have all seen it done. That does not make it right.

If your car will not let you switch the passenger airbag off, the answer is to put the small dog in the back with a proper cover and tether. The back seat is the safer place for any dog, big or small, and a small dog car seat cover works just as well behind you as it does in front of you.

A word on the beach run

Little dogs love the sand as much as Banjo does, and they bring just as much of it home. A front cover that zips off and washes makes the post-beach cleanup a two-minute job. We keep a towel in the esky for the worst of it, but the cover does the heavy lifting. Banjo reckons the little dog gets spoiled; Banjo reckons a lot of things.

The honest take: a small dog in the front is fine if you do it properly. Airbag off, harness on, tether short, cover down. Skip any of those and you are gambling with a mate who trusts you to keep them safe.

Frequently Asked Questions

Frequently asked questions

Can a small dog ride in the front passenger seat?

Yes, but only with the passenger airbag deactivated and the dog restrained by a harness tether or booster. If your car cannot switch the airbag off, keep the dog in the back.

Why is the airbag such a danger to small dogs?

Airbags deploy with force meant for an adult human. A small dog on the seat is directly in that path and can be badly injured or killed. Always disable the airbag or move the dog to the back.

What is the best front seat cover for dog travel?

A cover in 600D Oxford with PP cotton and an EVA backing that grips the seat. Pair it with a harness tether — the cover protects the seat, the tether protects the dog.

Is a booster seat safe for a tiny dog up front?

Yes, provided the airbag is off and the booster still tethers the dog's harness. The sides contain the dog and the height keeps it calm by letting it see out.

Should I use a collar or harness with the tether?

Always a harness. A small dog's neck and trachea are delicate; a collar under sudden load can cause injury. The harness spreads the force across the chest.

Is the back seat better than the front for a small dog?

Generally yes. The back avoids the airbag entirely and is the safer spot for any dog. A small dog car seat cover and tether work just as well in the back as the front.
