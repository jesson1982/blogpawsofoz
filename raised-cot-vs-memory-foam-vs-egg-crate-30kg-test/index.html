Raised cot vs memory foam vs egg crate: a 30kg test

By Jess AllenSep 4, 2026Beds & Mats5 min read

Short answer: with a 30kg dog, the raised cot won on cooling and toughness, the memory foam won on steady support, and the egg crate was the cheapest but flattened fastest. After a month of daily use, washes and a Perth heat spell, I would stock the cot for summer and the memory foam for older dogs, and treat egg crate as a short term filler. None are bad, they just suit different jobs and different weights, and the mistake owners make is buying one bed and expecting it to do all three roles through every season and every stage of the dog's life.

How we ran the test

One 30kg kelpie mix, three beds, four weeks. Each bed got a week as the main night bed, then shared duty. We weighed support by hand press and by watching how he rose, washed covers on a normal cycle, and logged which bed he chose on forty degree days. No lab, just a real dog and a real laundry, which is the only test that matters to the people who actually buy from my store and come back with questions.

The raised cot

The cot lifted him off the floor, which in our heat was the clear winner for comfort. The fabric stayed cool, the frame took his weight without a creak, and the hose down cleaned it in seconds. Downside: on cold nights he wanted more, and a cot alone is not orthopaedic support for sore joints, so I would never sell it as the only bed for an older dog, only as the summer and travel piece.

  • Best for: summer, outdoors, chewers.
  • Weak for: cold nights, senior joint support.
  • Wash: hose, drip dry, fastest of the three.

The memory foam bed

The memory foam kept its loft under 30kg better than I expected and he rose easier in the mornings, which matters for an older style dog. The cover unzipped and washed clean. It held heat more than the cot, so in a heat wave he sometimes chose the tiles beside it rather than on it, and that is the honest trade: great support, warmer sleep, which suits our winters better than our February afternoons.

The egg crate bed

The egg crate was the budget pick and felt soft on day one, but the bumps flattened where he lay within two weeks. It still beat the floor and washed fine, yet as support it faded fastest. For a growing puppy or a spare room it is fair, but I would not call it a long term joint bed, and I tell customers so plainly rather than letting the low price do the talking for me.

What this means for the shelf

In my store I steer big dogs to the cot in summer and memory foam year round if joints are a worry, and I keep egg crate as the cheap spare. Margins are better on the premium beds, but the honest match to the dog is what brings people back, so I sell the right one, not the priciest, because a returned bed costs more than a lost margin ever does.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which bed is best for a heavy dog in summer?

For a 30kg plus dog in Australian heat, the raised cot is my pick because it lifts them off the hot floor and air moves underneath. Pair it with a memory foam bed for cool nights if joints are a concern. The cot alone is great for cooling but offers little cushioning for an older dog's hips, so think of it as the summer half of a two bed plan.

Is egg crate foam a waste of money?

Not always. It is a fair cheap option for a puppy, a crate liner or a spare room, and it washed without trouble. But under a heavy adult dog it loses shape quickly, so treat it as short term. If you need real support that lasts, step up to memory foam or a cot, because the egg crate is a stopgap, not a solution for a big dog.

Do raised cots hold big dogs safely?

A good one does. Check the weight rating and the join quality, because a cheap frame can wobble or sag. The cot we tested rated well above 30kg and stayed solid, and the fabric cleaned fast. Read the rating, not the photo, before you trust it with a heavy dog, and hose it rather than machine it if the maker says so.

Why did he skip the memory foam on hot days?

Memory foam traps body heat, so on a forty degree Perth afternoon he chose the cool tiles next to it. That is normal for the material. If your dog does the same, add a cot or a cooling mat for summer and keep the foam bed for night, rather than fighting the heat with one bed that was never built to win the hottest days.

What would you actually buy for your store?

I stock the cot and the memory foam as the core range and keep egg crate as the budget line. The cot sells itself in summer and the foam bed sells to owners of older dogs, while the egg crate covers the tight budget. Matching the bed to the dog beats pushing the highest margin item every time, and my regulars know I will steer them honest.
