Two Cats, One Apartment, and the Toys That Survived the Rotation

By Ellie NguyenSep 5, 2026Cat & Dog Toys6 min read

One cat in a small flat is straightforward. Two cats in a one-bedroom flat is a small diplomacy problem with fur. Every toy becomes a potential resource, every play session becomes a scheduling question, and the floor space you had budgeted for toys has to double without the flat getting bigger. After two years of Mochi and Pepper in a Melbourne rental, I have a rotation that works, a shorter list of toys that failed, and a much better understanding of why the failures failed.

Why two cats change the toy maths

With one cat, a toy is entertainment. With two, it is also a claim. Cats in a shared territory manage access to resources pretty carefully, and toys are resources even when there is no food involved: a wand becomes a target both cats want, a tunnel becomes a piece of claimed territory, and a puzzle feeder becomes a contested position. That reframes everything. You are not buying toys for two cats, you are buying enough parallel access that neither cat has to spend its day managing the other one. In a small flat, parallel access is a design constraint, not a nice-to-have.

Resource guarding over toys, the quiet version

Nobody tells you that guarding can look like nothing at all. There was no growling in my flat. What I noticed instead was Pepper sitting at a distance watching Mochi play, then over-grooming, then sleeping in the bathroom more often. That was guarding expressed as withdrawal, which is the feline equivalent of letting somebody else use the computer and quietly resenting it for a week. The fixes were structural rather than behavioural: two of everything small, play sessions that run in different rooms at overlapping times, and a rule that toys in use are never left lying around to be claimed. If your second cat is spending more time alone than it used to, look at resources before you look at personality.

The three-toy rotation that ended the boredom cycle

Three toys out, everything else in the tub, rotated weekly. In a two-cat flat the rotation does a second job: it breaks up territory claims, because a tunnel that appears on Tuesday and vanishes on Sunday never becomes anybody's property. Novelty does the work that a bigger flat cannot. My set is deliberately boring: a wand with replaceable heads, a folding tunnel, and one chaseable small toy per cat, swapped weekly for two alternatives from the tub. The cats get variety, the flat gets its floor back, and nobody owns the tunnel.

Scent sharing and the toy that means comfort

The most useful toy in a two-cat flat is not a toy at all, it is a scent object. Cats recognise each other by smell, and a shared item that carries both scents makes each other feel less foreign. I keep one soft bed-mat that both cats sleep on and wash rarely, plus their own separate sleeping spots, and I rub the same cloth on both cats when they have been apart at the vet. That is the trick that turned two strangers into an alliance, more than any toy I bought, and it costs nothing.

Cleaning routine in a small flat

Small space means smells concentrate, so toy hygiene matters more than it would in a house. Fabric toys get washed when they smell of licked fur, plastics get a warm soapy rinse every couple of weeks, and the rotation tub gets wiped out monthly because crumbs and fur collect in the bottom. Interactive toys I wipe down after play, especially the wand handle, and anything with feathers gets checked for loose glue. Fifteen minutes a month keeps the flat smelling like a flat rather than a cattery, which in a rental matters for reasons beyond comfort.

What I would tell a two-cat renter

Buy two of everything small, keep almost nothing out permanently, and rotate rather than accumulate. Put the interactive play on a schedule in separate rooms, because a shared wand session in a small flat teaches one cat to watch and the other to perform. Watch for withdrawal rather than conflict, since the quiet cat is the one who pays. And keep one shared scent object that both cats use, because in a small flat the relationship between the two cats is the whole game, and gear exists to support it rather than replace it.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do two cats in a small flat need twice as many toys?

Twice as many of the small, contested ones, and no more of anything big. Balls, plush mice and puzzle feeders should exist in duplicate so neither cat has to negotiate access. Larger items like tunnels and trees can be shared if you rotate them rather than leaving them out, because an object that comes and goes never becomes anyone's territory.

How do I tell if my cats are fighting over toys?

Usually through the behaviour you do not hear: one cat watching from a distance, avoiding the room, sleeping somewhere new, or grooming more than usual. Overt hissing is the loud version and often the late one. If one cat's daily routine has shifted since a toy arrived, that toy is probably the reason, and rotating it out for a fortnight is a cheap experiment.

Should I play with both cats at the same time?

Briefly, sometimes, with a toy big enough for both, but not as your main routine. In a small flat, a shared session tends to become one cat playing and one cat monitoring, and the monitor is the one who loses out. Two shorter separate sessions in different rooms works far better, and it is what I do twice a day without fail, even when it means twenty minutes rather than ten.

How often should I wash cat toys in a flat?

Fabric toys when they smell of licked fur, which in a small space is more often than you would expect, and plastic or wand toys every couple of weeks with a warm rinse. Avoid strong detergents, since cats rely on scent and a toy that smells of laundry liquid is a toy that has been made strange. Warm water and a dry in the sun does most of the work.

What is the single best purchase for two cats in a small space?

A wand toy with replaceable heads, used separately with each cat, twice a day. It takes no floor space, costs the price of a coffee, and gives both cats the one thing a small flat cannot provide on its own: a proper hunt. Everything else in my cupboard is support for that toy, and the cats would agree if they had any interest in the question.
