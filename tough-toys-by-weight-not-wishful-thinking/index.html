Tough Toys by Weight, Not Wishful Thinking

By David ThompsonJun 19, 2026Cat & Dog Toys2 min read

Short answer: toughness is decided by your dog's weight, not the word on the packet, because Duke disproved most 'durable' claims in a fortnight and a toy rated for ten kilos on a thirty-five kilo Staffy is a choking risk. The truth is going one size up and choosing reinforced rubber or woven rope over thin vinyl matters whenever he's unsupervised. A ten-second evening check of every toy, cracks and frayed rope, is the habit that keeps the vet bill away.

Every toy packet says "durable". Duke has disproven most of them within a fortnight. The lesson I've learned the expensive way is that toughness isn't about the label — it's about matching the toy to the dog doing the chewing, and weight is the fastest guide.

Size up, check the label honestly

A toy rated for a 10 kg dog given to a 35 kg Staffy is a choking risk, not a bargain. Go one size up if your dog is between bands, and pick reinforced rubber or tightly-woven rope over thin vinyl and squeaky plush for anything unsupervised. Here's the cheat sheet I wish I'd had:

Dog weightToy classRealistic lifespan (power chewer)
Under 10 kgSmall rubber, ropeMonths
10–25 kgMedium reinforced1–3 months
25–40 kgLarge, solid rubber3–6 months
40 kg+ / known destroyerX-strong rated, supervised onlyWeeks to months

And a habit worth stealing from the clinic: an evening ten-second check of every toy. Cracks, exposed squeakers, fraying rope — straight in the bin. The cheapest toy is the one that never becomes a vet bill.

Frequently Asked Questions

Frequently asked questions

Are any dog toys truly indestructible?

No — treat "indestructible" as marketing. Solid rubber comes closest; inspect weekly.

What size toy does my dog need?

Match toy size to weight, and size up when in between — small toys are choking risks.

Why does my dog destroy everything?

Boredom plus breed jaw strength. Enrichment toys and rotation give the urge somewhere to go.

Are rope toys OK for power chewers?

For tugging yes; if the dog eats fibres, switch to solid rubber.

When should I throw a toy out?

The moment it cracks, splits or exposes small parts.
