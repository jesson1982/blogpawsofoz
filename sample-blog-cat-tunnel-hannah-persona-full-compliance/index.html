Sample Blog — Cat Tunnel (Hannah persona, full compliance)

By Hannah WrightJul 1, 2026Cat & Dog Toys5 min read
SAMPLE POSTPersona: HannahCompliance: full

A vet nurse's honest take on why a simple collapsible tunnel became the most-used toy in our flat — and how to choose one that actually lasts.

When Pippa arrived as a foster, she spent most of her days wedged behind the lounge, watching the world from a safe gap she'd claimed as her own. Like a lot of rescue cats, she wasn't unfriendly — she was unsure. Toys with bells and balls of yarn sat ignored. What finally got her moving was something I'd almost overlooked: a plain cat tunnel.

There's a reason tunnels work where a dangling mouse didn't. A tunnel turns a quiet loungeroom into a place worth investigating. The cat gets to hide, peek, and bolt from one end to the other, and none of it feels like a demand on them. For a shy cat, that choice matters.

Why tunnels tap into the hunt

Cats are built to stalk. A curved or multi-opening tunnel hides the exit, so every dash becomes a small mystery to solve. The crinkle of the fabric and a dangling ball add just enough noise and movement to spark curiosity without overwhelming a nervous pet. In our place, an S-shape design worked best — Pippa couldn't see through it, so each trip felt like a fresh ambush.

If you share a smaller home, a quiet version with a soft faux-fur lining is worth considering. Standard crinkle nylon is fun but can sound like a chip packet in a tiled apartment; the quieter cloth keeps playtime neighbour-friendly.

Picking a size that fits your cat

Diameter matters more than people expect. A kitten will vanish happily into almost anything, but a grown cat — let alone a broad-chested breed — needs room to turn around. Here's the quick guide I give new fosterers:

ShapeBest forDiameterLength / spread
2-way (straight)Kittens, small cats, solo play~25 cm~60 cm
3-wayMulti-cat homes, hide & seek~25 cm~90 cm total
Long straightZoomie runs along a hallway~25 cm~120 cm
T-shapePeek-and-bolt games~25 cm~75 cm
Large / big-catMaine Coon, Ragdoll, chunky adults~30 cmvaries

Most tunnels fold flat in seconds thanks to a sprung-steel frame, which is a blessing in a rental where storage space is scarce. Look for covered wire ends so the frame doesn't poke through after a few enthusiastic pounces.

A few safety notes from the clinic

Tunnels are low-risk, but a couple of habits keep them that way. Check the seams and frame weekly — claws are relentless. Choose washable fabric so you can freshen it between fosters. And supervise the first few sessions; some cats get so carried away they briefly forget where the entrance is.

One more thing worth saying: a tunnel is enrichment, not a substitute for time with you. Ten minutes with a wand toy at the tunnel mouth does more for a timid cat than any object left on its own. Pippa still prefers the wand, but now she'll chase it into the tunnel, which is a long way from hiding behind the couch.

Frequently asked questions

Are cat tunnels safe for kittens?

Yes, for supervised play. Pick a soft-framed, washable tunnel and check the wire ends are covered. Remove it if a kitten starts chewing the frame.

My cat ignored every toy — will a tunnel help?

Often, yes. Tunnels invite self-led exploration rather than direct interaction, which suits shy or rescue cats. Give it a few days in a quiet corner before judging.

What size tunnel does a large cat need?

Aim for at least a 30 cm diameter for big breeds like Maine Coons and Ragdolls so they can turn around comfortably.

Can I join tunnels together?

Many designs connect with ties or zippered doors, letting you build a larger play space. Just keep one end open as an easy exit.

How do I clean a cat tunnel?

Most polyester tunnels wipe down with a damp cloth and mild soap. Avoid machine washing if the steel frame isn't removable, as it can rust.

Is a quiet tunnel better for apartments?

If you're in a unit or near bedrooms, a faux-fur or cloth tunnel is far quieter than crinkle nylon and less likely to annoy neighbours or sleeping family.
