Perch and Bookshelf: Mixing Mounted and Freestanding Spots

By Ellie NguyenSep 2, 2026Cat Window Perches7 min read

Short answer: a mounted perch and freestanding cat furniture solve different problems, and the strongest small-flat setup uses both. The perch owns the window and the height without eating floor space, while freestanding pieces add climbing, scratching and territory you can rearrange, and pack up and take with you when the lease ends.

I write this from a one-bedroom flat in Melbourne where every square metre is contested by me, two cats and a landlord who inspects twice a year. For the first year we had a single suction perch on the lounge window and nothing else. The cats argued over it, the younger one started treating my bookshelf as a climbing frame, and two paperback casualties later I had to admit the setup was not working. The fix was not buying more gear. It was deciding what each piece of cat furniture should actually do, then arranging one mounted perch and two freestanding pieces so they work as a single system. That arrangement has now survived two inspections, one hot summer and both cats, and it leaves the walls almost untouched.

Why one perch is never enough

A window perch answers one specific want: height with a view. It does nothing about scratching, nothing about the midnight zoomies, and nothing for a cat who wants to be up high but out of the direct sun. In a small flat those gaps show quickly, because the cat fills them with your furniture. Mine chose a Billy bookcase, then the curtains, then the top of the kitchen cabinets. The trick that finally worked was to stop thinking of cat gear as one product and start treating it as a small furniture system. A mounted perch covers the window. A freestanding climber covers the floor-to-somewhere route. A scratcher covers the sofa arm. Each piece does one job well, and none of them has to do everything, which is what keeps the footprint small and the walls unmarked.

What the mounted perch does best

In this flat the mounted piece is a hammock-style perch bracketed into the reveal of the lounge window. Four small screws, all of which I can fill and touch up at move-out in an afternoon. It holds its shape through summer heat in a way no suction cup I have owned has managed, and it sits at the exact height the cats had already chosen, which is sill height plus a forearm. That is the core value of a mounted spot. It goes where the cat wants, at a height you control, and it stays put under a bounding two-stage landing. Everything else in the arrangement can then lean on it. The freestanding pieces do not need to reach the window, because the perch already owns that real estate, so they can sit where they suit the room instead.

Where freestanding pieces earn their floor

Freestanding cat furniture gets a bad name in small flats because so much of it is wide, carpeted and beige. The way around that is to treat floor space like a budget. A tall, narrow climber stands in the gap between the bookshelf and the wall, in the same footprint a floor lamp would claim. A slim scratching post sits beside the sofa arm that was being shredded, which redirects the behaviour to the right surface. A low bench-style perch under the bedroom window gives the needier cat a spot of her own with a view but no competition. Every piece earns its metres by doing a job the mounted perch cannot, and each one can be dragged to a new corner, sold or given away the day we move out.

The two-cat layout that worked

The layout matters as much as the gear. The mounted perch faces the street end of the lounge, where the bird traffic is. The tall climber stands about a metre away, close enough that a nervous cat can bolt from perch to climber without touching the floor. The scratcher sits at the sofa, and the bedroom bench perch closes the loop at the far end of the flat. That gives each cat a high spot and an exit route, which did more to end the window stand-offs than any new purchase. If you are starting from nothing, it is worth browsing cat window perches first to work out which mount style suits your frames, then choosing the freestanding pieces to fill whatever roles the perch leaves open.

  • Decide what each piece must do before you buy anything new.
  • Mount the perch where the cat already wants to be.
  • Give each cat a high spot with a clear exit route.
  • Choose narrow, movable freestanding pieces that fit dead space.
  • Pick mounts you can patch and repaint at move-out.

Why this mix suits a rental

A fully freestanding setup is safe for the bond but limits you to whatever footprint you can spare. A fully mounted wall system climbs higher than any rental wall should. The mix splits the difference. One small mounted commitment, four screws in a reveal that will be filled and repainted, buys the anchor spot. Everything riskier stays freestanding, so it can be moved when the agent visits, rearranged when the cats change habits or taken straight to the next place. Nothing is glued to glass, nothing leans marks into the plaster, and felt pads under every base keep the floorboards honest. For a renter who also wants a home that still looks like a home, that trade feels right.

Swapping pieces without new holes

The best part of a mixed setup is that it can change. When the cats went off the bedroom bench last winter, it moved to the hallway and became a launching step to the cabinet top. When the climber wobbled, new felt pads went under it in a minute. If you ever do add a second mounted piece, pick the spot by watching where the cats already sit, not by where a photo online looks good. One rule has held all year: nothing new enters the flat unless it either fills a job the current setup leaves open or replaces a piece that failed. Small flats do not forgive clutter, and neither do inspection reports.

Frequently Asked Questions

Will a mounted perch cost me my bond?

Not if you plan the patching. The four screws in my window reveal get filled with filler, sanded and touched up with matching paint before the final inspection. Take photos when you install so you can show exactly what you changed, and mention it to your agent. A small, disclosed, properly repaired hole in a reveal is a very different thing to hidden damage.

Can suction perches work in a mixed setup?

They can, as the light-duty option. I still use a small suction sill perch on a shaded window, but I never trust one with a big cat, a warm window or a long absence. Check the seal by hand each week and clean the glass before remounting. In a mixed setup a suction perch is a bonus seat, never the anchor.

How much floor space do the freestanding pieces need?

Less than you would expect if you choose narrow pieces. My climber claims a footprint of roughly forty by forty centimetres, the scratcher about the same, and the bench perch sits in a spot that would otherwise hold nothing. The honest answer is that a one-bedroom flat can fit all of it, but only if every piece earns its place.

What if one cat hogs the mounted perch?

Add a second option at a different height rather than a second perch at the same height. My layout works because the perch, the climber platform and the bench perch sit at different levels and in different rooms. Cats negotiate heights far more easily than they share one spot. If the stand-offs continue, that is a behaviour question for your vet.

Do I need my landlord's permission to mount a perch?

It depends on your lease and your state or territory's rules, so read the agreement and ask the agent in writing before drilling. Some landlords are relaxed about small, repairable fixings; others want everything freestanding. A short message describing what you plan to install and how you will repair it usually gets a clear answer and keeps you covered.
