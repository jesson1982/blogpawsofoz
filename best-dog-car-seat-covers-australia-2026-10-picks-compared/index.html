Best Dog Car Seat Covers Australia 2026: 10 Picks Compared

By Mick O'DonnellAug 23, 2026Dog Car Seat Covers8 min read

Mick from the Bush — dog car-travel & ute gear

Banjo and I have covered a fair few kilometres together over the years. Fifteen of them, to be exact. He's been my working Blue Heeler since he was a pup, first out on the paddock helping me move sheep, now riding in the ute on the long drive into town or down to the coast for a proper run on the beach. Any dog that works hard brings the outside world back with him. Mud after rain, salt after the surf, the odd stick he decides belongs in the cabin. If you've watched a Heeler shake himself dry across a clean seat, you already know why the first bit of kit I tell new dog owners to buy is a proper dog car seat cover.

Out here in regional NSW the shelves at the servo and the local pet place are a mixed bag. Some of it is good, some of it falls apart after one trip. So I sat down, went through what's actually available across Australia for 2026, and put the honest ones side by side. This isn't a sponsored list. It's me, Banjo reckons, and a tape measure, sorting the gear that holds up from the gear that doesn't.

Below you'll find ten picks that run from the cheap soft hammocks right through to the hard-bottom extenders and the leather premium jobs. The point is you can read one page, work out what fits your car, your dog, and your budget, and not waste an afternoon scrolling.

What I reckon matters most

Before the table, a word on what actually counts when you're buying a car dog seat cover. I've learnt these the hard way, usually with a wet dog and a ruined seat.

Waterproofing comes first. On the property the dog is wet more often than he's dry. Beach trips mean saltwater that'll rot a cheap liner in a season. Rain means muddy paws. A cover that soaks through is worse than no cover, because it traps the damp against your seat. Look for a coated Oxford cloth with a backing layer, not a towel with a strap.

Non-slip backing is the second one people forget. Banjo slid like a stunned mullet the first time I braked hard with a soft towel-type mat. You want EVA or a rubberised backing so the cover stays put through the corners on the bitumen into town.

Fit is the third. A hammock that clips to the front headrests stops the dog falling into the footwell. A bench cover just lies across the seat. An extender builds a hard floor out over the footwell so a big dog can actually lie down instead of sliding into the dark.

Then there's the small stuff that adds up: door flaps to save your panels, a mesh window so the dog can see you and settles down, storage pockets for leads and the lead bag, and whether it'll go through the washing machine without shrinking.

Reading the table

The budget end, around thirty to forty-five dollars, is where you'll find the soft hammocks like the OUYOOOO and the entry-level universal pad. They do the job for a small dog or a short trip to the servo. They won't survive a full-grown cattle dog who thinks the back seat is a trampoline, and the backing is thinner, so they move about. For a first cover or a calm little dog, they're fine.

In the middle, fifty to seventy dollars, is where the real value sits. The CHANCCI hammock, the PETICON extender, the Dehlso, the INSMEER and the Laadd or Lamicall hard-bottom units all live here. This is the band I'd spend my own money in. The PETICON and the Laadd both use a rigid board and a rated capacity up around 160 to 195 kilos, which is plenty for a big dog and means the floor won't bow when Banjo flops down.

The leather jobs from Pecute and BONTHEE sit at the top, eighty to a hundred and ten dollars. They look the part and they're easy to wipe, but Banjo's claws would have the surface marked in a week, so I'd only bother if the dog is calm and the car is the good one you keep for town.

The boot liners — NYLSYT, Alfheim, MIXJOY, F-color, Bulldogology and Kurgo — are a different animal. If your dog travels in the cargo area of an SUV rather than the back seat, that's the line to look at, and the bumper flap versions save the paint when he jumps out at the lake.

How I'd spend it

If you've got a working dog or a large breed, skip the soft hammock and go straight to a hard-bottom extender in that fifty to seventy band. The PETICON at 51.2 by 60.5 inches gives a proper floor, and the Laadd or Lamicall rated to 430 pounds is the one I'd pick for a really big animal. For the best dog car seat cover Australia buyers with a big dog can buy, that hard floor is the difference between a dog that fits and a dog that doesn't.

For a small dog or the occasional trip, the OUYOOOO at thirty-odd dollars is fine. Keep it clean, don't expect it to last forever, and upgrade when the dog earns it. A dog seat cover Australia families use for a weekly run to the park doesn't need the heavy gear.

For the beach regular, the waterproof car seat cover for pets Australia buyers want is the one with the coated backing and door flaps. Salt finds every gap, so cover the doors too, not just the seat. I rinse Banjo's cover with the hose out the back of the property and hang it on the fence, and it's still going strong.

A note on the ute

Most ute owners reckon the dog rides in the tray. Fair enough in winter, but in the heat of summer the tray is no place for a dog, and a ute with a back seat still needs a cover. The bench and hammock forms fit a dual-cab ute fine. Just measure first — some of the extenders are built for SUVs and run wide, and you don't want a cover that won't clip to your headrests.

Banjo's verdict

He doesn't care which one. He cares that the seat's there and the window's down. But I care about the seats, and after fifteen years I've learnt the cheap ones cost more in the end once you count the detailing and the replacement trim. Buy once, buy the hard bottom, and the car stays clean. That's the list. Pick to your dog and your budget, and you'll wonder why you put up with a filthy cabin for so long.

Frequently asked questions

What's the best dog car seat cover Australia buyers can get for a big dog?

For a large or working dog, a hard-bottom extender in the fifty to seventy dollar band is the pick. The Laadd or Lamicall units are rated to 430 pounds and the PETICON gives a rigid floor at 51.2 by 60.5 inches, so the dog can lie flat instead of sliding into the footwell.

Are the cheap soft hammocks worth it?

For a small, calm dog or short trips they're fine, and the OUYOOOO at thirty to forty-five dollars does the basics. They won't last under a big dog and the backing is thinner, so they move about on sharp corners. Treat them as a starter, not a forever cover.

Do I really need door flaps?

If your dog shakes, scratches at the window, or you do beach runs, yes. Door flaps save your panels from water marks and claw scuffs. Most hammock forms include them; bench covers often don't, so check before you buy.

Can these covers go in the washing machine?

Many of the Oxford cloth and composite ones can, on a gentle cycle, though I hose mine off on the property and let it dry on the fence. The PU leather jobs shouldn't go in the machine — wipe them instead. Always check the label.

Will a cover fit my dual-cab ute?

The bench and hammock forms clip to the headrests and fit most dual-cab utes. The wide hard-bottom extenders are built with SUVs in mind and can run wide, so measure your seat before ordering.

What about saltwater and the beach?

Pick a coated, waterproof cover with door flaps and rinse it with fresh water after the surf. Salt will eat a cheap liner fast, so the backing layer matters more than the colour. Hang it to dry before you fold it away.
