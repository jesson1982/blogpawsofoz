Perch Colour and Material: Matching the Window Frame

By Sofia MarchettiSep 2, 2026Cat Window Perches7 min read

Short answer: Match the perch to the window frame you already have, then let the cushion do the talking. Frame colour and hardware finish decide whether the perch reads as part of the window or as something bolted on, and that is the difference most people notice from the street.

A Bulimba client rang me about a perch her cat had stopped using. The cat was fine. The perch was glossy black, mounted on a cream aluminium frame in a room of pale oak and linen, and it looked like a bicycle rack on a wedding cake. We changed nothing structural. We swapped the frame finish and the cushion, kept the position exactly as it was, and the cat was back on it inside a week. Most of the perch work I do is that unglamorous: colour, finish and fibre, settled before anyone drills anything. It is also the part buyers skip, because the hardware aisle is louder than the paint chart.

Read the frame colour before anything else

Aluminium and timber frames in Australian homes fall into a few families, and each one has a perch finish that sits naturally beside it. Gloss white and cream frames want a white, ivory or pale grey perch frame. Charcoal and matte black frames want matte black or dark bronze, never chrome. Clear-sealed timber wants oiled timber, or matte black if the room is already busy. Bronze anodised frames, common on 1970s and 1980s brick homes, want dark bronze or black and look wrong with anything shiny. Take a photo of the frame in daylight rather than at night under a warm globe, and hold it beside the perch you are considering. A perch in the same family as the frame stops the eye at the window instead of at the bracket.

One thing worth knowing before you shop: window frames are rarely one flat colour. Aluminium picks up the sky and reads cooler or warmer through the day, so a perch that matches at noon can look green against the same frame at dusk. Comparing samples in the afternoon, when the light is flattest, gives you a more honest match.

Match the hardware to the window fittings

Look at the hinges, winders and latches on the actual window before you choose brackets. If those fittings are brushed stainless, use brushed stainless. If they are black, use black. If the home is old enough to have brass, brass works, and a modern chrome bracket on the same window will look like a rental repair. Finish matters more than most buyers expect, because the bracket sits within a hand's width of the frame and gets compared with it constantly. Powder-coated and anodised finishes read flatter and age better than high-gloss chrome, which scratches and shows every fingerprint. One caveat I give every client: if the perch your cat needs comes in only one bracket finish, keep the bracket that holds and change the perch frame colour instead. The animal's safety outranks the palette.

Cushion fabric and colour that survive cats

The cushion is where colour goes wrong fastest. Start by pulling a tone out of something already in the room, the rug, the curtain, the throw on the sofa, rather than choosing from the perch listing. A tonal cushion in oatmeal, stone, sage or charcoal will still look considered in three years. A bright novelty print will not. On fibre, a tight-weave solution-dyed acrylic or a mid-weight cotton canvas holds colour and resists snagging, while fleece pills and loose linen catches claws and looks tired within a season. Look for removable covers you can wash at 30 degrees and dry in the shade rather than the dryer. Dark grey and mid-tone stone hide cat hair better than cream or black, which are the two hardest colours to keep looking clean on a perch.

  • Pull a colour from the rug or curtain, not the perch catalogue.
  • Match bracket and screw finish to the window hinges, not the perch frame.
  • A tight weave outlasts fleece and loose linen on a daily-use perch.
  • Choose the cushion after the frame is mounted and you can see the light.
  • One colour family across bed and perch keeps an open room calm.

The perch is seen from both sides

Stand on the footpath and look at your own window. From outside in daylight the perch is a silhouette against your interior, so a heavy dark frame on a white window draws a hard line through the glass. From inside at night the same perch becomes a silhouette against black glass, which is when a pale cushion helps. That is why I favour matte finishes over gloss and one dominant material over two. It also means the back and the sides of the cushion matter more than people assume. A cushion that sags at the rear edge, or a bracket plate that is out of level, is visible from both directions. Level it once, properly, and the whole arrangement reads as though it came with the window.

Keep the outside face plain. Patterned or branded backs read as clutter through glass, and from the street they are the only part you actually see.

What dates a window fastest

The details that age a window badly are consistent. Shiny chrome hardware on cream or ivory frames. Gloss white perch frames against warm-white walls, because the two whites fight each other in daylight. Novelty prints and character cushions in a room that is otherwise quiet. Raw pine going orange in Brisbane sun. Oversized suction cups left on display when a smaller plate would have done the job. What ages well is duller: one material, a matte finish, a tonal cushion and a frame colour that matches the joinery rather than the appliance. If you are fitting several windows, plan the cat window perches as a set rather than buying one at a time, because three mismatched finishes in one open room read as leftovers. Write the finish and the fabric down before you order anything, so the second and third windows match the first.

Frequently Asked Questions

Should the perch frame be the exact same colour as the window frame?

Same family, not necessarily the same shade. A matte black perch on a charcoal frame looks deliberate, while a gloss black perch on the same frame looks like a mismatch under daylight. If you cannot get close, go one step darker rather than one step lighter, because a lighter perch frame tends to glow against the glass at night.

Can I paint a perch frame to match?

You can, and on metal it needs proper prep to hold up. Scuff the surface lightly, use an etch primer, then build the colour in thin coats rather than one heavy pass. Let it cure for several days before the cat uses it, because uncured paint and claws are a bad combination. Refit the bracket afterwards and check the perch still feels solid.

Does the perch colour matter if a curtain covers the window most of the day?

It matters more than you would think, because curtains are usually drawn to one side and the perch sits in the brightest gap. A perch that clashes with the curtain fabric is visible in that gap all day. Choose the cushion against the curtain and the frame against the window, and the two will not compete.

What cushion colour hides cat hair best?

Mid-tones do the most work. Stone, oatmeal, warm grey and muted sage hide a mix of light and dark hair far better than cream or black, which are the two colours that look grubby fastest on cat furniture. A slightly textured weave also breaks up the look of hair better than a flat, tight surface.

Is it a problem if the perch and the blinds use different metals?

In a small window it can be, because the chain or cord sits close to the bracket and the two get compared. Keep the perch to the window fittings and let the blinds be the one element that differs. In a wide window with a generous gap between them, nobody will notice.
