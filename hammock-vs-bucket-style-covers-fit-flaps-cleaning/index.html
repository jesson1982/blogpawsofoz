Hammock vs Bucket-Style Covers: Fit, Flaps and Cleaning Compared

By Jess AllenSep 1, 2026Dog Car Seat Covers7 min read

Short answer: the hammock spans the whole back seat and stops a dog sliding into the footwell, so it wins for two dogs, mud and beach runs. The bucket style wraps a single seat like a cradle with higher walls, which suits one dog who wants a contained, cosy spot. Fit is where both styles fail when they are cheap, so measure your seat before you buy. Cleaning is much the same, a cold machine wash, except the bucket's walls hold more water and take longer to dry. Pick by the dog, not the photo on the box, and you will avoid the return queue.

How the two actually fit

A hammock clips to the front and rear headrests and spans the cabin, which is why it suits wide back seats and pairs of dogs. A bucket sits on one seat base and squab, with straps to that headrest, leaving the rest of the seat bare. The bucket suits a small car with one dog far better than a full hammock ever will, and it looks tidier doing it.

The fit complaints I hear most are too narrow for my ute on buckets and sags in the middle on cheap hammocks. Spend on the base and the anchors and both behave themselves, because the shape is sound, it is the build that lets water and claws through when the price drops too far to be real.

Worth knowing: a bucket that fits a sedan can be hopeless in a dual cab, because the rear squab is upright and shallow there. That single difference accounts for a lot of the returns I process, and it is why I always ask the car type before I suggest a style to a customer in the shop.

The flap difference

Hammocks usually come with side flaps that protect the door cards, which is the feature customers underestimate until claw marks appear. Buckets have built-up walls instead, which catch the shake-off but leave the door exposed unless the particular model adds a flap. If your dog shakes on entry, the door coverage is the detail that saves the trim.

  • Hammock: door flaps standard, full coverage front to back.
  • Bucket: high walls, the door trim often left bare.
  • Both: check the zip, because cheap zips die first.
  • Both: look for a seatbelt slot that lines up with your buckle.

The zip point is the quiet killer. A flap you cannot remove means you wash the whole hammock for one muddy edge, while a zip-off flap means a quick wash and the cover stays in. On the bucket the walls zip off less often, so factor the extra dry time into your routine before you buy the cradle style.

Cleaning them side by side

Both go in the machine on cold, but the bucket's walls hold far more water, so it takes longer to dry on the line. The hammock is a flatter load and dries quicker, which suits a dog who gets wet often, because you are not waiting two days for the cover to be usable again. For a weekly swimmer the dry time alone decides the style.

I tell customers to hose both before the machine, because sand in the seams wears the stitching. A quick driveway rinse takes a minute and adds months to the life of either style, and it stops the grit working at the thread every time the dog shifts, which is how the good covers quietly outlast the cheap ones.

Which dog suits which

Two dogs, a mess, or a ute, reach for the hammock. One small dog in a sedan who likes a walled bed, reach for the bucket. An anxious dog often prefers the bucket's contained feel, while a big bouncy dog is safer spanning the cabin where they cannot launch into the footwell on a corner, so match the style to the temperament as well as the car.

The cosy factor is real. A bucket reads as a den to a dog who likes edges, and some rescues settle faster in a walled seat than on an open hammock. If your dog is the curl-up type, the bucket's higher walls may do more for calm than any training, which is a point the spec sheet never mentions but the dog tells you daily.

For a puppy who has not learned the car yet, the bucket also limits the mischief, because a small dog in a walled seat cannot roam to the front footwell and wedge a paw somewhere silly. The hammock wins for mess, but the bucket wins for containment of a single young dog, and that trade is worth weighing before you buy the style the photo sold you rather than the one the dog needs.

What I check before selling

Width of the seat, depth of the base, anchor type, and whether the flaps zip off. Those four decide if it fits and lasts, and I measure the customer's car on my phone rather than trust the box. A cover that matches the seat shape is a cover that stays, and the return rate on measured sales is a fraction of the photo-bought ones.

Price and where the value sits

A decent hammock and a decent bucket land in much the same band, roughly forty to ninety dollars for a rigid base and sealed seams, so the price is not the decider between the two, the dog is. Spend the money on the base and the anchors either way, and pick the style by the car and the temperament rather than by which is on special this week at the chain.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which style for two dogs?

The hammock, every time. It spans the whole back seat and stops a dog sliding into the footwell on a corner, and the side flaps protect both door cards. A bucket only covers one seat, so the second dog ends up on bare trim. For a pair, mud, or a beach run, the hammock's coverage is the safer and cleaner choice by a clear margin.

Does the bucket suit an anxious dog?

Often yes. The higher walls read as a den, and some rescues settle faster in a contained seat than on an open hammock. If your dog is the curl-up type, the bucket's edges may do more for calm than training alone. Just note the door is often left bare, so add a towel if your dog shakes on entry and marks the trim.

Why does the bucket take longer to dry?

The built-up walls hold far more water than the flat hammock, so on the line it lags a day or two behind. For a dog who swims weekly that dry time alone can decide the style, because you do not want the cover unusable after every beach trip. Hose both before the machine to keep the seams from wearing.

What fit problems should I expect?

On buckets, too narrow for a ute because the rear squab is upright and shallow. On hammocks, middle sag if the base is soft. Both fail at the zip if it is cheap, and both need a seatbelt slot that lines up with your buckle. Measure the seat shape, not the photo, and the returns drop to almost none.

Is the hammock harder to fit?

Not really, it just clips to front and rear headrests and spans the cabin, which is fewer fiddly bits than a bucket's cradle straps once you know the trick. Thread the straps through the headrests rather than loop them so they do not slip, and a hammock that fits flat stays put through a whole summer of dog trips.
