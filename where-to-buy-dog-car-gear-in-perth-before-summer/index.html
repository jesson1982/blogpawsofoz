Where to Buy Dog Car Gear in Perth Before the Summer Holidays

By Maddie SorensenSep 1, 2026Dog Car Seat Covers6 min read

Short answer: for dog car gear in Perth I send people to the big chains for range and quick pickup, the independent pet stores for hands-on fitting help, and the weekend markets for a bargain cover if you know what to check. Buy before December, because the good covers sell out in the pre-holiday rush and you are left with the thin end of the shelf. The best buy is the one that fits your seat, which is why I like a shop where you can actually unfold it across the car, rather than guessing from a photo at midnight.

Big chains for range and speed

The national stores stock the known brands and usually have a hammock, a bench and a boot liner on the shelf. You can grab one on the way home, and the price points span cheap to mid. The downside is thin fitting advice, so measure your seat first or you may return it, because the aisle photo never matches your actual rear squab and door cards.

I tell clients to photograph the back seat on their phone, then match the shape in the aisle. It saves a second trip, and it lets the staff point at the right box instead of the pretty one, which is the difference between a cover that fits and a cover that joins the pile of returns after the first beach run of the holidays.

Independent stores for the fit

The smaller Perth shops earn their keep on help. The staff will often unfold a cover over a display seat and show you the anchors, which is worth more than a discount when you are unsure. For an odd car, a ute or a seven-seater, this is where I point people, because the fit is the thing that decides if the cover lasts the summer.

  • Ask to see the base, rigid or soft, in person.
  • Check the strap width, thin means weak.
  • Confirm the return window before summer trips.
  • Watch them fit it, because the demo shows the flaps.

A good independent will spend ten minutes on the fit, and that ten minutes is the warranty on the buy. The chains cannot, because the queue will not allow it, so for a first cover or a tricky car, the indie is the cheaper lesson even at a slightly higher ticket, since you avoid the photo-bought mistake.

Markets and online for bargains

Markets and online listings have cheap covers, and some are fine for a calm dog. The risk is unknown waterproofing and no warranty, so I only suggest them for light use. If the dog swims or sheds, spend at the chain or indie instead, because a bargain that wets through is no bargain by February when the seat smells.

I have bought market covers myself for the spare car, and the decent ones are the ones where the seller lets you feel the backing. If the backing is a thin shiny sheet that creases, walk away, because it will bead once and soak the second time, and the dog will teach you that lesson on the first river trip of the season.

What to check before you pay

Width, base depth, anchor type and whether the flaps zip off. Those four decide if it fits and lasts, and I fit covers for a living, so the returns I see are almost all measured the photo, not the car. Hold the cover up to your seat shape in your head, not the box, and the right one becomes obvious before you queue.

The zip-off flap is the detail people forget. A flap you cannot remove means washing the whole hammock for one muddy edge, while a zip-off flap means a quick wash and the cover stays in. For a dog who gets wet often, that one feature decides whether the cover stays in rotation or ends up in the cupboard by March.

Boot liners are the other pre-summer buy worth planning, and the same rules apply, measure the well, check the grippy underside, and avoid the single-strap version that rides up when the dog turns. I stock both a seat cover and a liner for customers with a hatch, because the dog rides up top and the wet gear goes in the well, and splitting the mess is what keeps the whole car clean through January.

Timing the pre-summer buy

Buy in November if you can. December the shelves thin, the good bases sell out, and you are choosing from the soft hammocks nobody wanted, which sag by February. The early buyer gets the rigid base, the wide straps, and the colour they actually liked, and that is the cover that survives the holidays rather than the one that barely makes it.

If it does not fit, return it fast

A cover that rides up the squab or misses the door is not a keeper, so use the return window before the holidays, not after. I tell buyers to fit it in the driveway the day they buy it, because a fault found in December is swappable, while one found in February is a write-off. Keep the box and the receipt, and the swap is a five-minute counter job rather than a bin job.

Frequently Asked Questions

Where should I buy in Perth?

Chains for range and quick pickup, independents for hands-on fitting help, markets for a bargain if you know what to check. For a first cover or a tricky car like a ute or seven-seater, the independent is worth the slightly higher ticket, because the ten-minute fit demo is the warranty on the buy and avoids the photo-bought mistake.

When should I buy before summer?

November if you can. By December the shelves thin, the good rigid bases sell out, and you are left with soft hammocks that sag by February. The early buyer gets the base, the wide straps and the colour they liked, which is the cover that survives the holidays rather than the one that barely makes it through one beach season.

Are market covers any good?

Some are fine for a calm dog on light use, but the risk is unknown waterproofing and no warranty. Feel the backing, and if it is a thin shiny sheet that creases, walk away, because it will soak through on the first river trip. For a swimmer or a shedder, spend at the chain or indie instead, where the backing is honest.

A zip-off flap means a quick wash instead of the whole hammock, which keeps the cover in rotation through a wet summer rather than the cupboard.

Can the chain fit it for me?

Usually not in detail, because the queue will not allow the ten-minute demo an independent gives. Photograph your back seat and match the shape in the aisle, and ask to see the base and straps. For an odd car, the indie that unfolds it over a display seat is the safer buy, even at a few dollars more.
