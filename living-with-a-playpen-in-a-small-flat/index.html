Living With a Playpen in a Small Flat

By Sophie BennettSep 5, 2026Pet Playpens8 min read

Short answer: a playpen can work well in a small flat, but only if you plan for where it lives, what it does to your floor, and the quiet weeks when nobody uses it. Ours takes up about a square metre of living space, sits on a foam mat, and earns its keep in bursts.

When Miso arrived as a foster-turned-forever rescue kitten, our Perth apartment already felt small for two people and a Cavoodle. The playpen went up in the living room on day one and never really came down. Eight months later it has been a kitten nursery, a post-grooming recovery pen, a quiet zone for a sick dog, and for one long fortnight a barrier between a kitten who thinks the bookshelf is a climbing wall and everything we own. Living with it day to day taught me more than any product page did.

Where the pen actually lives

We planned to put the pen in the neat corner near the balcony door, where it looked tidy on our floor plan. It lasted four days there. That spot caught the afternoon sun, Miso cooked in it, and whenever we cooked dinner we had our backs to her. The pen now lives between the sofa and the TV unit, ugly and central, where someone is always within sight and it gets used precisely because it is in the way enough to feel part of the room.

The lesson from our first week is that a pen in the wrong room is a pen nobody opens. Ours works because whoever is home can see the animal, reach in without leaving the couch, and hear it if something goes wrong. Convenience beats aesthetics every time in a small flat.

What it does to your floor

Renting means every mark on the floor threatens our bond, and the pen left two kinds of damage before we got smart. The panels scuffed the boards when Miso launched herself at them, and anything spilled inside sat on the timber because the tray bottom is thin. We now keep an interlocking foam mat under the whole footprint, cut to size, with a washable mat on top of that.

The foam does three jobs. It stops the scuffing, it catches accidents before they reach the boards, and it deadens the racket of a kitten bouncing off panels at 5 am. It cost less than a takeaway dinner and it is the single best thing we bought for this setup. The mats now have chewed edges, which is a fair trade.

The weeks it earns its space

A pen in a small flat has to justify its footprint, and ours does it in bursts. It earned its place during Miso's first fortnight, when she needed a safe space the Cavoodle could not crowd. It earned it again when Biscuit came home from a dental and needed to stay quiet, and again when a friend's toddler visited and we needed a guaranteed separation. Between those stretches it sits empty for weeks at a time.

I have stopped feeling guilty about the empty weeks. The alternative to having it ready is scrambling for a crate or the bathroom when something goes wrong, and we did that exactly once. The pen earns its floor space the way a fire extinguisher does: rarely, then completely.

  • Put the pen where someone is always nearby, even if it looks cluttered.
  • Use a foam mat underneath to protect rented floors from scuffs and spills.
  • Expect bursts of heavy use with quiet weeks in between; keep it set up.
  • A washable mat inside turns cleaning up accidents into a two-minute job.
  • Choose a pen you can step into or open wide, not one you reach over.

Living around it day to day

A square metre sounds small until it sits in your living room. We moved the coffee table closer to the wall, gave up one armchair, and accepted that vacuuming now involves lifting one side of the pen to get underneath. The worst part was the door: our first layout had it swinging outward into the walkway, where it caught my hip every morning until I rehinged the opening against the sofa.

What makes sharing the room bearable is keeping the pen furnished but sparse. A bed, a water bowl that clips to the panel, one toy. Loose clutter inside becomes missiles within minutes. Everything in ours is either clip-on or heavy enough that a bored kitten cannot relocate it, which stops the pen from leaking into the rest of the flat.

What we would choose differently

Knowing what we know now, we would have bought a pen with a door we can walk through rather than one we climb over, and we would have gone one panel size up. The extra half metre of floor matters less in practice than the times we have wedged a shoulder through a small door to refit a flipped water bowl. We would also have bought the foam mats on day one instead of after the first scuff.

The one thing we got right early was prioritising panel count over fancy features. Latches, roofs and clip-on trays matter less than having enough panels to shape the pen around furniture, which in a small flat is the only way it fits at all. That is the spec I tell anyone to check first when comparing playpens.

When a pen stops being useful

Miso outgrew the pen as a nursery within a couple of months and now treats it as an occasional snack station we happen to leave standing. Biscuit still uses it after grooms and vet visits. I have learned to watch for the moment a pen starts causing more friction than it prevents: an animal panicking at the door, latching onto the panels out of frustration rather than play, or us using it as a substitute for actually managing the flat.

When Biscuit needed rest after his dental, our vet's advice was to keep pen time short and positive and to ask about anything that looked like distress rather than boredom. A pen is a tool for specific seasons, and being honest about when the season ends is part of living with one well.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much floor space does a playpen really need in a small flat?

Ours takes up roughly one square metre as configured, plus walking room on the door side, and the foam mats extend a few centimetres past that on every edge. The honest answer is that you also lose the flexibility of that space for anything else while it stands. Measure the actual spot, including the door swing, before buying rather than guessing from the flat's total floor area.

Will a playpen damage rental floors or carpet?

It can, and ours did before we fixed the problem. Panel feet scuff timber when an animal throws its weight against the sides, and anything spilled inside sits against the surface. An interlocking foam mat under the whole footprint solved both for us and cut the noise too. On carpet, a washable mat on top handles the accident side of things instead.

Where should a playpen go in an open-plan apartment?

Somewhere central enough that whoever is home can see and reach the animal, even if it is not the prettiest spot. Ours ended up between the sofa and the TV unit after a failed attempt at a tidy corner that caught the afternoon sun. Avoid direct sun, draughty doorways, and anywhere the pen door swings across your main walking path.

Is it fair to leave a pet in a pen while I am at work all day?

That is a question for your vet, because the right answer depends on the animal, their age and their needs, and I would not guess at it. What I can say from our flat is that the pen was never our whole solution. Someone came in at lunch during the kitten's first weeks, and the pen covered short stretches of the day, not the whole one.

Do playpens work for cats or just for puppies?

Ours has done more work for a kitten than for our Cavoodle. The differences are that a cat needs a roofed or tall setup much sooner, climbs rather than pushes, and works out latches frighteningly fast. For the kitten we stacked panels for height and removed anything launchable from inside. For a calm dog, the standard open-top arrangement has been plenty.
