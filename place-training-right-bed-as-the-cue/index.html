Place training: how the right bed becomes the cue

By Daniel WhitlockSep 4, 2026Beds & Mats5 min read

Short answer: in place training the bed is not just furniture, it is the cue. When a dog learns that a specific mat or bed means 'settled here', you can send them to it instead of nagging them off the couch or out from under your feet. The right bed is distinct, portable and comfortable, so the dog chooses it willingly, and 'place' becomes a calm default you can use at the door, the barbecue or the caravan. The trainers who get the best results are the ones who stop thinking of the bed as decor and start treating it as a command post.

What place training actually is

Place is a stay with a location. You ask the dog to go to their bed and remain there until released, even with life happening around them. It is calmer than a sit stay because the dog gets to lie down and switch off, which is why it works so well when guests arrive or dinner is cooking. The dog is not frozen, they are settled, and that is a far easier state to hold for a busy household.

Picking the bed that becomes the cue

Choose one bed and use it only for place. It should look and feel different from the lounge cushions, be easy to move, and be comfy enough that lying there is its own reward. A raised cot or a clear mat works well because it reads as a spot, not a soft toy to mouth, and the dog learns the shape means the job rather than a nap they can take or leave.

  • One bed, one job: do not share it with nap time elsewhere.
  • Portable: carry it to the door, the park, the van.
  • Comfortable: the dog should want to lie, not perch.

Teaching the step in three stages

Stage one: lure the dog onto the bed with a treat, say 'place', reward the moment all paws are on. Stage two: ask from a step away, then across the room, rewarding on the bed. Stage three: add distance and distractions like the doorbell, building seconds into minutes before the release word. Keep the reward on the bed so the location, not your hand, becomes the reason to stay.

Using place around the home

At the door, place stops the dash out. At the barbecue, it keeps paws off the snags. In the caravan, it gives the dog a known spot in a strange space. Because the bed is the cue, anywhere you put the bed is a place, which is why portability matters more than plushness, and a bed that folds into a tote will get used far more than a pretty one bolted to the lounge.

Common mistakes I see

Owners release the dog the instant it gets up, which teaches place ends when they move. Hold the stay until your release word, every time. Others use a dozen beds for place, so the dog never learns which one is the cue. One bed, used consistently, beats a house full of look alikes, and a release you give too early is the most common reason place falls apart at the door.

If your dog already has a favourite couch cushion, do not fight it by banning the couch; send them to place first, then let them earn the couch later once the cue is solid. Trying to remove the comfy spot before the cue exists just builds resentment, whereas a reliable place makes the rest of the house calmer without a standoff over the lounge.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can any bed be a place bed?

Any bed can, but the best place bed is one the dog uses only for this. If it is also the couch cushion, the cue gets muddy. Pick a distinct, movable bed and keep it for place so the dog learns that specific spot means settle, and the command stays clean in their head instead of competing with every soft surface in the house.

How is place different from a stay?

A stay can be stand, sit or down anywhere; place is a down stay tied to a location. The bed is the anchor, so you can send the dog to it from across the room. That location link is why place is handier around the house than a generic stay on the spot they happen to stand, and it scales to real life far better.

My dog gets up the second I step away?

You moved too far too soon. Reset to a smaller gap and reward staying while you take one step, then two. Build the distance over sessions, not in one. If they pop up, calmly restart; the release word should only come when they are still, or they learn up equals free, and that one slip undoes a week of work.

Will place work for a excited greeter?

Yes, with practice at the door. Start with no real doorbell, just the word, then add a knock, then the actual door, rewarding calm on the bed each time. An excited greeter needs slower steps, but place is exactly the skill that takes the jump out of hello when guests arrive, and it keeps everyone's knees intact.

Can I use place in the caravan?

Absolutely, and it is one of my favourite uses. Set the same bed in the van and the dog has a known spot in a new place, which settles them fast. Because the bed is the cue, the familiar mat does the calming work, so travel becomes less chaotic for everyone inside the small space, and you stop tripping over a restless dog.
