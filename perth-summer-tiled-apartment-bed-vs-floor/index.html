Perth summers in a tiled apartment: bed vs the floor

By Maddie SorensenSep 4, 2026Beds & Mats5 min read

Short answer: in a Perth apartment with polished concrete or tiles, dogs often ditch the bed for the floor in summer because the floor is simply cooler, and no plush bed beats radiating tile on a forty degree day. The win is to give them a bed that works with that instinct: a raised cot for airflow, a cooling mat for relief, or a thin washable mat on the tiles so they get cool and clean at once. The floor is fine, the bed just needs to match the heat, and fighting a dog's wish to cool down is a battle you will lose every January afternoon.

Why the tiles win here

Perth summers run hot and our apartments hold that heat in the floor and the walls. A dog's natural radiator is the belly and the paws, so a cool tile pulls heat away better than any padded bed. I see it weekly in grooming: clients' dogs flop on the laundry tiles the moment they get home, not on the lovely bed in the lounge, and no amount of coaxing moves them until the sun drops.

When the floor is the right call

For a healthy young dog on a cool, clean tile, the floor is a fair choice and nothing to fight. The problems start when the floor is the only option: no support for an older dog, no defined spot in a shared apartment, and a bed that sits ignored and gathers fur. The aim is to meet the dog on the tiles, not ban them from them, because the cooling is doing real work on a hot body.

  • Tiles: free cooling, but hard and unsupported.
  • Raised cot: cool air underneath, off the hard floor.
  • Thin mat: cool feel with a washable layer on top.

Matching a bed to a Perth apartment

In a small rental I steer people to a raised cot they can hose and a thin mat they can wash. Both respect the dog's wish to be cool while giving a clean, defined spot. A giant plush bed just becomes a sun warmed lump by January, so size and breathability beat thickness in our climate, and the apartment thanks you for the floor space you did not fill with foam.

Renters and the lease

Polished floors and tiles are common in Perth rentals, and they scratch, so a non slip mat under any bed protects the floor at inspection time. I tell renters to choose beds that fold and covers that wash, because move out is smoother when the pet gear packs flat and leaves no marks on the boards, and a scratched floor is the kind of bond hit nobody budgets for.

My local verdict

Let the dog have the tiles in the worst of the heat, but give them a cot or mat so the cool comes with support and cleanliness. In a Perth summer the bed that wins is the one that admits the floor is cooler and works alongside it, not the fluffy throne that bakes in the afternoon sun and gets rejected by a dog with better sense than the marketing.

Frequently Asked Questions

Should I stop my dog lying on the tiles?

Generally no, it is smart cooling and harmless for a healthy dog. Step in only if the floor is the sole option and your dog is older or stiff, because then they lose support. Offer a cot or mat so they get the cool with a little cushion, and let the tiles do their job through the hot afternoons rather than turning it into a fight you will not win.

Will a cooling mat beat the floor?

A good cooling mat helps, but on a truly hot day a tile still wins on raw coolth. The mat's edge is that it gives a clean, defined, portable cool spot you can move to the shade, while the floor stays where it is. For many Perth dogs the mat plus tiles combo works better than either alone, and the mat keeps the fur off your dinner plates.

What bed survives a Perth rental best?

A raised cot and a thin washable mat. Both respect the heat, pack flat at lease end, and clean easily after beach and park trips. Skip the bulky plush bed as the main sleeper, because it bakes in summer and hogs space in a small apartment, exactly the wrong fit for our climate and our floor plans, so save the room.

How do I protect tiles from scratches?

Put a non slip mat or a bed with a grippy base under the dog's favourite spot, and keep nails trimmed at the groomer. Most tile marks come from scrambling or long claws, so the combo of a base liner and regular clips saves the floor and your bond at inspection time, and it costs far less than a repair claim at move out.

Is the floor too cold in Perth winter?

Our winters are mild but tiled floors still go cold at night, so the same dog that loved the tiles in February may want the foam bed in July. Keep both options available through the year, and watch which they pick, because the dog reads the temperature better than any forecast does, and will tell you when the season turns.

For apartment renters with balcony access, a cot outside on the shaded end is a winner on a hot evening, because the dog gets the breeze without tracking sand onto the good floor. Just bring it in before dew, and check the frame suits the surface, because a cot on a wobbly rail is a vet visit waiting to happen rather than a cooling win.
