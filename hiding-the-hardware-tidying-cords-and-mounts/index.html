Hiding the Hardware: Tidying Cords and Mounts

By Sofia MarchettiSep 2, 2026Cat Window Perches8 min read

Short answer: the perch itself is rarely the problem; it is the visible cord drooping to the power point, the bare mount bracket and the strap trailing down the glass. Route cords along the frame, wrap or box the bracket, and choose mounts that sit flush, and the whole window reads as deliberate rather than cluttered.

Last month I styled a Queenslander in Paddington where the cat hammock itself was lovely, a soft grey canvas sling, but the room never looked finished. The charger for the heated cushion looped off the sill and down the wall in a bright green curve, and the wall brackets stuck out like unpainted screw heads. The owners had done everything right at purchase and nothing at install. Ten minutes with a handful of matte cable clips, a short length of cord cover and a folded fabric tab later, the window looked like it had been planned. That gap between a good perch and a tidy one is what this post covers: where the mess actually comes from, and the choices I make so the hardware disappears.

Where the visual mess comes from

There are only three offenders, and every untidy perch window I have seen has at least one. The first is the cord: power leads for heated pads, dangling blind pulls, or the strap of a hanging bed swinging loose below the platform. The second is the mount itself, whether that is a suction cup ringed in dust or a bracket with two bright screws facing the room. The third is everything that accumulates around the base: spare suction cups on the sill, packaging behind the curtain, a towel shoved under the frame to stop a rattle.

Cats make it worse in specific ways. A hanging cord is a toy, so a loose lead will be batted, chewed and eventually dragged off the sill. Anything that swings invites climbing. The mess is not only visual; it changes how the cat uses the perch too.

Cord routes that disappear

My default route runs the lead along the underside of the sill or the vertical edge of the window reveal, then down behind the curtain hem where the wall is already shadowed. Adhesive cable clips in a matte finish do the holding; I place them every 30 cm or so so the lead never sags between points. On a painted timber frame I test the adhesive on an inconspicuous spot first, because some pulls lift paint.

If there is no curtain, a slim cord cover painted to match the wall works, and in rentals I have run leads inside the gap between the flyscreen frame and the reveal. Battery pads remove the route entirely, which is often the cleanest option of all. What matters is that the cord never crosses open glass, because against the sky it is the line every eye in the room follows.

Brackets, straps and suction rings

Exposed brackets are the easiest fix in the whole job. A bracket painted the same colour as the frame reads as part of the joinery, and a folded strip of canvas tied over a suction mount turns a shiny disc into a soft detail. Straps on hanging beds should be adjusted so the sling sits snug against the glass; a taut bed leaves no dangling tail to catch the light or the cat's attention.

Dust is the quiet enemy. A suction cup that was clear on install grows a grey halo within weeks, and that halo is what makes the mount look grubby rather than glossy. Wipe the cups and the glass whenever you clean the windows, press each cup back down firmly, and the mount stays nearly invisible for months.

  • Run cords along frame edges and behind curtains, never across open glass.
  • Use matte cable clips every 30 cm so leads never sag between fixing points.
  • Paint or wrap exposed brackets in the frame colour so they read as joinery.
  • Keep hanging straps taut so nothing dangles to swing or invite climbing.
  • Wipe suction cups when you clean the windows to stop the grey dust halo.

Choosing mounts with concealment in mind

When a client is still choosing, I steer them toward mounts that hide themselves. Wall brackets that screw flat to the reveal need no dressing at all once painted over. Sill clamps that fix down from the top leave the face of the frame clean. Suction models vary enormously: low-profile cups with a clear rim nearly vanish on glass, while chunky adjustable arms show every joint and hinge.

This is a shopping decision as much as a styling one, so it is worth looking at how each mount presents before you buy. Our cat window perches range includes several low-profile options that suit a styled window far better than scaffolding-style frames. Buy the discreet version even if it costs more, because no amount of tidying will hide a mount designed to be seen.

What you place beneath the perch

The window does not end at the sill, and the eye travels straight down from any visible hardware to whatever sits beneath it. A console table with a lamp under the perch gives the downward glance something intentional to land on. A bare wall under a bracketed hammock does the opposite; it isolates the bracket and makes it read as an error rather than a choice.

In tight rooms I use paint instead of furniture. Repainting the reveal and the surround in the same colour as the wall, rather than leaving glossy white joinery, melts the window into the room and shrinks every mount's visual weight. In rentals, a linen curtain panel hung wider than the frame achieves a softer version of the same effect without a drop of paint.

A ten-minute reset routine

Once the routes and covers are in, keeping the window tidy is maintenance rather than a project. I give clients a ten-minute reset: unclip the lead and wipe the dust off the clips, check each suction cup edge and re-press any that have lifted, brush the fabric tab or strap cover, and clear the sill of whatever the cat has parked there. Done monthly, the window never falls back into mess.

What I refuse to chase is perfect. A perch is for a cat, and a cat who kneads the canvas, drags toys onto the sling and sheds across the cushion is using it properly. The hardware should be hidden; the evidence of a well-loved perch should not be. That distinction is the difference between styled and sterile.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I hide a cord when I rent and there is no curtain?

Run the lead inside the gap between the flyscreen frame and the window reveal, or along the shadowed top edge of the sill, using matte adhesive clips. Test any adhesive on a hidden patch first, and keep the backing tabs so everything peels off cleanly at the end of the lease.

Are adhesive cable clips safe on painted timber frames?

Most are, but some adhesives lift paint when pulled, especially on older weatherboard. Test on an inconspicuous edge first, warm the clip with your fingers before peeling, and pull the tab slowly along the grain rather than straight out. If the paint is already flaking, use a cord cover screwed into the reveal instead and touch up later.

Should I cover a suction mount or leave it clear?

Clear mounts on clear glass nearly disappear, so I usually leave them and keep them dust-free. Cover them when the glass is tinted, when the cup has yellowed, or when the mount sits against a painted frame where a fabric tab looks more deliberate than plastic. Match the covering to the room, not to the product photo.

What do I do about a cat chewing the hidden cord?

Chewing usually means the route is not hidden enough or the cord is still reachable, so re-route it deeper behind the fabric or inside a cord cover. If the chewing seems compulsive rather than playful, mention it to your vet. Never rely on bitter sprays alone; they do nothing about the dangling line that started the game.

How often should the mounts be checked and cleaned?

Line it up with your window cleaning, so monthly in most Brisbane homes and more often through the humid summer when dust sticks. Press every suction cup back down firmly after wiping, since most failures begin at an edge that lifted weeks earlier. A ten-second press each month saves you a fallen perch and a startled cat.
