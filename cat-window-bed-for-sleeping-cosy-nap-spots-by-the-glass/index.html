Cat Window Bed for Sleeping: Cosy Nap Spots by the Glass

By Sophie BennettJul 18, 2026Cat Window Perches6 min read

Short answer: a sleeping window bed earns its place when you pair the natural warmth of the glass with a soft, snug surface Miso returns to every day. In my Perth flat her favourite spot became the sill once I added a cushioned suction perch, and she now naps there through the winter instead of balancing on cold tiles. You should keep the spot low-traffic and away from Wally's boisterous play so the sleep stays undisturbed.

There is a particular kind of quiet joy in watching a kitten fall asleep in a patch of sunlight. Since Miso, my rescue kitten, moved into my Perth flat, the window has become her favourite place to doze. I wanted to give her a proper cat window bed for sleeping rather than letting her balance on the sill, so I set up a suction-cup perch and watched what happened. What I learned is that the glass itself, the warmth, and a soft surface together create a nap spot she returns to every single day.

I should say up front: I am not a vet and this is not clinical advice. I am a first-time pet parent and a renter who likes practical, budget-friendly solutions. Everything below is from living with Miso and a Cavoodle puppy called Wally, not from any medical training. If your cat has health concerns, please chat with your own vet.

Why cats love sleeping by the window

Cats are sun-seekers by nature. A warm window ledge mimics the safe, elevated, sunny perch they would choose in the wild, and it lets them keep an eye on birds, passers-by, and the occasional neighbouring cat. For Miso, the window is part observatory and part heated mattress. She will sleep almost anywhere, but she always circles back to the glass when the sun is out.

In my flat the lounge room window catches the morning light, and that is where the perch lives. Within days she had worked out that the sun moves, and she now follows it from one end of the sill to the other. A cosy cat window perch just makes that instinct comfortable instead of awkward.

The warmth of glass in winter

People think of windows as cold, and in the evenings they can be, but on a sunny winter day the glass warms up beautifully. Miso presses her whole side against it like a little radiator, and on crisp Perth mornings that gentle heat seems to lull her straight to sleep. A padded bed on the perch lifts her off the chill and holds a bit of her own warmth, so she stays curled and sleepy instead of wandering off to find a better spot.

I swap the summer mesh for a plusher cover once the evenings turn cold. It takes a minute and means her cat window bed for sleeping stays inviting through the whole winter without me buying a second frame.

Making the spot feel safe and snug

Sleep is about feeling safe as much as feeling warm. The perch sits up off the floor, away from Wally's enthusiastic paws, and the suction cups hold it steady so there is no wobble to startle her awake. I keep the area calm — no loud appliances near the window, and the blinds partially open so she gets light without a draft. A small routine like that turns a simple perch into a proper sleep zone.

Miso also seems to like the slight give of the fabric. The soft bed cradles her, and I have caught her kneading it sleepily before she tucks her paws under her chin. Those little behaviours tell me the spot is doing its job.

Where to put a sleeping perch

Placement matters more than people expect. I avoided the window above the heater, because warm air there dries the air and can get too stuffy. Instead I picked a window with steady, gentle light and a view she finds interesting. If your cat is nervous, a quieter window away from the front door works better than a busy one.

For renters, the no-drill mount is the clincher. I can shift the perch if I rearrange the room, and when my lease ends I take it down without leaving a mark. That flexibility is part of why a window perch fits a rental life so well.

Sizing and an honest note on our hammock

A quick word on fit, because I would rather be straight with you than oversell. Our standard twin-suction hammock is sourced through 1688 and is suited to small and medium cats up to about 15 kg. Miso fits it perfectly and uses it daily. I will not claim it safely holds a heavy large breed, because that would not be true. If your cat is 15 kg or more, please look at a reinforced four-suction or steel-cable model built for that weight. Safety comes first, always.

My verdict after a winter of naps

The cat window bed for sleeping has been one of the easiest wins in the flat. Miso sleeps longer and more deeply on it than she does on the couch, and I get the quiet pleasure of watching her breathe slowly in the sunlight. For a renter on a budget, it is hard to beat a no-drill perch that turns an ordinary window into a daily comfort.

If you are thinking about it, start with where your cat already likes to sit. Chances are there is a sunny patch of glass calling their name, and a soft perch there will quickly become their favourite place to dream.

Frequently asked questions

Where is the best window for a cat sleeping perch?

Pick a window with steady, gentle light and a view your cat enjoys, away from drafts and busy doors. In my flat the lounge room window works best because Miso can watch the street without being startled.

Is a window bed warm enough in winter?

On a sunny day the glass warms up and a padded cover lifts your cat off the chill, holding their body heat. I swap to a plush cover in winter so Miso stays curled and sleepy instead of wandering off.

Will the suction cups hold while my cat sleeps?

Yes, on a clean, flat pane with firm cups. I re-press them every few weeks. The steady hold means no wobble to wake a dozing cat, which matters for a proper nap spot.

Does your standard hammock suit large cats for sleeping?

Our 1688-sourced twin-suction hammock suits small and medium cats up to about 15 kg. For cats over 15 kg we recommend a reinforced four-suction or steel-cable model rather than our standard bed.

How do I stop my puppy disturbing the sleeping cat?

Mount the perch up off the floor and keep the area calm, away from boisterous play. Wally the Cavoodle has learned the window is Miso's quiet zone, and the height keeps her naps undisturbed.

Can renters install a window bed without drilling?

Yes. A suction-cup perch needs no drill or permanent fixings and removes cleanly at the end of a lease, which is exactly why it suits a rental flat like mine.
