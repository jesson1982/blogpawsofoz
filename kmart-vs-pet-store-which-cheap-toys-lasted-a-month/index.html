Kmart vs Pet Store: Which Cheap Toys Lasted a Month in Our House

By Jess AllenSep 5, 2026Cat & Dog Toys5 min read

The bottom line: cheap Kmart toys won on price and lost on lifespan for keen players, while pet store toys at two to three times the cost often outlasted them by weeks. For a gentle cat or a puppy, the budget option is fair value; for a determined chewer, the pet store toy paid for itself. I bought matched pairs and ran them a month in a real household, not a showroom, because that is what owners actually ask me at the counter.

How I set up the test

I matched toy types, a plush, a rope, a rubber chew and a cat wand, from both shops and gave each to a real pet for a month. My store sees the returns, so I wanted honest wear data, not a brochure claim. Same play time, same supervision, then I scored what survived.

Margins matter here: the pet store toy costs more because the maker warrants it, the cheap one is built to a price.

What the Kmart toys did

The plush lasted four days with a chewer before the seam went. The rope frayed by week two but was still usable. The rubber bone held up surprisingly well, a genuine win at the price. The cat wand's ribbon detached by week three, which is a swallowing risk I flag to customers.

  • Plush: four days for a chewer, fine for a bat-and-release cat.
  • Rope: frayed week two, still safe if trimmed.
  • Rubber bone: best budget surprise, a month plus.
  • Cat wand: ribbon failed, retire it once loose.

What the pet store toys did

The branded plush took a month of chewing with only a bald patch. The rope was thicker and untouched at four weeks. The rubber chew showed tooth marks but no splits, and the wand's attachment was stitched, not glued. You pay more, but the warranty and the wear told the story.

For a rescue kitten like Miso, the cheap wand is fine if I replace the ribbon. For Wally the Cavoodle, the pet store rubber toy is the better spend.

Where the money actually lands

Buy three cheap plushes that last a week each and you spend more than one good one. But for toys a pet grows out of fast, like puppy teething gear, cheap is smart. Match the tier to how long the pet will use it, not to a brand loyalty.

Supervision changed my read on price. The cheap plush that failed under a chewer was fine in my hands for a photo, but real play is not a photo. I now test toys the way owners use them, on the floor, with a keen pet, because that is the only honest lab we have.

One more note for puppy owners: the cheap teething toy is the smart buy, not the premium one. A puppy chews through stages fast, so spending big on a toy they outgrow in a fortnight wastes money. Save the budget for the keeper chew they will use for years once adult teeth settle.

I also watch the returns desk. The toys that come back under warranty are the ones I trust to last, and the ones that never return are usually the ones nobody complains about because they simply disappear into a happy home. The warranty claim is the quiet proof of durability.

For a first pet, I suggest one cheap explorer set and one good chew, then let the pet tell you which tier to fill in. The shop can wait, the pet decides.

For the record, I am not against cheap toys, I sell plenty of them. The point is matching the tier to the pet and the timeframe, not to the price tag. A three dollar plush in the right home is better value than a forty dollar one the dog ignores, and that is the whole of the lesson.

Frequently Asked Questions

Are Kmart pet toys worth it?

For gentle players and short term needs, yes. A kitten or a bat-and-release cat gets weeks of fun from a few dollars. For a power chewer, the seams and ribbons fail fast, so the pet store tier is the better value over a month of real use.

Why do pet store toys cost more?

They use thicker stitching, rated rubber and often a warranty the maker stands behind. That costs the supplier more, and the shop passes it on. You are paying for lifespan and a returns path, not just a label.

Which cheap toy surprised you?

The budget rubber bone. It took a month of Cavoodle chewing with only surface wear, which beats plenty of pricier toys I have seen returned. Solid rubber with no squeaker unit is the sweet spot in the cheap aisle.

When is cheap the smart buy?

Puppy teething, a toy a pet will outgrow in weeks, or a gentle cat. Spend up on the things a pet uses daily for months, like a good chew or a wand, and save on the passing fancies. Match the tier to the use.

Do you take returns on worn toys?

We take faulty toys under warranty, not normal wear, and that is standard across the trade. Keep the receipt and check the seam in the first week. If a part detaches early, that is a warranty job, not 'the dog destroyed it'.
