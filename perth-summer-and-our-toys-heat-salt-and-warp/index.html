Perth Summer and Our Toys: Heat, Salt Water and What Warps Outside

By Maddie SorensenSep 5, 2026Cat & Dog Toys5 min read

Direct answer: Perth summers are brutal on toys left outside. Rubber goes brittle and warps in the forty degree heat, rope rots faster once it meets salt water and stays damp, and fabric fades and grows musty if it never dries. The toys that survived our backyard were solid rubber, closed cell foam and anything I brought in each night. I learned this the hard way one February when a favourite chew came inside cracked like old plastic.

What the heat actually does

Left on a brick patio, a rubber bone reaches a temperature that softens it, then it re sets out of shape by morning. Plastic squeakers cloud and crack. Even a wand left in the sun loses its flex. The rule now is simple: if it lives outside, it must handle the heat or it comes in.

WA summers are long, so a toy's outdoor life is measured in weeks, not seasons, unless it is built for it.

Salt water and the beach run

After a swim at the dog beach, rope toys hold salt and stay damp in the heat, which rots the fibres within days. Rubber rinses clean and lasts. Wally's beach rope now gets a fresh water soak and a full dry before it goes near the toy box, or it retires.

  • Solid rubber: rinses clean, survives salt and sun.
  • Rope: rots if salt damp, wash and dry hard.
  • Plush: mildews outside, indoor only.
  • Plastic: cracks and clouds in the heat.

What we keep outdoors now

One rubber cone and a closed cell fetch buoy live by the back door for the yard, both hose off and dry by evening. Everything else, wands, plush, rope, comes inside. The cat tunnel stays in the shade house, never in full sun, or the frame warps.

It cost me a few ruined toys to learn, but the swap saved the good gear through the worst of February.

Storing toys through the hot months

A lidded tub in the laundry keeps the rotation dry and cool. I wash fabric toys before storing so salt and sand do not sit in them. A monthly sort catches the ones the heat has quietly aged before they become a safety issue.

One habit that saved our gear was a Friday toy check. Anything warped, cracked or salty got pulled before the weekend heat peaked, so a failed toy never sat in the sun worsening. Ten minutes with a cold drink and a quick sort kept the box safe through February.

Owners near the coast ask me about rinse off toys most. My rule is simple, if it went in the sea it gets fresh water before it goes near the toy box, full stop. Salt left in rope or plush is what ends a toy, not the play itself, and a hose fixes it in seconds.

Cats suffer the heat too, even if they stay indoors. A wand left by a sunny window goes floppy, and a fabric mouse left on a hot tile fades and smells. Keep the small toys in the cool part of the house and they last the season without a warping lesson.

If a toy does crack, bin it rather than risk a swallowed flake. The few dollars saved by keeping a failed toy is never worth a vet trip in the middle of summer, when clinics are flat out with snake and heat cases.

When in doubt, bring it in. The ten seconds to grab a toy off the patio at the end of the day is the difference between a toy that lasts a summer and one that cracks by January. Perth heat is unforgiving, but a lidded tub and a habit beat it every time.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can dog toys be left outside in Perth summer?

Only heat stable ones like solid rubber and closed cell foam. Bring plush, rope and plastic inside, because the heat warps or cracks them and salt speeds rot. A lidded, cool storage tub keeps the rotation safe through the hot months.

Why does my rope toy go smelly after the beach?

Salt water soaks in and, in the heat, the damp fibres rot and smell within days. Rinse it in fresh water, squeeze it out and dry it fully before it goes near the toy box. If it stays musty, retire it, a rotten rope is a swallowing risk.

Which toy material survives salt water?

Solid rubber is the clear winner, it rinses clean and does not hold salt. Closed cell foam floats and dries fast. Avoid rope and plush at the beach, they hold salt and never truly dry in a Perth summer.

How do I stop rubber toys warping?

Keep them out of direct sun and off hot surfaces like brick or metal. A shaded hook or the laundry is enough. Once rubber has re set out of shape it stays that way, so prevention is the only fix, bring them in.

Should cat toys go outside at all?

Most cat toys are indoor by nature, and the heat fades fabric and warps wands fast. A cat tunnel in a shaded, breezy spot is fine, full sun is not. Keep the small toys inside where they stay clean, cool and safe to chew.
