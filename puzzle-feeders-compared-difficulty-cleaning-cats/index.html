Puzzle Feeders Compared: Difficulty, Cleaning and Whether Cats Bother

By Jess AllenSep 5, 2026Cat & Dog Toys5 min read

Plain answer: puzzle feeders range from a shallow snuffle mat a cat will use in seconds to a screw top globe that defeats everyone, and cleaning difficulty tracks closely with how fiddly the toy is. Cats will bother with the easy and medium levels but quit on anything that makes them work too long for too little food. I sold and tested the common models, and the best all rounder was a simple silicone wobble dish that washes in the sink and keeps a cat interested.

How I rated the feeders

I scored each on three things owners actually care about: how long a pet stays engaged, how many parts need washing, and whether a cat returns to it voluntarily. A toy that takes twenty minutes to clean loses the daily use battle even if it is clever. I watched Miso the kitten and a neighbour's spaniel across a fortnight.

Listings love to claim 'for all pets', but the cat versus dog gap is real and worth knowing before you buy.

Difficulty and who sticks with it

Easy level mats and wobble dishes had both species returning. Medium screw or slider feeders kept the dog going but lost Miso after a few tries. Hard globe feeders frustrated everyone and collected dust. The lesson: start easy and only level up if the pet asks for it.

  • Snuffle mat: easy, cats and dogs both engage.
  • Wobble dish: easy to medium, daily use winner.
  • Slider feeder: medium, dogs persist, cats often quit.
  • Screw globe: hard, mostly ignored by cats.

Cleaning, the part nobody mentions

Silicone dishes rinse in seconds and go through the top shelf of the dishwasher. Plastic screw feeders trap wet food in threads and need a brush. Fabric mats need a machine wash and take a day to dry, so you need two in rotation. Ease of cleaning decided real world use more than clever design.

I tell customers to buy the feeder they will actually wash, because a grubby one stops getting used by week three.

Do cats actually bother?

Yes, at the right level. Miso works a snuffle mat nightly and ignores the globe she 'failed' at. The cats that quit are usually handed a puzzle two steps too hard, which reads as pointless. Drop the difficulty and most come back.

For more ways to slow a fast eater, our toy guides cover lick mats and slow bowls too.

One detail listings miss is portion size. A puzzle stuffed with a full dinner frustrates a cat who expects speed; a light scatter keeps her coming back. I start new cats with a few biscuits on top, not buried, so the win comes fast and the habit forms before the challenge rises.

Cleaning also decides safety in our climate. A wet feeder left in a Perth kitchen overnight grows something unpleasing by morning, so I rinse after every use and run silicone through the dishwasher. Fabric mats get a weekly wash and a full dry day, never back on the floor damp.

For dogs, a puzzle at dinner time doubles as calm training. A spaniel who bolts food learns to slow down, and the feeder quietly does the work a trainer would charge for. The trick is consistency, the same feeder at the same meal, so the dog learns the new pace.

If a pet gives up, drop a level rather than push. The point is engagement, not a test they fail. A feeder they ignore helps no one, but one they master builds confidence for the next step up.

If you only buy one, buy the silicone wobble dish. It sits in the easy and medium band, washes in seconds, and both cats and dogs use it without a fight. The clever screw globe can wait until you know your pet loves the game, because an ignored puzzle is the most expensive toy in the drawer.

Frequently Asked Questions

Will my cat use a puzzle feeder?

Most will, if it starts easy. A snuffle mat or shallow wobble dish suits a cat's patience. Hard screw or slider feeders lose them fast. Begin simple and only level up if your cat clearly wants more challenge, not because the box says advanced.

Which puzzle feeder is easiest to clean?

A one piece silicone wobble dish wins. It rinses in seconds and goes on the top shelf of the dishwasher. Avoid threaded plastic screw feeders if you hate scrubbing, they trap wet food. Fabric mats need a wash and a dry day, so keep two.

My dog inhales dinner, will this help?

A puzzle or slow bowl slows most dogs noticeably by making them work for each mouthful. Start with an easy wobble dish, then try a slider if he masters it. It is not a cure for speed, but it turns a ten second meal into a few calm minutes.

Are puzzle feeders safe to leave out?

Empty ones are fine, but do not leave wet food sitting in a feeder for hours, especially in an Australian summer. Wash after use and store dry. Check for cracks where food and bacteria hide, and retire a feeder that splits.

How do I start a cat on puzzles?

Sprinkle a few favourite biscuits on a snuffle mat, no rules, just exploration. Once she hunts there daily, move to a shallow wobble dish. Keep it short and rewarding, and never starve a cat into using one, that causes stress, not fun.
