A Vet Nurse's Hammock Safety Checklist: Falls, Frays and Fits

By Tessa NgataSep 2, 2026Cat Window Perches5 min read

Most of the hammock near-misses I hear about as a vet nurse come down to three things: suction that quietly let go, cords or straps that frayed unnoticed, and platforms loaded well beyond their rating. None of these are exotic failures. All of them are findable in a two-minute check. This is the checklist I'd run before a cat's first session on any window hammock, and the shorter weekly version I'd keep up afterwards.

What actually goes wrong

The classic failure is a partial release. One cup peels, the platform tilts, and the cat slides rather than falls; startling, but usually harmless. The nastier version is a full release from height onto tiles or a hard floor, or a strap letting go while the cat is mid-pounce onto the perch, which turns it into a fall from momentum as well as height.

Cords and straps bring their own risk for cats who chew or who hook a claw while scrambling. And then there's placement: a hammock above a hard floor, beside a heater, or opposite a sliding door the family forgets to close gently. Gear rarely fails on its own; it fails in context.

The checklist before first use

Run through all of this before your cat's first proper session:

  • Glass is clean, dry, smooth and out of direct sun when you press the cups on
  • Each cup is pressed firmly from the centre out, with no trapped air or dust under the rim
  • The platform is hand-tested with firm downward pressure and a sideways shove before any cat gets on
  • Your cat's weight, plus landing force, sits comfortably under the stated rating
  • Straps, cords and stitching have no frays, cuts or soft spots
  • The drop below is forgiving: rug, carpet or sofa, not tiles or floorboards over concrete
  • No heaters, power cords or blind cords within reach of the platform

If any line fails, fix it before the first paw goes up. Cats remember falls, and a bad first experience can end the perch project entirely.

Cords and the cat who chews

Chewing is where hammocks get genuinely dangerous, because a fraying cord is both a strangulation and a swallowing risk. If your cat chews straps, don't wait for her to grow out of it: pick a design with rigid frames and short fabric loops instead of hanging cords, or use a hammock that clips to brackets rather than tying. For claw-catchers, check that no loop is wide enough for a paw and leg to slip through, and trim snags immediately rather than leaving a strand for her to worry at.

Heat, cleaning and the cups that let go

Australian summers are hard on suction. Cups that behave in April can fail during a February heatwave, because glass expands, condensation cycles and the plastic hardens with UV exposure. Re-press every cup weekly, and after any day over 35°C, hand-test before your cat's first session. The same applies after you've washed the platform or cleaned the window: cups need a clean, dry, cool pane to grip properly, and a damp or warm pane is a quiet failure waiting for a nap to end.

If a fall happens anyway

Cats are famously good at landing, but a fall from a tilted platform can still sprain a limb, chip a tooth or, in older cats, cause real injury. After any fall, let her settle, then watch for limping, reluctance to jump, hiding, or tenderness when you touch her along the spine and legs. Anything beyond a brief startle deserves a vet visit the same day, and that assessment is one for your vet rather than a checklist. Meanwhile, work out why it failed: nearly every fall I hear about traces back to a check on the list above that had lapsed. The perch itself is usually fine. The maintenance was the missing piece.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much weight can a cat window hammock really hold?

Treat the stated rating as a static maximum, not a licence. A cat landing on the platform hits it with far more force than her standing weight, and two cats taking turns doubles the wear. I'd want the rating comfortably above your heaviest cat, and I'd ignore ratings on cups that show any sign of lifting.

Where should a hammock be placed to make falls safer?

Lower than you think, ideally under a metre, and above something forgiving like carpet, a rug or the end of a sofa. Avoid tiles, floorboards over concrete, and anywhere beside stairs. Also keep it clear of heaters, dangling blind cords and sliding doors that get slammed, which cause their own share of incidents.

How do I stop the suction cups from failing?

Clean glass, dry glass, cool glass. Wipe the pane, press each cup firmly from the centre outward, and re-press weekly. Avoid mounting in full afternoon sun, and hand-test after heatwaves or cleaning. If cups keep releasing on glass that others grip fine, the cups have hardened and it's time for replacements.

Are hammocks with hanging cords dangerous for cats?

They can be, mainly for cats who chew or who hook claws while scrambling aboard. Choose designs with short loops, rigid frames or clip-on straps, and inspect the cords weekly for frays. Any strand a cat can worry at should be trimmed or the strap replaced, because fraying accelerates quickly once it starts.

My cat fell off the perch but seems fine. Should I worry?

Watch her closely for a day or two: limping, reluctance to jump, hiding, reduced appetite or tenderness along her back and legs all warrant a vet visit, even if she seemed okay immediately after. Cats mask discomfort well. Then re-run the full checklist, because something on it, usually the cups or a strap, will explain the fall.
