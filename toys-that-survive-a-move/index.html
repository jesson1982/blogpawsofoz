Toys That Survive a Move: Packing and Restarting Play

By Ellie NguyenSep 5, 2026Cat & Dog Toys8 min read

Short answer: pack soft toys flat in a lidded tub, bin anything split or rattling, and keep one familiar wand and one crinkle mat in a separate first-night bag. Cats restart play faster when a few known toys arrive smelling of the old flat than when a whole box of new ones lands in an unfamiliar room.

Two moves in four years, both inside Melbourne, both one-bedroom flats with one car space and a landlord who likes the place to look unlived-in by inspection day. The second move taught me the lesson. I packed the cats' toys last, in a rush, into a bag that ended up at the bottom of a stack of kitchen boxes. Nori and Bette arrived at the new flat, found nothing that smelled like them, and spent two days under the bed. The toys were fine. The order I unpacked them in was not. I have packed for two friends since, and neither of them made that mistake twice.

Which toys earn a place in the box

Before a single box is taped, everything comes out of the basket by the couch and gets sorted into three piles on the floor: coming, undecided, gone. It takes twenty minutes.

The sorting rule is shape, not sentiment. Anything that folds, coils or stacks flat travels well. Tunnels crush down to a disc, crinkle mats fold into thirds, and a 60 cm wand coils into a circle about the size of a dinner plate. Hard plastic balls are the opposite: they roll out of open boxes, end up under a car seat, and arrive scratched.

I also count what is in use. Usually it is four or five toys out of a basket of twenty, and the rest live under the couch. I keep a list of the ones I would buy again from the cat toys range, because that list is the packing list.

Packing flat without wrecking the good stuff

The container matters more than the technique. A 40 litre lidded tub with a towel in the bottom holds every soft toy in a one-bedroom flat and still lifts with one hand. That is the test: if I cannot carry it up a stairwell, it is packed wrong.

Coil wands at about 30 cm across and hold the coil with masking tape. Do not fold a wand in half to fit a gap. The wire takes a crease and the toy never casts properly again, which is a small tragedy at 9pm when you want ten minutes of play before bed.

Flatten tunnels and stand them against the side of the tub. Roll crinkle mats rather than folding on the same crease. Never use a garbage bag, even for the ten metre walk to the car; a bag that rustles is a bag a cat will climb into.

Label the tub on the side, not the lid. In a stack of eleven boxes, the sides are what you can read.

The bin list and the box you leave undecided

Some things should not make the trip. Split seams with stuffing showing, a bell loose inside a seam, elastic gone thin and shiny on a wand, foam that crumbles when you press it, and any toy missing a piece after ten minutes of looking.

Everything else goes into the undecided box, taped shut and dated. The rule is a fortnight: if nobody has asked for anything in it two weeks after the move, it goes out, unopened. I have opened one such box in four years and kept two things.

A cat's attachment is usually to a smell and a routine rather than to a particular object. The crinkle mat that has been slept on for a year is worth more in week one than anything new.

Build a first-night bag

The bag is separate from the tub and it travels in the car with the cats, not in the truck. Mine holds a wand, the oldest crinkle mat in the flat, one food puzzle, and two soft balls both cats have carried around. It weighs almost nothing.

The first evening in a new flat is loud in a way that is hard to explain. Doors, footsteps in the stairwell, a fridge that hums at a different pitch. Four familiar objects give the cats something to rub against and something to recognise.

Put the bag in the room you are starting them in, usually the smallest quiet room with a door that closes. Unpack with the door shut so nothing rolls away.

  • Fold tunnels and mats flat; they travel better than anything with a bell sewn in.
  • Coil wands at about 30 cm and tape them, because a sharp fold kinks the wire.
  • Tape the undecided box shut, date it, and open it a fortnight after the move.
  • Keep one first-night bag with the two toys your cats actually chase.
  • Label the toy tub on the side, so you can read it inside a stack of boxes.

Reading a new floor plan for play

Every flat has a different play geography and it takes about a week to learn it. A wand needs roughly two metres of clear floor and a little height at the end, so a hallway beats a lounge room with a coffee table in the middle. Carpet slows a ball and stops it early; floorboards send it into the skirting board and then under the fridge.

Noise is the other half. With a neighbour below, a hard ball at 10pm is a complaint waiting to happen. I keep the loud toys for daylight and switch to a wand or a mat after dark, which suits the cats because they are flatter in the evening.

Then there is the gap under the new furniture. Measure it before you unpack. Anything that fits will be under there by the end of week one.

Restarting play in stages

The temptation is to empty the tub on day one and hope. That usually produces a lot of sniffing, no play, and a cat walking away.

Better: three minutes with a wand on the floor, twice on day one, sitting down rather than standing over them. Week one is short sessions in the same room at the same two times. Week two, open the tub and put out three toys, not twenty. Whatever gets used stays out, and whatever has not moved in three days goes back in.

Most cats come back to play within a fortnight, once the flat stops being strange. If a cat is still hiding and off food after the first day or two, ask your vet. The toys can wait.

Frequently Asked Questions

Should I wash cat toys before packing them?

Spot clean only, and only the ones that need it. A machine wash strips most of the smell a cat uses to recognise a toy, and heat can shrink a crinkle mat or soften a glued seam. Wipe hard plastic with a damp cloth, hand wash fabric in cool water, and let everything dry before it goes in the tub.

Why did my cat ignore her toys after the move?

Because the flat is new and the toys are the least interesting thing in it. Cats spend the first days mapping a space, and a toy waved at a cat still working out where the food bowl lives will be ignored. Keep sessions short, sit on the floor, and start with the toys that smell of the old place.

Is it worth buying new toys for a new flat?

Not in the first fortnight. New toys compete with an entirely new environment and usually lose. Once the cats are moving around normally, one new toy at a time is plenty, and it tells you far more about what they like. Save the rest for a wet weekend.

How do I stop balls rolling under the new couch?

Measure the gap first, then block it. A rolled towel or a length of foam pipe insulation pushed into the gap does the job without drilling or marking the floor, which matters in a rental. Larger balls roll less predictably and stop sooner. If the gap is under about 4 cm, most toys will not fit.

Can I vacuum-pack a cat tunnel?

I would not. Tunnels hold their shape with a wire coil or a foam core, and a vacuum bag presses that into creases that never come out, so it arrives lopsided and the cat refuses it. Roll it, strap it with a velcro tie, and stand it against the side of a tub.
