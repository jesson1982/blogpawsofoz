Cat Window Bed Materials Explained (ABS, Oxford, Mesh)

By Sophie BennettJul 17, 2026Cat Window Perches6 min read

Sophie & the Fur Babies — suction-cup cat window hammocks for renters & sun-loving cats

Before Miso arrived I thought a cat window bed was just a bit of fabric on a frame. Then I started comparing listings and realised the materials decide everything: how cool the bed stays in a Perth heatwave, how easy it is to wash, and whether it survives a kitten who thinks the mesh is a scratching post. So I went through the common options one by one. If you are choosing your first bed, or upgrading from a tired old one, this is the plain-language version of what each material actually does.

ABS: the frame that does the quiet work

ABS is a tough plastic used in everything from luggage to power tools. In a window bed it forms the frame that holds the shape and connects to the suction cups. The reason I like ABS is that it is rigid enough to stop the bed sagging under a cat, yet light enough that the suction cups are not fighting extra weight. It also copes with temperature changes better than brittle recycled plastic, which can crack near a cold winter window.

Our 1688-sourced twin-suction hammock uses an ABS frame, and that stiffness is why Miso can stretch out without the whole bed folding under him. When you compare beds, check the frame material in the listing. A vague "plastic frame" might mean something flimsier than ABS.

Oxford fabric: the hard-wearing everyday surface

Oxford is a woven fabric, usually polyester, with a basketweave texture. You will see it on backpacks and camping chairs because it resists tears and shrugs off spills. On a window bed, an Oxford cat window hammock gives a solid, supportive surface that is easy to wipe down. It is not as breezy as mesh, so on the hottest days it can feel a touch warm, but for year-round use it is a dependable choice.

I keep an Oxford bed for the cooler months because it holds a little warmth from the sun and Miso seems to like the slight cushion of it. If your cat is a vigorous scratcher, Oxford tends to outlast thinner textiles.

Mesh: the breathable summer favourite

A mesh cat window hammock uses an open weave that lets air move through. In a warm climate this is the big selling point, because a cat lying in a sunbeam can still cool off. Mesh also lets you see straight through to the view, which some cats appear to enjoy. The trade-off is that mesh can snag if a cat's claw catches, so it suits cats that mostly lounge rather than dig.

Living near the coast, I notice Miso pants less on mesh during February. For a hot apartment, mesh is my first recommendation, with the honest note that it is a summer hero rather than an all-season cushion.

Plush and liner options

Some beds add a plush or padded liner on top of the base fabric. This is cosy in winter and gentle on older cats' joints, but it traps heat and needs more washing. I treat plush as a seasonal extra rather than a must-have. If you choose a plush liner, make sure it is removable so you can wash it when Miso decides the sunniest spot is also the shedding spot.

How the materials affect cleaning

Whatever the surface, the bed will collect fur, the odd biscuit crumb, and paw prints. Oxford and mesh both wipe down with a damp cloth, and many beds have a cover you can unzip. I give Miso's bed a quick vacuum with the brush head, then a wipe, roughly every fortnight. A bed you cannot clean easily becomes a bed you stop using, so material choice matters for hygiene as much as comfort.

I want to be straight about limits. Our standard twin-suction hammock is suited to small and medium cats up to about 15 kg, whether it wears Oxford or mesh. For large cats at 15 kg and above, I would point you to a reinforced 4-suction or steel-cable model rather than suggest our standard bed will hold a heavy large breed. The materials are lovely, but the mounting is what carries the weight, and that is where the bigger model earns its keep.

Frequently asked questions

What is ABS in a cat window bed frame?

ABS is a tough, light plastic that keeps the bed rigid without adding weight the suction cups must hold. It handles temperature changes better than brittle recycled plastic.

Is an Oxford cat window hammock better than mesh?

Oxford is tougher and easier to wipe, good year-round. Mesh breathes better in heat. I use Oxford in winter and mesh in summer, depending on the weather.

Do mesh beds snag on cat claws?

They can if a claw catches, so mesh suits cats that lounge more than they dig. Check the surface occasionally and wipe it clean.

Are plush liners worth it?

For winter warmth and older cats, yes, but they trap heat and need washing. Choose a removable liner so you can clean it when fur builds up.

How should I clean the bed materials?

Wipe Oxford and mesh with a damp cloth, vacuum fur with a brush head, and wash any removable cover gently. A clean bed stays in use longer.

Will the Oxford or mesh bed hold a large cat?

Our standard twin-suction hammock suits small and medium cats up to about 15 kg. For cats at 15 kg and above, use a reinforced 4-suction or steel-cable model.
