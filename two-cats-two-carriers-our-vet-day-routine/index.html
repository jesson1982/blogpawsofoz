Two Cats, Two Carriers: Our Vet Day Routine

By Sophie BennettSep 4, 2026Pet Carriers & Backpacks7 min read

Short answer: Two cats need two carriers, not one big one. We load Miso first because she walks in calmly, put both carriers side by side on the back seat with a seatbelt through each handle, and cover the noisier one with a towel. The trip works because we pack the night before and leave ten minutes earlier than we think we need to.

We only meant to have one cat. Then Pip came through our spare room as a foster kitten in March and never left, and suddenly vet day meant two cats, two appointments and one 20 minute drive with both of them complaining in stereo. The first attempt was a disaster with a single large carrier, a late start and a waiting room full of dogs. The second attempt, a month later, went so smoothly that I did it alone. The difference was not the cats. It was the order we did everything in.

Why we stopped using one big carrier

One large carrier with two cats in it looks efficient until you are in the waiting room. They lean on each other, one gets a tail in the face, and by the time the vet opens the door both are wound up and the appointment starts from a bad place. Two carriers also mean two separate openings, so one cat can be examined while the other stays covered and out of sight. Ours are the same model, hard-sided, 55cm long, with a top opening and a front door that lifts off. That top opening is the feature I would look for again in cat carriers, because it is what makes the consult table easy. Identical carriers matter more than people expect: no cat gets the better box, and both smell the same. The big one we started with now lives in the laundry holding towels.

The loading order

Order is not about who is more important. Miso loads first because she walks in when there is a towel in the bottom and a treat at the back, and once she is in, Pip will follow the noise rather than fight the box. If we load the difficult one first, the crying starts in the hallway and does not stop until we are home. Front door closed, top lid checked, then straight out to the car. The second carrier comes out already open with the same kind of towel, and we never put it down on the ground where a cat can bolt under a parked car. Both loads happen inside the flat, not on the driveway.

The car setup

Both carriers go on the back seat, side by side, each with the seatbelt through the handle and the belt locked so the carrier cannot slide. Not stacked, not on a lap, not in the boot. The air conditioning goes to the rear vents before the cats go in, and in summer the car is cooled for five minutes first, because a carrier in a hot car turns into an oven faster than people believe. A towel over the top of the noisy carrier cuts most of the crying. There is a spare towel, a roll of paper towel and a flat plastic tray in the boot, and the tray has been used twice in eighteen months. A folded towel between the two carriers stops them knocking together on the drive.

  • Two carriers of the same model, so neither cat gets a strange box.
  • Load the calm cat first and let the other one hear it happen.
  • Seatbelt each carrier separately on the back seat, never stack them.
  • Cool the car before the cats go in, not once they are inside.
  • Pack the night before: towels, wipes and both appointment times.

The waiting room

The waiting room is where the routine either holds or falls apart. We book back to back appointments with the same vet, first slot of the morning if we can get it. Both carriers go on the floor against the wall, side by side, half covered with a towel, and we sit on the floor beside them rather than on the chairs. One of us takes the first cat in while the other stays with the second, so the cat left behind can hear a familiar voice through the mesh. The receptionists know us now and put us in the corner. If the room is full of dogs, we wait outside or in the car with the air running.

What we pack the night before

Two towels that smell of home, one per carrier, a pack of wipes, a spare liner, both cats' records in one folder and a list of the questions I always forget to ask. The carriers come out of the cupboard and sit open in the lounge room with the towels inside, so the cats spend the evening walking past them instead of meeting them cold at the door. The folder stays in the same place, so nobody is hunting for vaccination records at 8am. That single habit did more for our vet days than anything else we tried. On the morning itself, feeding depends on what is happening at the clinic, so ask your vet what applies to your cats rather than copying our routine.

What we changed after a bad trip

The worst trip involved one carrier, both cats and a 34 degree afternoon. Nobody was hurt, but it was two weeks before Miso would walk past a carrier without flattening herself against the wall, and I have not repeated it. What changed: two carriers, always; the car cooled before loading; a towel over the loud one; appointments booked for the first slot; and a hard rule that a vet trip is not combined with anything else. No groceries on the way home. The cats come out of the car, straight into the flat, and the carriers stay open on the floor for a day so nobody links them with a closing door. The towels go straight into the wash, and the carriers get wiped down before they go back in the cupboard.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can two cats share one carrier for a short trip?

They can, and some cats tolerate it for five minutes to a local clinic. The trouble is that you cannot separate them if one panics, and a carrier with two frightened cats in it is hard to open safely. For anything longer than a short local drive, two carriers is the calmer and safer choice.

What size carrier suits a cat around 5kg?

Long enough for the cat to lie down and turn around, which for most 5kg cats means about 50 to 55cm of internal length and at least 30cm of height. Check the internal measurement, not the outside, because handles and vents eat into the useable space. A carrier the cat can stand up in is easier at the clinic.

Where is the safest place for a carrier in the car?

On the back seat, with the seatbelt through the handle and the belt locked, or on the floor behind the front passenger seat if the carrier fits without tipping. Never on a lap, never loose in the boot and never stacked. The carrier should not be able to slide if you brake hard, because a sliding box is what frightens cats most.

Do I need two carriers if only one cat has an appointment?

No, but take the second cat's carrier anyway if you are bringing both. Cats who travel together and wait together tend to settle faster, and the second carrier gives you somewhere calm to put the cat who is not being seen. If you are leaving one at home, one carrier is fine.

My cat cries the whole trip. What helps?

Cover the carrier so there is less to look at, keep the car cool, and drive smoothly rather than braking late. Leave the carrier out at home with a towel inside so it is not a once-a-year object. If the distress is severe, ask your vet about it rather than trying to manage it at the traffic lights.
