Lick Mats and Slow Bowls: Calm in a Smear of Yoghurt

By David ThompsonJun 23, 2026Cat & Dog Toys2 min read

Short answer: a lick mat bought me ten quiet minutes with Duke in a thunderstorm, because repetitive licking releases calming chemicals and gives an anxious dog a job that isn't pacing. You'll want one for storms, nail-trim time and busy evenings, and a slow feeder bowl for the dog who hoovers dinner, since its maze walls pace the meal. Wash the mat daily and check the suction cups stick, or it slides and redecorates your floor.

Someone told me licking is calming for dogs, and I'll admit I was sceptical. Then I smeared greek yoghurt on a mat during a thunderstorm and watched Duke — normally a pacing, drooling mess — lie down and work at it until the noise passed. We were both converts after that night.

Why licking settles a dog

Repetitive licking releases calming chemicals and gives an anxious dog a job that isn't pacing or barking. That makes lick mats handy for storms, nail-trim time, post-wind-downs, and busy evenings when you need twenty minutes of peace. A slow feeder bowl does a related job at mealtimes, pacing a dog who'd otherwise hoover dinner in seconds.

Hygiene is the boring-but-important part: wash mats daily (most are top-rack dishwasher safe) and check the suction cups actually stick — a sliding mat frustrates the dog and redecorates your floor.

Frequently asked questions

Do lick mats really calm dogs?

For many, yes — repetitive licking helps settle mild anxiety like storms or grooming.

What should I put on a lick mat?

Plain yoghurt, xylitol-free peanut butter or wet food. Freeze for longer sessions.

Are lick mats safe to leave with a dog?

Supervised use is best — some dogs chew the mat itself once it's clean.

How is a slow feeder different?

It paces meals through maze walls; a lick mat is for settling, not dinner.

How do I clean a lick mat?

Top rack of the dishwasher or hot soapy water daily.
