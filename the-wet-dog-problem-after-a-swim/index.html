The Wet Dog Problem: What a Seat Cover Faces After a Swim

By Tom RileySep 1, 2026Dog Car Seat Covers7 min read

Short answer: A wet dog in the back seat is not the problem, it is what stays wet after the swim that does the damage. Water trapped under a cover breeds mould, rots stitching and turns the cabin into a swamp within a day in Top End humidity. Drain and dry the dog and the cover, and most covers last a full season of river stops.

Last wet season I pulled into a billabong west of Katherine with Scout, my Border Collie, after a long corrugated run from Darwin. He hit the water before the ute stopped rolling, came back sopping, and shook all over the back seat. By the time we reached the next camp the cheap cover I had thrown in was already dark with water along the seams. Every Top End run ends the same way: a swim, a wet dog, and a cover that has to cope with what the water leaves behind. The question is not whether your dog gets wet. It is what the cover does once it is.

What the water actually does to a cover

Most car seat covers are not waterproof in the way people imagine. They are water resistant, which means water sits on the surface, then finds the seams. On a quilted or padded cover the fill soaks it up like a sponge and stays damp for days. I have pulled covers out of the boot after a weekend trip and found the underside black with mould, even when the top looked clean. In the build-up, when the humidity sits at ninety percent and will not break, a damp cover can go sour before you finish the drive home. This is why the swim is the easy part and the drying is the real test.

Why the cheap ones go mouldy in the boot

The cheap covers fail in one predictable spot: the backing. A ten dollar special has a thin plastic backing that cracks along the fold lines, and once it cracks the foam underneath holds water. I kept one in the boot rolled up after a swim near Mary River, and a week later it smelled like a swamp. The stitching is the other weak point. Cotton thread swells, then rots, and the seams split where the dog's nails catch. A costlier cover uses polyester thread and a tighter weave, and survives a wet season without going rank. You do not need the most expensive option, but the bottom of the market is where the mould starts. When you shop for dog seat covers, the backing and the thread tell you more than the label does.

The draining routine that saves the back seat

I drain Scout before he gets near the ute. At the water's edge I use a short lead and a towel to rub him down, working from the chest back, and I make him wait in the shade for ten minutes so the worst of it drips out. Once he is in the back I lay a cheap microfibre mat under him that I can pull out and wring. The mat takes the load, the cover stays mostly dry, and at the next stop I shake the mat and hang it on the bull bar. It is a small habit, but it is the difference between a cover that smells fresh and one that ferments in the heat.

Drying the cover on the road

You cannot wash a cover clean on the road the way you would at home, but you can dry it. At every stop longer than half an hour I unclip the cover and drape it over the roof rack or a fence, never folded. Folded damp fabric traps the wet in the middle and the middle is where the mould begins. If the day is overcast I run the ute with the air conditioner on fresh, windows cracked, cover hung across the headrests. The key is moving air. Still, damp fabric in a sealed cabin goes off fast, so I never leave a wet cover rolled in the boot overnight if I can help it.

  • Water finds the seams, so a cover's weakness is where the stitching sits.
  • Drain the dog at the water's edge before he reaches the back seat.
  • Dry the cover unfolded on a rack, never rolled in the boot overnight.
  • Cheap cotton-threaded covers rot at the seams within a single wet season.
  • Moving air beats cabin heat, so crack the windows when drying inside.

Reading the early signs of a failing cover

Long before a cover fails it tells you. The first sign is a faint sour smell that returns a day after you wash it, which means mould has reached the padding. The second is stiffening along the seams, where the backing has started to crack. I check my cover at every wash and replace it the moment the backing goes brittle, because a cover that sheds bits of plastic onto the seat is worse than no cover. In the Top End the season is long and the humidity is relentless, so I treat a cover as a consumable, not a lifetime buy. Scout does not care what the cover looks like. He cares that it is dry when he lies down.

A setup that works for a season of swims

My current setup is boring because it works. A hard-shell bench cover clipped tight, a microfibre mat on top that I swap and wring at each stop, and a second mat in the esky so one is always dry. Scout gets drained at the water, matted in the back, and the cover comes off to dry whenever we park for lunch. It is just the habit of not letting water sit where the dog lies. The cover is the last line, not the only one, and that is what keeps a wet season from ruining the back seat.

Frequently Asked Questions

Should I buy a waterproof or water resistant seat cover?

For a dog that swims, water resistant is fine if you drain and dry the way I described, because no cover stays dry under a soaked Border Collie. A fully waterproof layer just moves the water to the seams and the dog. I would rather have a cover that dries fast than one that claims to block every drop. Ask your vet if your dog has skin trouble that a damp ride might worsen.

How do I stop the back seat smelling after river trips?

The smell is trapped water, not the dog, so the fix is drying, not perfume. Unclip the cover at every long stop and drape it somewhere with moving air, and wring the underlay mat so it is not sitting in a puddle. If a sour note returns after washing, the mould has reached the padding and the cover needs replacing. A dry seat does not smell, full stop.

Can I just towel the dog and skip the cover?

You can, but a soaked Border Collie holds more water than a towel takes, and he will shake it onto the seat the moment you corner. The cover is there for the shake and the claw, not the initial wet. I towel at the water and still run a cover, because the back seat is easier to clean than the car's foam underneath it.

What is the fastest way to dry a cover on a cloudy day?

Hang it across the headrests with the ute running on fresh air and the windows cracked, so the cabin pulls the moisture out. Do not fold it, because folded damp fabric goes mouldy in the middle. If you are camped, rig a line between two trees and let the breeze do it. Still air in a closed boot is the enemy, so avoid that whenever you can.

How often should I replace a seat cover used for swims?

Treat it as a consumable. In Top End humidity a cover that sees weekly swims is usually done in a season, shown by a brittle backing, split seams or a smell that survives a wash. A cover used only on dry trips lasts far longer. When the backing cracks, replace it, because bits of plastic on the seat are worse than the bare vinyl underneath.
