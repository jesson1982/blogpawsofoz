The Bed the Cat Took Over: Sharing Sleep Space

By Sophie BennettSep 4, 2026Beds & Mats7 min read

Short answer: Two beds, placed apart and at different heights, ended our nightly standoff. Wally sleeps on the floor bed beside ours, Miso sleeps up on a chest of drawers in a covered bed, and neither has to give ground. One bed shared by a cat and a Cavoodle worked for about a week, and never overnight.

Our bedroom is 3.4 by 3 metres, which is enough for a bed, a wardrobe and not much else. For the first month after Miso arrived, Wally's bed sat at the foot of ours and she treated it as a thing to be claimed. He would stand over it, sigh, and look at me. She would settle in the middle with her chin on the rim and go to sleep. The standoff always happened at 2am, and it always sounded the same: one heavy flop, one hiss, and then a dog standing in the doorway waiting to be invited up.

How the standoff started

Wally had that bed for three years before Miso came home. It is a flat oval bed, about 90cm long, with a low bolster and a cover that comes off for washing. When we brought the kitten in, she was small enough to disappear into the middle of it, and she did, immediately. The first week looked like a truce. The second week she started sleeping there at night, which meant Wally arrived at 10pm to find his bed already occupied and no obvious way to negotiate. He is a polite dog, so he did not growl. He stood there for a while, then came and put his chin on my side of the mattress, and I lay awake feeling guilty about furniture.

The peace deals we tried

We tried three. The first was letting Wally up on the bed, which lasted four nights before my hip gave out and I started sleeping on a diagonal. The second was putting a second bed right beside the first, which solved nothing, because both animals wanted the same patch of floor and Miso simply took the better of the two. The third was a covered cat bed on the floor near the window, which she ignored for a fortnight and then used in daylight only. Each one worked for a few nights and then unravelled. Looking back, all three had the same flaw: both beds sat at the same height, in the same traffic path, at the same time of night, so there was always something to argue over.

Why the second bed failed at first

Placement, mostly. The spare bed sat between the wardrobe and the door, which is the coldest, draughtiest strip of floor in the flat, and it faced the doorway so any movement woke whoever was in it. Miso used it once and went back to the oval bed. Wally would not use it at all. We also made the mistake of putting it down at bedtime, so it smelled of nothing and had no history. A bed that appears at 10pm and disappears at 7am reads as furniture rather than as a place to sleep. Beds need to sit still for a while before an animal trusts them, and we had been moving ours around like props.

  • Two beds at different heights solved more than any amount of coaxing.
  • A bed that moves every night never becomes a place the pet trusts.
  • Put the cat bed up high, out of the dog's eyeline, not beside his.
  • Wash covers on a rota so no bed is ever empty on wash day.
  • A dog needs a bed he can enter without ducking under a rim.

The placement change that ended it

The fix was a chest of drawers, 90cm high, against the wall on my side, with a covered bed on top. Miso can get up there in one jump and Wally cannot, which sounds unfair until you watch what happens. She goes up at 9pm and stays there. He takes the floor bed at the foot of ours. The bedroom goes quiet. The dresser bed is also out of his eyeline when he is lying down, so he stops monitoring it, and a dog who is not monitoring the cat is a dog who sleeps. The other half of the fix was putting his oval bed back exactly where it had been for three years, against the wall, head end into the corner, so he could sleep with his back covered. Nothing else changed.

What the two beds are now

The floor bed has a removable cover and a waterproof liner, because a 9kg Cavoodle who has been at the beach brings half of it home. The cover is washed weekly and the liner wiped. The dresser bed is smaller, about 45cm across, with a hood and a fleece pad that goes in the machine every second week. Neither was expensive. The test we apply to anything in dog beds is whether the cover comes off without a fight, and both of these pass. The orthopaedic mattress we bought for Wally two years ago lives in the lounge room, where he uses it in the evenings, and it has never been part of the bedroom argument. Three beds, three jobs, and only two of them in the room where we sleep.

The rule we keep now

Nothing moves after dinner. Beds stay where they are, covers get changed in the morning, and if one is in the wash the spare goes down in the same spot rather than somewhere new. We also stopped inviting Wally up, which was harder than it sounds, because he asks so politely. Two weeks of consistently pointing at his own bed and he stopped asking. Miso still takes the warm patch on the duvet if I get up in the night, and that is fine. The standoff was never really about the cat or the dog. It was about one bed doing two jobs in a room too small to hold two arguments, and it took us a month to see that.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do cats and dogs ever share one bed happily?

In daylight, sometimes, especially if the dog is old and the cat is bold. Overnight it is a different calculation, because a cat moving at 3am wakes a dog who then wants to investigate. If you want one shared bed, expect it to be a daytime arrangement and keep a second bed ready for the nights.

Should the cat bed be up high or on the floor?

Up high works better in a house with a dog, because height gives the cat a way out of any interaction. A chest of drawers, a wide windowsill or the top of a sturdy bookcase are all fine. Check that the landing is clear and that the cat can get down without a jump onto tiles.

Is a covered cat bed better than an open one?

Ours is, but mostly because it sits at height and the hood blocks the light. A covered bed gives a cat somewhere to hide with one eye on the room. Some cats will not use a hood at all and prefer a flat mat, so if you are unsure, buy the cheaper open option first and see which one gets used.

What size bed suits a small dog who likes to stretch out?

Measure the dog nose to tail base while stretched out flat, then add about 15cm. Wally is 9kg and 55cm from nose to tail base, and the 90cm oval gives him room to sprawl without hanging over an edge. A bed that only fits him curled up gets abandoned by morning.

Can I move a bed once the pet has claimed it?

You can, but do it in daylight and leave the old spot empty for a few days rather than filling it with something else. Moving a bed at night, or swapping the bed itself, tends to restart the whole settling process. Ours moved twice before we worked this out, and both times cost us about a week.
