Where to Buy Playpens in Perth: What's in Stock at Each Price

By Maddie SorensenSep 5, 2026Pet Playpens6 min read

Short answer: Perth has playpens at every price, but what's on the shelf swings with the season. As a local groomer I see under $50 soft pens at the big-box chains, reliable $80 to $120 metal at the pet store chains, and niche sizes through WA specialists. Stock thins in the late-spring puppy rush, so timing your visit helps, and a phone call ahead saves a wasted drive across the city.

The budget end: big-box and variety chains

The large Perth chains carry soft six and eight-panel pens from around thirty to sixty dollars. They're fine for small dogs and cats, and they turn over fast, so the shelf is usually full. The trade is basic zips and lighter frames, which is fair at the price, and they suit a first try without much risk.

I send first-time cat owners here, because a cheap soft pen is a low-risk way to try the idea. If the cat refuses it, the loss is small; if it works, you've spent wisely and learned what you want next time.

Check the zip and the stitching before you leave the car park, though. At this price the quality control varies store to store and even box to box, and swapping a dud for a better one is easy on the day and slow by email afterwards. Five minutes with the zip slider tells you more than any label on the shelf.

The middle: pet store chains

The national pet chains around Perth stock the $80 to $120 metal and better soft pens, including eight-panel units with zip floors. This is where most of my clients land, because the latch quality steps up and the warranty is easier to claim in person, which matters when a zip inevitably tires.

  • Under $50: soft, big-box chains.
  • $80 to $120: metal and zip-floor soft, pet chains.
  • $120 plus: specialist and some online WA stock.
  • Seasonal: thinner late spring and December.
  • Phone specialists for niche sizes first.

The chains also run frequent sales, roughly in line with long weekends and the big pet events through the year. I'm not going to pretend to know each chain's calendar, but in my experience the same $100 pen moves within a few dollars of $80 a few times a year. If your puppy date is flexible, waiting a fortnight for a promo costs nothing except patience.

Specialist and niche sizes

For an unusual size, a heavy breed, or a particular colour, the WA specialist stores and some smaller stockists are worth a call. They don't always hold deep stock, so phone ahead. I've saved clients a wasted drive by checking first, and a specialist will often order a floor or pins you can't get at the chains.

Some carry spare floors and pins too, which matters more than people expect once a zip tires. A twenty-dollar spare part beats binning a hundred-dollar pen, and the specialists are where those parts live in Perth.

For the bigger breeds, the specialists also carry the tall panels, generally around the eighty-centimetre mark and above, that the chains rarely shelve. A springy young kelpie or a Staffy cross clears a standard sixty-centimetre panel sooner than most owners expect, so if you already know your breed is athletic, ask for the tall panels by name.

When shelves run thin

The puppy-buying wave hits Perth in late spring and builds into December. Pens, especially metal eights, sell out locally then. If you're expecting a puppy, buy in autumn or early spring and store it, or order early rather than scramble, because the shelf goes bare exactly when everyone wants one.

Summer itself stays busy with moves and new pets, so the thin window is real and predictable. I tell expecting owners to treat the pen like a cot: sort it a month before the puppy, not the day they arrive.

Online versus in-store in WA

Online widens choice but adds a wait and a freight cost on heavy metal. In-store lets you feel the latch and check the zip, which I rate highly for a first pen. For a second pen or a known model, online is fine; for your first, the hand test at a Perth shop is worth the trip.

If you're comparing before you leave home, our playpen category shows the types stocked locally. Match the price band to the home and the pet, and you'll rarely need to return what you buy.

Freight across WA is the other honest cost. Outside the metro area, delivery on a heavy metal pen can add a noticeable sum, and returns mean a drive or a courier fee either way. That tips the maths further towards buying well the first time and calling ahead to confirm the exact model is on the shelf before you commit to the drive.

My Perth buying rule of thumb

Budget soft for a cat or a trial; mid metal for a puppy or a dog; specialist for an odd size. Buy a season early if you can, and always feel the latch in person for a first pen. That simple ladder has steered most of my clients right without a single wasted Saturday.

Pair the rule with one habit: ring at opening time on a weekday if you can. Staff have more time to pull boxes out, open a demo, and check the parts list with you, and my clients who shop mid-morning midweek always come home better informed than the Saturday crowd.
