Metal vs Soft Dog Playpen: Which Suits Your Dog?

By Sophie BennettJul 29, 2026Pet Playpens6 min read

Sophie & the Fur Babies — pet playpens for renters, puppies & cats

Soon after Wally arrived I found myself stuck on the same question every new dog owner hits: do I buy a metal dog playpen or a soft dog playpen? The shops show both, the prices sit in different ranges, and the photos all make each look perfect. I ended up owning one of each at different points, so I can tell you from messy first-hand experience what actually separates them once the initial excitement wears off.

This is not a "one is best" piece, because the honest answer depends on your dog, your home and your week. A Cavoodle in a Perth flat has very different needs from a cattle dog in a regional backyard. Below I break down the real differences so you can pick the one that fits your life instead of the one with the prettiest listing.

How each style is built

A metal dog playpen is usually eight powder coated panels you join with clips or hinges. The coating matters in Australia because bare steel rusts fast near the coast, so a good powder coated finish keeps it tidy through humid summers. The panels stand on their own feet and can be arranged into a square, a rectangle or an octagon depending on your space.

A soft dog playpen is fabric and mesh over a light frame, often with a zip roof and a padded base. It pops up like a tent and folds to a flat disc. There are no sharp edges, which is why Miso the kitten tolerated it near her food bowls, and why I felt calm leaving Wally in it while I showered. The give in the sides is the trade-off: a soft pen is gentle but not escape-proof for a climber.

The side-by-side comparison

Here is the plain comparison I wish someone had handed me at the start. I have rated each on the things that actually affect daily life rather than the spec-sheet fluff.

For my situation the soft style won on three of those five points, which is why I lean on it day to day. But I would not pretend the metal one is useless — far from it.

When metal is the better call

If your dog is large, strong, or still chewing everything in sight, the metal option earns its price. Wally's metal-owning friend is a Kelpie cross who would have the mesh version shredded by lunchtime. Metal also suits outdoor time: it stands up to rain, the panels do not blow around, and you can peg it into grass at a campsite.

The other win for metal is shape. Because each panel moves, you can build a long barrier across a verandah or wrap it around a tree. A soft pen basically stays one shape. So if flexibility of layout matters more than fold-away ease, metal is the practical pick.

When soft is the better call

For a flat like mine, the soft octagonal pen is the calmer choice. It is quiet when the dog bumps it, it will not scratch the floor, and it disappears behind the laundry door before a rental inspection. Wally sleeps in his without a fuss, and because the panels are see-through mesh he does not feel shut away from the room.

We sell the soft octagonal style in our own store because it is what I would recommend to most apartment and first-time owners. It comes in three sizes, folds flat, and costs less than a metal kit while still giving a small dog or cat a proper den. I am not claiming it beats metal for a big backyard dog, only that for the average renter it is the smarter starting point.

Mixing the two

You do not have to choose forever. Plenty of owners keep a soft pen indoors and a metal one for the yard or trips away. I started with soft, borrowed a metal one for a coastal caravan weekend, and now keep both. The soft stays in the lounge, the metal lives in the car boot ready for camping. That combo has covered every situation Wally has thrown at me.

My honest verdict

If you are unsure, begin with a soft dog playpen in the medium size and upgrade to metal only if your dog outgrows it or you need outdoor toughness. You will waste less money that way, and a puppy grows faster than you expect. Match the tool to the dog, not the other way around, and either style will serve you well.

Frequently asked questions

Which lasts longer, metal or soft?

A powder coated metal pen outlasts a soft one, especially outdoors or with a chewer. Soft pens are built for gentle indoor use and will show wear sooner if scratched.

Is a soft pen safe for a teething puppy?

It is fine for supervised quiet time, but a puppy in full chew mode can damage mesh. Move to a metal pen if your pup is destroying the fabric rather than resting in it.

Will a metal pen scratch my rental floors?

The panel feet can mark timber if dragged, so lift rather than slide, or place a mat underneath. Many renters keep a rug under the pen to protect the bond.

Can I use a soft pen outside?

For short, supervised sessions on grass it is fine, but soft fabric and mesh do not love rain or strong wind. Bring it in after use and let it dry before folding.

Does the soft octagonal style fold flat?

Yes. It collapses to a slim disc or a carry bag, which is exactly why renters like it — it slides behind a door or under a bed before an inspection.

Which style is cheaper in Australia?

Soft pens generally start lower, around $69 to $119, while metal 8-panel sets sit closer to $99 to $160 depending on size and coating quality.
