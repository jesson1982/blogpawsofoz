Puppy Toilet Training with a Playpen

By Sophie BennettJul 25, 2026Pet Playpens7 min read

Short answer: set the pen up as two zones, a bed at one end and a toilet tray at the other, because puppies keep the two apart if you give them room. At eight weeks his bladder was basically a timer with a tail, and the lease flat floor stays forgiving when accidents are easy to clean. You'll want a real-grass tray rather than a pad if your pup chews the substrate, and winding the pen back as he grew kept the habit honest without locking him in all day.

Bringing Wally home was the loveliest kind of chaos, and the first lesson he taught me was that a tiny Cavoodle bladder is basically a timer with a tail. I had read the books, but nothing prepares you for the 3am realisation that the floor of a rented flat is not forgiving of mistakes. The thing that saved my sanity, and probably my bond with my property manager, was setting up a puppy exercise pen that gave him room to be right and made accidents easy to clean rather than catastrophic.

Why a pen helps a puppy learn

Dogs are naturally tidy creatures. Given the choice, they will not soil the spot where they sleep, and a pen lets you use that instinct instead of fighting it. The trick is space: too small and the bed and the toilet are forced together, so the puppy has no choice but to mess near where it rests. Too large and a small pup can wander off and hide an accident in a corner you do not notice until later. A puppy confinement pen sized so the bed sits at one end and the toilet at the other teaches the right habit by making it the easy habit.

In our place near the Perth coast, the tiled kitchen corner was perfect. Tiles wipe clean, the pen sits flat, and I am usually in the kitchen anyway, so Wally was never lonely while he learned. The pen was not a cage to shut him away; it was a clear, kind classroom with two rooms and a very simple rule.

Setting up the two-zone pen

I put a washable bed and a soft toy at the far end from the door, and a puppy toilet tray with a real-grass pad or a newspaper layer at the door end. Puppies tend to want to go as soon as they wake or finish a meal, and because the toilet was right where he naturally headed, success came quickly. When he used it, I praised him in a calm voice and gave a tiny treat, then let him out for a play in the lounge once he was empty. The pen made the good choice the obvious one.

If you are in an apartment on a high floor like me, a balcony toilet trip is not always quick, so having the tray inside the pen during the early weeks is practical. As he grew and our routine settled, we moved more of his business to proper outdoor walks, and the indoor tray became a backup rather than the main event.

A sample toilet-training schedule

Every puppy is different, and Wally's rhythm will not match your dog's exactly, but a schedule gives you a framework so you are not guessing. This is the pattern that worked for a young Cavoodle in a rental flat, adjusted as he got older and could hold on longer.

TimeActionNotes
6:30amStraight to toilet zone on wakingBladder full after sleep, go before play
7:00amBreakfast, then toilet 5–10 min laterEating triggers the urge, watch closely
Mid-morningToilet every 1–2 hoursYoung pups cannot wait long
AfternoonToilet after each nap and playExcitement and sleep both prompt a trip
5:00pmDinner, then toilet 5–10 min laterSame as breakfast routine
8:30pmLast toilet before bedtimeCalm, no rough play beforehand
OvernightOne trip at ~2am early onDrop night trips as he matures

Following that rhythm, Wally was reliably clean in the pen within a few weeks, and clean in the flat not long after. The schedule did the remembering so I did not have to, which is a gift when you are tired and a puppy is nudging your ankle at dawn.

Kindness is the whole method

I want to say this plainly: punishment does not work for toilet training, and it can make things worse. A puppy that is scared of you will sneak off to toilet where you cannot see, which builds exactly the hidden-accident habit you are trying to avoid. When Wally had a slip, I cleaned it without fuss, used an enzymatic cleaner so the smell did not call him back, and quietly thought about what in the schedule had failed. Almost always it was me being slow to the door, not him being naughty.

The positive side is lovely. When he got it right, the calm praise and the small reward told him everything he needed, and he started heading to the tray on his own because good things happened there. A puppy confinement pen makes this easy because the right spot is always within reach, so the puppy practises success dozens of times a day instead of failing out of confusion.

Managing the kitten while you train

Miso, my rescue kitten, was already litter-trained and unimpressed by the new roommate. Her tray lives well away from Wally's pen, because cats are private about these things and a puppy investigating the cat toilet is a hassle nobody needs. Keeping their systems separate also meant Wally never confused the cat's covered tray with his own open one. If you have both, give each species their own clearly different setup and the household stays calmer.

There is a bonus for renters here too. Because the pen contains the learning, my carpets and the landlord's carpets stayed clean, and the final inspection was uneventful. A puppy exercise pen is, in a funny way, also a bond-protection device for anyone renting a flat.

Winding back the pen as they grow

The goal was never to pen Wally forever. As he learned, I left the door open during the day so he could choose the tray or the lounge, and I praised him for using the outdoor spots on walks. Slowly the indoor tray became unused, and I folded the pen flat to live behind the laundry door until the next camping trip. The pen had done its job: it taught a baby dog where to go, kindly and clearly, and then got out of the way.

If you are starting out with a puppy in a small home, set up the two zones, keep a gentle schedule, and trust the puppy's own tidy nature. With a bit of patience and a puppy confinement pen that works with you rather than against you, the accidents become a fading memory and the good habits stick for life.

Frequently asked questions

How big should a puppy toilet-training pen be?

Large enough for a bed at one end and a toilet at the other, with a little floor between so the puppy keeps the two separate. Too small forces messing near the bed; too large hides accidents.

Should I punish my puppy for accidents?

No. Punishment builds fear and hidden accidents. Clean calmly with an enzymatic product, review your schedule, and praise the successes so the puppy learns where the good things happen.

How often does a young puppy need the toilet?

Very often: after waking, after meals, after play, and roughly every one to two hours through the day. A simple schedule helps you stay ahead of a small bladder.

Can I use the pen instead of outdoor walks?

The pen is for learning and safety at home, not a replacement for walks. As your pup matures, shift more toileting to outdoor trips and keep the indoor tray as a backup.

My puppy eats the toilet pad, what do I do?

Swap to a real-grass tray or a different substrate, supervise closely, and ask your vet if ingestion continues, as some puppies need guidance away from chewing the pad.

When can I stop using the pen?

Once the puppy is reliably clean and tells you it wants out, leave the door open during the day, then pack the pen away gradually as the habit proves itself.
