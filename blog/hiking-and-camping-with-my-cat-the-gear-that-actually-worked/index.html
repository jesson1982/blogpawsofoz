Hiking & Camping With My Cat: The Gear That Actually Worked

By Ella TranJan 15, 2026Pet Carriers & Backpacks7 min read

Not everything survived the trip. Here's what did, and what I'd pack again tomorrow.

Starting small before the big nights out

Before I ever pitched a tent with a cat, I spent a month just wearing my hiking cat backpack Australia folks seem to love around the house with Mochi inside. We did the lounge, then the backyard, then a flat riverside path. People imagine adventure cats are born confident, but most are made through boring repetition. By the time we drove to the Blue Mountains for a real walk, the backpack was old news to her, which meant the new part was only the trees and the noise. That slow ramp-up is the single thing I'd tell any beginner to copy. Skip it and you're asking a prey animal to trust you in a scary place with zero prep.

The camping pet carrier backpack that earned its keep

For the overnight trips, a camping pet carrier backpack with a proper firm base and side pockets became my anchor. At the campsite I'd unclip it from my back and use it as her little bedroom, lining the base with a folded fleece. Cats like defined space, and giving her a "this is yours" zone stopped the 2am zoomies across the tent. I kept her food and a small scoop of litter in the side pockets so I wasn't digging through my own pack in the dark. The backpack wasn't just transport; it was her portable home, and that consistency is what let her actually relax in a strange place.

Where the bike fits into all this

I'll admit the two-wheeled idea made me nervous, but a bike cat carrier backpack changed the maths. On fire trails that are too long to walk but too rough to drive, I'd strap the carrier to a rear rack or wear it and pedal slowly on flat sections. Mochi tolerated the gentle rides far better than I expected, probably because the motion is smoother than my walking bounce. The rule I made for myself: never faster than a kid on a footpath, and never near traffic. Used that way, the bike opened up trails we'd never have reached on foot in a day, and she got to be a very smug cat about it.

Building outdoor cat backpack adventures that don't backfire

The dream of outdoor cat backpack adventures is easy to romanticise on Instagram and horrible to live if you're unprepared. I pack a collapsible water bowl, a cooling mat for summer walks, and a tiny first-aid pouch with vet wrap. I check for off-leash dog areas and give them a wide berth, because even a friendly dog is a catastrophe for a cat in a backpack. I also tell every walker I pass that there's a cat in the bag, so nobody's startled when a paw appears at the zip. A little honesty on the trail goes a long way toward keeping everyone calm, including Mochi.

The adventure cat backpack gear I'd buy again

If I had to rebuild the kit from scratch, the adventure cat backpack gear I'd absolutely rebuy is simple: a ventilated carrier with a firm base, a clip-in chest strap so the weight doesn't swing, a collapsible bowl, and a long lightweight lead with a harness for supervised grass time. Everything else was nice-to-have. The harness especially paid for itself the one time she slipped a clip at a picnic table and decided the world was hers. I scooped her up in about three seconds because the lead was already on. Gear fails; habit saves you, and the habit is built on the right basics.

Weather, water, and not overthinking the distance

The mistakes I made early were all about ambition. I tried a ten-kilometre loop in summer heat and learned the hard way that a cat in a backpack heats up faster than I do. Now I walk in the cool of the morning, carry more water than I think we need, and treat any plan as flexible. If Mochi gets restless at the zip, that's her telling me we're done, and I listen. The best outdoor days were the modest ones where we turned back early and both enjoyed the ride home. Distance is a vanity metric with a cat; a calm, willing traveller who wants to come again beats a long GPS track every single time. Patience on the small stuff is what buys you the big trips later.

Trail etiquette, because you're not the only one out there

The unwritten rules matter as much as the gear. I step off the path for other hikers, I never let her out where she could disturb wildlife, and I pack out every bit of her litter and waste. An off-leash dog and an exposed cat is everyone's bad day, so I read the energy of oncoming walkers and zip her up early if needed. Honestly, most people are delighted to meet a hiking cat, and a few have asked how I started. I always say the same thing: the gear gets you out the door, but respect for the trail and the other people on it is what lets you keep coming back.

Would I do it again tomorrow?

Every time I unpack the wet gear and find a paw print on the tent pole, I remember the quiet moment at the campsite when Mochi flopped in her carrier and watched the stars come out. The prep is real and not every trip goes to plan, but the trust she's built and the stories I get to tell make it worth every damp sock. If you're on the fence, start with one night at a familiar park and let the cat set the pace. The wilderness will still be there when you're both ready for it.

From a muddy tent and a contented cat, Mochi and me.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I start hiking with a cat?

Ramp up slowly at home first: wear the empty pack, then wear it with the cat for short sessions in the lounge, backyard, and a flat path before any real trail. That boring repetition is what turns a nervous prey animal into a confident adventure cat.

What camping carrier setup works best?

A pack with a firm base and side pockets doubles as her bedroom at camp. Line the base with a folded fleece, keep food and a little litter in the pockets, and give her a defined "this is yours" space to stop midnight zoomies in the tent.

Can I take my cat on a bike?

On quiet fire trails, yes, strapped to a rear rack or worn while you pedal slowly on flat sections. Keep it slower than a kid on a footpath and never near traffic; the smoother motion usually suits cats better than a walking bounce.

What gear is essential for adventure cats?

A ventilated carrier with a firm base, a clip-in chest strap so the load doesn't swing, a collapsible bowl, and a long lightweight lead with a harness for supervised grass time. The harness pays for itself the moment a clip slips.

What trail etiquette should I follow?

Step off the path for other hikers, never let her out where she could disturb wildlife, pack out all her waste, and tell people there's a cat in the bag. Zip her up early around dogs, and respect the trail so you're welcome to return.
