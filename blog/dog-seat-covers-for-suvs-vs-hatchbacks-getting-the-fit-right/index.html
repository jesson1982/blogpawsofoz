Dog Seat Covers for SUVs vs Hatchbacks: Getting the Fit Right

By Mick O'DonnellAug 15, 2026Dog Car Seat Covers7 min read

Mick from the Bush — dog car-travel & ute gear

The missus has a little hatch and I have the SUV, and for the longest time Banjo's cover only fit one of them. I bought the wrong size the first time, a universal thing that left the hatch seat half bare and the SUV seat overflowing. A cover that does not fit is worse than no cover, because the dog finds the bare patch and uses it. So let me save you the argument with your partner and the wasted money. Fit is not glamorous, but it is the difference between a cover that works and a cover that lives in the ute tub unused.

The short version: an SUV has a wider, deeper rear bench and often a taller seat back, while a hatch has a smaller bench and a more sloped seat. A dog seat cover for SUV needs to be broader and longer; a dog seat cover for hatchback can be smaller and still cover the lot. The trap is assuming one size covers both.

Measure before you buy, not after

Grab a tape measure and do it properly. You want the width of the rear bench from door to door, and the depth from the top of the seat back down to the front edge of the cushion. Most listings give the cover size in centimetres or inches, and you match your numbers to theirs with a bit of slack. Do not measure the dog. Measure the seat. Banjo is a medium Heeler and he fits a standard bench, but the seat size is what counts, not the dog size.

I keep a bit of paper in the glovebox with the two measurements written down, one for the hatch and one for the SUV. Sounds simple, but it has stopped me buying the wrong thing more than once at the servo display.

Why the SUV needs the bigger cover

An SUV rear bench is a broad, flat seat built for three adults. Covers sized for that run around 147 by 137 centimetres, which is enough to drape over the backrest and tuck down the front of the cushion. Some of the bigger hammock styles go even wider. The point is coverage: you want the whole cushion and the lower part of the seat back, plus the side flaps reaching the doors. A small cover on a big bench leaves the outer edges bare, and that is exactly where the dog's muddy flank lands.

The SUV also tends to have a higher seat back, so a cover that is too short leaves a gap at the top where hair collects behind the dog. Measure the back height too if you run a hammock style that clips to the front headrests.

The hatch is smaller but trickier

A hatch rear seat is narrower and the backrest leans more. A dog seat cover for hatchback often fits within a 180 by 103 centimetre layout, which sounds bigger on paper but covers a smaller, more compact seat because there is less to wrap. The catch with hatches is the slope: a cover that lies flat on an SUV bench can bunch on a sloped hatch seat if it is not anchored well. So for a hatch you want a cover with decent anchor straps and a grippy backing more than you need a giant size.

Banjo rides in the hatch when the missus takes him to the footpath for a walk, and the smaller cover with good anchors has behaved better than the big one ever did. Size is not everything; how it holds matters.

The universal cover middle ground

A universal dog car seat cover is built to fit most benches with adjustable straps, and for a lot of folks it is the right call. It will not be laser-perfect on either car, but it will sit acceptably on both the SUV and the hatch if you pick the larger size and anchor it well. The downside is a bit of excess fabric on the hatch that you fold under, and slightly less coverage on the very widest SUV benches. For most mixed-household fleets, universal beats buying two.

The time to skip universal is when you have a unusually wide SUV third row or a very small city hatch where the excess fabric becomes a trip hazard. Then you buy to the seat, not the dog.

Getting the fit right on the day

When the cover arrives, fit it in the driveway, not on the way out. Lay it over the bench, clip the headrest straps, tuck the front edge into the seat crease, and check the side flaps reach the doors. Sit the dog on it and watch for bare patches. If you can see the seat through a gap, the cover is too small for that car and you need the next size or a different style.

For us, the SUV runs the 147 by 137 cover and the hatch runs the 180 by 103 with the slack folded under. Banjo reckons neither is his idea of a comfortable ute tub, but both keep the seats clean, and that is the whole point of the exercise.

Frequently asked questions

What size dog seat cover do I need for an SUV?

Most SUV benches need a cover around 147 × 137 cm to cover the wide, flat seat and the lower seat back, with side flaps reaching both doors. Always measure your bench before ordering.

Will a hatchback fit a standard dog seat cover?

Yes, a dog seat cover for hatchback typically fits within a 180 × 103 cm layout, but hatches have sloped seats, so good anchor straps and a grippy backing matter more than raw size.

Can one universal cover fit both my SUV and hatch?

Often yes. A universal dog car seat cover with adjustable straps will sit acceptably on both if you choose the larger size and fold the slack under on the smaller car. It will not be perfect on either.

How do I measure my rear seat for a cover?

Measure the bench width from door to door and the depth from the top of the seat back to the front edge of the cushion. Compare those numbers to the cover's listed dimensions with a little slack.

Why does my cover bunch on the hatch seat?

Hatch seats lean back more than SUV benches, so a flat cover can fold. Use the anchor straps to pull it taut and tuck the front edge into the seat crease to stop the bunching.

Should I use a seat cover or a boot liner?

If the dog rides on the rear seats, use a seat cover. If he rides in the boot, use a cargo liner sized to the boot floor, typically 40" × 70" up to 55" × 91" depending on the car.
