Dogs and Kids Sharing the Back Seat: Covers That Do Both

By Mick O'DonnellAug 14, 2026Dog Car Seat Covers8 min read

Mick from the Bush — dog car-travel & ute gear

Out here the back seat of the ute is not a showroom. It is where Banjo rides after a morning in the paddock, where the nieces and nephews pile in for the run to the servo, and where the esky sits sideways when we head to the coast for the day. For years I figured you had to pick one or the other: a car that looks tidy, or a car that actually gets used. Then the youngest decided the back seat was hers and Banjo reckons it is his, and the two of them started sharing it every weekend. That is when I went looking for a proper dog and kids back seat cover rather than the old towel I had been folding in half.

What I found is that most covers are built for one job. A soft towel soaks up mud but slides the moment you brake. A plastic mat wipes clean but a kid drops a biscuit and you are hunting crumbs for a week. The trick is to find a cover that handles both mud and kids without turning into a project every time you get home. Banjo is not a delicate traveller. He brings in red dust, creek water and the occasional dead thing he is proud of. The kids bring sand, ice-cream and questions. The cover has to shrug off all of it.

Why a shared back seat is its own problem

When it is just the dog, a hammock is the easy answer. It stops him dropping into the footwell when I take a corner too quick and it keeps the door panels clean. But sling a hammock up and you have just made the seat useless for a child in a capsule. That is the first thing to understand: a dog car hammock for two dogs is brilliant for dogs, but if you also cart kids you need a cover that converts. The good ones unclip at the front straps and become a flat bench cover in about ten seconds, which means the same bit of gear serves the school run and the beach run.

I learnt this the hard way. First cover I bought was a hammock only. Fine for Banjo. Useless the day my sister turned up with the pram and the toddler and nowhere for them to sit. I ended up with a wet dog on the bench and a cranky kid on my lap for forty k's. Not safe, not smart. A convertible cover, one that does hammock and bench, is the real fix for a family that also runs a dog.

What actually matters when both ride

Washability comes first. Out here the cover gets washed, not wiped. Look for something you can hose off at the trough or chuck in the machine. A 600D Oxford cloth with a PP cotton fill and an EVA backing is the sweet spot: it sheds water, it does not hold smell, and it survives the spin cycle. The EVA backing is the bit that stops it sliding on the seat, which matters more than people think when you are hauling a kid and a Heeler around a roundabout.

Non-slip is the second point. A cover that slides becomes a tripping hazard and a dog that slides becomes a nervous dog. The EVA or rubberised backing keeps it put. Side flaps are the third, and they are the difference between a clean car and a door card that smells like wet kelpie. If the cover has door flaps, the mud stops at the flap, not on your trim. And if you run child seats, ISOFIX-friendly matters: the cover needs openings that line up with the anchor points so you are not fighting the straps.

For the leather-seat crowd, a leather seat dog protector in PU leather is worth a look. The ute has cloth, but the missus's wagon has cream leather and Banjo's claws used to worry her something fierce. A PU leather cover wipes clean and the claws do not dig in the way they do on fabric. It costs more, up around the $80 to $110 mark, but on a leather interior it pays for itself the first time he jumps in soaking wet.

The numbers that tell you what you are buying

Size matters more than the marketing. A basic universal mat runs about 180 by 103 centimetres, which covers a bench but not the doors. A proper hammock like the OUYOOOO style runs 147 by 137 centimetres, which gives you height up the front as well. The hard-bottom extenders, the ones that give a big dog more floor, come in at 51.2 by 60.5 inches and carry anything from 350 to 430 pounds. That last figure is the one I trust: if a cover says it holds 430 pounds, Banjo and a kid and the esky are not going to test it past its limit.

Run the table against whatever you are eyeing and you will see quick which covers are built for both jobs and which are dressed up for one. Most of the cheap $30 to $45 soft hammocks do the dog fine and fail the kids test because they have no ISOFIX access and no real structure. Spend up to the $50 to $70 band and you start getting the hard bottom and the flaps. Go to the leather protector at $80 to $110 and you are protecting the car more than the passenger.

Two dogs, one kid, no drama

If you run a dog car hammock for two dogs, the same logic applies but doubled. Two heelers in the back means twice the dust and twice the chance of a scuffle when one sees a roo. A hammock with a centre divider keeps them apart, and the side flaps keep the doors safe. Add a kid and you want the bench mode available, because a capsule does not fit in a hammock. I know a bloke near Cobar who runs two kelpies and a toddler and he swears by the convertible style: hammock for the dogs on the solo trips, flat bench when the family comes along. Sensible.

The point I keep coming back to is this: do not buy the cover for the dog and hope it suits the kids, or buy it for the kids and expect the dog to behave. Buy the one that converts, that washes, that stays put, and that leaves your ISOFIX anchors free. Everything else is noise.

What I run now

These days the ute wears a convertible hammock with side flaps and an EVA bottom, and the wagon wears a PU leather seat dog protector on the bench because of the cream leather. Banjo reckons the hammock is his couch. The kids reckon the bench is theirs. The car, for the first time in fifteen years, does not smell like a wetland. That is a win worth the money.

Frequently asked questions

Can one cover really work for both a dog and a child seat?

Yes, if it is a convertible style with ISOFIX openings. Clip it up as a hammock for the dog, unclip the front straps for a flat bench when the child seat goes in. Check the anchor points line up before you buy.

Is a hammock safe with a kid in the car?

A hammock blocks the seat, so use bench mode for the child and keep the dog in the cargo area or on the opposite bench. Never let a hammock cover a child seat anchor.

What size cover fits a dual-cab ute bench?

A 180 by 103 centimetre mat covers the base. For door protection and a hammock, look at the 147 by 137 centimetre styles. Measure your seat before ordering.

Will a leather protector stop claw marks?

A PU leather cover takes claws far better than fabric and wipes clean, but no cover is scratch proof against a panicked dog. It is protection, not armour.

How do I wash a cover used by dogs and kids?

Hose it at the trough for mud, or machine wash on cold if the label allows. The 600D Oxford with EVA backing dries quick and keeps its shape.

Are side flaps worth the extra cost?

For a shared back seat, yes. They keep mud and kick marks off the door cards, which is the expensive bit to clean. Skipping them saves a few dollars and costs you the trim.
