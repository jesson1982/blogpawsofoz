Do Dogs Get Car Anxiety? How a Mesh-Window Seat Cover Helps

By Mick O'DonnellAug 19, 2026Dog Car Seat Covers8 min read

Mick from the Bush — dog car-travel & ute gear

I am not a vet, and I will say that plainly before we start, because a fair few of you will read this looking for a cure and the honest answer is that some dogs need a professional. What I am is a station hand who has spent fifteen years with a Blue Heeler, and in that time I have watched Banjo go from a pup who hated the ute to a dog who hops in without a word. That change was not magic. Part of it was the right gear, and a big part of that gear was a cover with a window he could see through.

So, do dogs get car anxiety? In my experience, yes, some of them do. Not in the human sense of worrying about the future, but they get worked up, they pant, they pace, they drool, and some will flat-out refuse to get in. I have seen it in rescue dogs brought onto the property and in townie pups that only ever ride to the vet. The car means something they do not like, and they let you know.

How I read the signs

Banjo was never the worst of them, but as a young dog he would plant his feet at the ute door and refuse. That is anxiety, or at least a firm objection, and you do not want to force a dog that is dug in. What I noticed was that once he was in and could see out, he settled. When he could not see out, boxed in by a solid cover, he paced. The difference was the view.

A dog that cannot see where it is going has no idea if the trip will end somewhere good or bad. The vet, the pound, the beach, all feel the same from a dark box. Give them a window and they start to read the world, and most of them decide it is fine.

Where a mesh window comes in

This is the practical bit. A dog seat cover with mesh window is a back-seat cover with a panel of breathable mesh in the centre, usually set into the hammock that hangs between the front and rear seats. The dog can see through it to the cabin and out the windscreen. The mesh is tough enough to take a paw, but open enough to let air move and let the dog watch where you are going.

For a dog car cover for anxious dogs, that window is the single most useful feature I have found. It is not a sedative. It is just a way to let the dog be part of the trip instead of a prisoner in the back. Banjo will sit and watch the road now, and on a long run to town he will even doze, which he never did when he could not see.

What else settles a nervous traveller

The mesh window does a lot, but it is not the whole answer. A few things work alongside it. A non-slip base means the dog is not sliding every time you corner, which is its own kind of stress. Side flaps that wrap the doors mean no cold draught up the sides and no gap to fall into. And a familiar smell, his own blanket or an old shirt of mine, gives him something that says home.

I keep a worn towel on the cover for Banjo. It smells like the property. On the first trips with the mesh cover I left that towel there, and I reckon it did as much as the window to settle him. Gear helps, but the dog reads the whole picture.

A plain note before we go further: I am sharing what worked on my own dog and the dogs I have known. If your dog is genuinely distressed, drooling heavily, or has hurt itself trying to escape the car, that is a job for a vet or a qualified behaviourist, not a seat cover. The cover is comfort, not treatment.

Building up the miles slowly

You cannot fix a nervous dog with one big trip. I started Banjo on short runs, just down the drive and back, then to the gate, then into town for the servo. Each time the ute meant something good, a walk or a swim, not just the vet. Fifteen years on he still perks up at the sound of the keys, and I put a fair bit of that down to those early easy trips and a cover that let him see where we were headed.

If you have a pup or a rescue, do the same. Do not make the first long run a four-hour haul to a boarding kennel. Make the first run two minutes to the end of the street, with a treat at the end.

Sizing and the mesh panel

Most mesh-window covers follow the same sizing as a standard hammock. A rear bench takes around 147 by 137 centimetres. A wider wagon or SUV wants closer to 180 by 103 centimetres with the flaps down. The mesh panel itself is usually centred and sized so the dog can see over the front seat, not just sideways. When you are comparing, check that the mesh is actual mesh and not a clear plastic sheet, because plastic fogs up and traps heat, which makes a nervous dog worse, not better.

Weight ratings run from about 350 pounds on the cheaper soft hammocks up to 430 pounds on the hard-bottom styles. For a calm small dog the lower rating is fine. For a big, restless traveller, spend up for the higher one.

When the gear is not enough

I will be straight with you. A cover will not fix a dog with real, deep fear. Some dogs have been hurt in cars, or never socialised, and they need proper help. If the mesh window and the slow miles do not move the needle after a few weeks, or the dog is hurting itself, that is the point to call a professional. I am not too proud to say Banjo is easy compared to some, and the gear met him halfway. For a harder case, meet the dog with a vet.

Banjo reckons the ute is the best room in the house, and these days he is probably right. The mesh window started it, the slow trips finished it, and the cover keeps the seats clean while we work it out. That is about all a bloke can ask from a bit of gear.

Frequently asked questions

Do dogs actually get car anxiety?

From what I have seen, yes. Some dogs pant, pace, drool or refuse to get in. It is not worry in the human sense, but they clearly find the car stressful, often because they cannot see where they are going.

How does a mesh window help an anxious dog?

It lets the dog see into the cabin and out the windscreen, so the trip feels less like a dark box. Most dogs settle once they can read where they are headed.

Is a mesh cover a replacement for vet help?

No. It is comfort, not treatment. If a dog is seriously distressed or hurting itself, a vet or behaviourist should be the call. I am not a vet, just a dog owner with a few years behind me.

Should the window be mesh or clear plastic?

Mesh. Plastic fogs and traps heat, which makes a nervous dog worse. Mesh breathes and stays clear, so the dog keeps the view without the stuffiness.

What else calms a dog in the car?

A non-slip base so they are not sliding, side flaps against draughts, and a familiar blanket or old shirt for smell. Slow, short trips that end somewhere good build the habit.

What size mesh cover fits a standard seat?

Around 147 by 137 centimetres for a rear bench; closer to 180 by 103 centimetres for a wider SUV with flaps down. Measure before you buy.
