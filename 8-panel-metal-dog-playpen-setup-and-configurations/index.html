8-Panel Metal Dog Playpen: Setup & Configurations

By Sophie BennettAug 7, 2026Pet Playpens7 min read

Short answer: an 8-panel metal pen is the most flexible thing you can buy because you can reconfigure it into a square, a rectangle or an L in under a minute. Reconfiguration takes under a minute once the logic clicks, with no instruction sheet in the box to guide you. If you only want one takeaway, make it the section on the three shapes, since Wally stays safe at sixty centimetres and a mat under the feet keeps the rental's timber happy.

The first time I unboxed an 8 panel dog pen I stared at the pile of metal sections and wondered if I had ordered flat-pack furniture by mistake. There was no instruction sheet, just eight identical panels and a handful of clips. Once I worked out the logic, it clicked, and now I can reconfigure the whole thing in under a minute. If you have just bought one, or you are weighing it up against a soft style, this post walks through the setup and the shapes you can build.

I am still a Perth renter with Wally the Cavoodle and Miso the kitten, so my view is practical rather than showy. A powder coated dog pen is the tougher cousin of the soft octagonal one I use indoors, and it earns its keep outside and on camping trips. Let me show you how the panels actually work.

What comes in the box

Most eight-panel sets include the panels themselves, metal or plastic clips that hinge two panels together, and sometimes grounding stakes for outdoor use. The powder coating is the coloured finish you can see and feel; it protects the steel from rust, which is the thing that kills cheap pens near the salty Perth air. Check the corners for any chipped coating when you unpack, because a bare spot will rust first.

Set the panels on a flat surface and group the clips where you can reach them. I learnt to do this on the lawn rather than the lounge, because a dropped panel makes a racket and can mark a timber floor. Lay them in a line and you are ready to start connecting.

Basic setup, step by step

Stand two panels upright and slot a clip over the joined edge so they hinge. Add the next panel the same way, and keep going until all eight are linked in a loop or a row. The pen stands on its own feet, so you should not need tools for the standard build. Give each join a wiggle to confirm the clip is seated, because a loose panel is a gap a clever pup will find.

For an outdoor setup, push the stakes through the foot holes and into the ground so a gust does not tip it. At a caravan park I also weigh the feet with sandbags, since grass can be soft after rain. A stable base is what keeps the enclosure safe, far more than the height of the panels.

The three shapes you can build

The joy of eight separate panels is that one product becomes several layouts. Link them in a ring and you get an octagon; push four corners straighter and you get a square; open the loop and you get a long barrier. The table below shows what each shape is good for.

ConfigurationHow to build itBest use
OctagonJoin all 8 panels in an even ringOpen yard, maximum floor area, no sharp corners
SquareBend every second joint to 90 degreesTight patio or balcony, tidy footprint
BarrierLeave the loop open, anchor both endsBlock a doorway or verandah edge

I keep the octagon for the backyard because Wally gets the most room, and I switch to the barrier shape indoors to stop him bolting down the hall when the doorbell rings. Having one pen do both jobs is why I rate the format highly for the price.

Popular styles on the Australian market

When I was researching, a few names kept turning up as common examples people compare: i.Pet, PaWz and VAKA all sell eight-panel metal enclosures through local marketplaces. I am not affiliated with any of them and have not been sent products, so treat this only as context for what is out there, not a recommendation. What they share is the same panel-and-clip idea, with differences in coating colour, panel height and whether stakes are included.

The useful takeaway is that the concept is standard, so once you can set up one brand you can set up any of them. Look for a proper powder coat, panels around sixty centimetres for a small dog or taller for a jumper, and a clip style that feels secure when you test it in the shop. Prices for these kits in Australia generally land between about ninety-nine and a hundred and sixty dollars depending on height and finish.

Keeping it renter-friendly

Even a metal pen can suit a rental if you are careful. Lift it rather than drag it across timber, park a mat under the feet, and fold it flat into the car boot when you move. Because the panels separate, it stores in a slim stack against a wall, which passed my last inspection without a word. The soft octagonal pen is still my indoor favourite, but the metal one handles the jobs it cannot.

My setup tips after a year

Build it where it will live, not in the middle of the room and then shove it, because moving a full ring scratches the floor. Keep one spare clip in the drawer in case one goes missing. And if your dog learns to nose the clips open, a small carabiner through the join fixes it cheaply. A powder coated dog pen is a simple tool, but a few habits make it last for years.

Frequently Asked Questions

Frequently asked questions

Do I need tools to assemble an 8 panel dog pen?

Usually no. The panels clip together by hand with the supplied hinges, and you only need stakes or sandbags for outdoor stability. A screwdriver is rarely required for the standard build.

Can the same pen make a square and an octagon?

Yes. All eight panels join in a ring for an octagon, or you bend the joints to right angles for a square. Opening the ring turns it into a straight barrier as well.

Will the metal rust in coastal Perth?

A proper powder coated finish resists rust well, but any chipped spot can corrode near the sea. Touch up bare steel promptly and store the pen dry when not in use.

Is a powder coated dog pen safe on rental floors?

It is, with care. Lift rather than slide the panels, and place a mat under the feet so they do not mark timber. Fold it flat and store it before an inspection.

How tall should the panels be for a Cavoodle?

Around sixty centimetres suits most small breeds, but if your dog jumps, choose taller panels or add a roof. Wally stays in at sixty centimetres because he is not a climber.

Are i.Pet, PaWz and VAKA the only options?

No, they are just common Australian market examples I came across while researching. Many local sellers offer the same eight-panel design, so compare coating, height and what ships in the box.
