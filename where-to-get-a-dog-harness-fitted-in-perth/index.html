Where to get a dog harness fitted in Perth

By Maddie SorensenSep 2, 2026Dog Harnesses6 min read

The practical answer: to get a dog harness properly fitted in Perth, take your dog to a stockist that offers an in-person fitting service, measure at home first with a soft tape, and skip the mailbox brands that size your dog from a photo. As a mobile groomer and fitter working across the northern suburbs, I see the sore armpits that come from gear bought blind. A ten-minute fitting with the actual dog in the room beats a week of returns, and Perth has enough options if you know what to look for. The dog in the room is the only accurate sizing chart there is.

Why in-person fitting matters here

WA dogs come in every shape, from lean kelpies to barrel-chested bull breeds, and our heat means a bad fit rubs faster in summer. A fitter sees the dog move, checks the armpit clearance, and adjusts the sliders while the dog stands.

Online-only guessing misses that movement. A harness that looks right in a listing photo can twist the moment your dog trots, and by then the mark is already on the skin. In Perth's summer a twisted strap becomes a sore in days, not weeks, so the fitting pays for itself in avoided vet visits.

What a good fitting session includes

I treat a fitting like a groom prep: look, measure, try, move. If a store clips it on and sends you to the counter without watching the dog walk, that is not a fitting.

  • Measure girth and chest depth with the dog standing calmly.
  • Try the harness, then watch the dog walk a few steps indoors.
  • Check armpit clearance and that the straps do not twist.
  • Show you how to re-tighten after the first week of wear.

If any of those four is missing, you paid for a box, not a fit. The movement check is the one online shopping can never give you, and it is the step that catches most problems.

Perth options worth knowing

Several WA pet stores and independent stockists offer fitting, often free if you buy the harness there. Mobile groomers like me also fit during a visit, which suits renters without a car or those with anxious dogs who hate the shop.

Ask whether the fitter measures the dog or just reads the box. The ones who measure are the ones whose advice you can trust, and they will tell you when a style simply will not suit your breed. A good fitter also stocks a return policy, so a wrong call costs postage, not a wasted purchase in the cupboard.

Measuring at home first

Before any appointment, take your own readings so you can sanity-check the fitter. Soft tape around the girth behind the front legs, then chest depth from shoulders to breastbone, then a note on whether the front straps clear the armpit. Bring those numbers on your phone.

This does two things: it speeds the fitting and it tells you if the shop is guessing. If a fitter ignores your measurements and reaches for a label, that is a red flag. The dog's shape, not the breed name, should drive the size, and your home numbers are the proof.

Avoiding the mailbox guess

The brands that size from a photo or a breed dropdown are the ones I see most in the returns pile. A kelpie cross is not a kelpie, and a photo cannot show chest depth or armpit clearance. You end up with a harness that fits the breed on paper and rubs the dog in real life.

If you must buy online, use a stockist with a real sizing chart in centimetres and a free returns policy, and still do the home measure. Better yet, use a Perth stockist who will fit it and stands behind the sale. The few dollars saved by guessing is rarely worth a sore dog and a wasted harness.

After the fitting

A fitting is not a one-off. Webbing settles in the first week, so the snug harness you left with can loosen by the second walk. A good fitter shows you the two-finger rule and tells you to recheck at day seven, because that is when most rubs would start.

Keep the receipt and the fitter's name. If the dog marks despite a correct fit, a proper stockist will swap the style, not blame the dog. That aftercare is the real value of an in-person Perth fitting, and it is why I tell renters to find one local and keep them.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I get a harness fitted in Perth for free?

Often yes. Many WA pet stores and independent stockists fit the harness for free if you buy it there, and some mobile groomers include a fit during a home visit. The fitting should still involve measuring the dog and watching it walk, not just reading the box. A free fitting is only worth it if the fitter actually measures and moves the dog, so ask what the session includes.

How do I know the fitter is good?
