Best Back Seat Extenders for Large Dogs in Australia

By Mick O'DonnellAug 24, 2026Dog Car Seat Covers6 min read

Short answer: if your dog is bigger than a basketball, buy the hard-bottom extender and skip the sling. Big dogs need a flat floor to lie down on, and a sagging hammock drops them into the footwell on every corner and works their shoulders the whole drive. You'll want 600D Oxford cloth, PP cotton fill and an EVA or rubber base, rated between 350 and 430 pounds, with PU leather only for a dog that stays clean.

If your dog is bigger than a basketball, a soft hammock is a waste of good money. Banjo's a Blue Heeler, not a giant, but I've travelled with mates' retrievers and mastiff-crosses, and the rule's the same: a big dog needs a flat floor, not a sling that drops him into the footwell. That's what a back seat extender large dog setup gives you, and after fifteen years with working dogs I reckon it's the single best bit of gear you can bolt into the cabin.

This post is for the big breeds. The ones that don't fit on a bench cover, the ones that slide into the gap the moment you brake. I've pulled together the extenders that are actually rated for real weight — 350 to 430 pounds, about 160 to 195 kilos — with the dimensions that matter, and I've called it like I see it. No sponsor, just a station hand and a tape measure.

Why a large dog needs an extender

A normal car seat stops at the cushion edge and the footwell drops away. A medium dog can curl there. A large dog can't — he hangs half in, half out, and every corner slides him further. The extender lays a rigid board across that gap so the floor runs level from the back of the seat to the front of the footwell. Suddenly the dog has the whole rear to lie in, and a dog that can lie flat is a dog that sleeps, not a dog that paces.

The best back seat extender dogs Australia buyers can get also spreads the weight. A 40-kilo dog on a soft cover funnels all his load into one sagging point; on a hard board it's spread across the panel. The cover lasts longer and the dog rides level. Banjo reckons level is the only way to travel.

The top picks, up close

The PETICON is my daily. At 51.2 by 60.5 inches it covers the seat and the footwell, the mesh window lets Banjo see me and settle, and 400 pounds is well past a Heeler. If you've got a bigger breed or two dogs, the Laadd or Lamicall rated to 430 pounds is the one I'd point you at — that 160 to 195 kilo spread means a mastiff won't bow it.

The Dehlso is the value play. It's rated to around 37 kilos in the maker's test, sits near fifty-five dollars, and punches above its price. For a single large dog that isn't a giant, it's honest gear. The INSMEER 7-in-1 is the family option — machine-washable, which matters when the dog and the kids share the cabin.

The Pecute and BONTHEE leather unit is the flash one. Raised sides, wipe-clean PU, 53 by 40 inches. Lovely in the good car with a calm dog; I wouldn't trust it under Banjo's claws after a wet day on the property. And if your big dog rides in the boot of an SUV rather than the seat, the Bulldogology or Kurgo cargo liner with a bumper flap is the right call — different fit, same idea.

Materials that hold up

For a working dog, 600D Oxford cloth with a PP cotton fill and an EVA or rubber backing is the sweet spot. The Oxford takes the claws, the fill softens the ride, the backing grips the seat so the whole board stays put through a corner. The leather versions trade toughness for easy cleaning. Out here, where the dog is wet more often than dry, I'd take the Oxford every time and hose it off behind the shed.

Fit in the ute and the SUV

Most dual-cab utes take the hammock-style extender fine, clipped to the headrests. The wide boards are built with SUVs in mind, so measure your rear seat before you order — a board that's too wide won't seat flat and the dog will feel the ridge. Fold the board and check it stows behind the seat when the dog's not in the car. Banjo doesn't come out much, but the esky does, so I want it to pack away.

My pick for a big dog

If you want the best back seat extender dogs Australia has for a single large dog, the PETICON at 400 pounds is the safe bet and the one on my seat. For two big dogs or a giant breed, spend the same band on the Laadd or Lamicall at 430 pounds and you've got room and rating to spare. Skip the soft hammock, buy the hard floor, and the long drive into town gets quiet. Banjo's already asleep. Can't say fairer than that.

Frequently asked questions

What's the best back seat extender dogs Australia buyers can get for a large breed?

For a single large dog, the PETICON rated to 400 pounds at 51.2 by 60.5 inches is the safe pick. For two big dogs or a giant breed, the Laadd or Lamicall rated to 430 pounds gives the room and the rating to spare.

How much weight will a back seat extender large dog model hold?

The proper hard-bottom units run 350 to 430 pounds, about 160 to 195 kilos. That covers a single large dog or two medium-to-large dogs. Cheaper soft covers have no real rating and will sag under a big animal.

Will a large dog car seat cover fit a dual-cab ute?

The hammock-style extenders clip to the headrests and suit most dual-cab utes. The wide boards are built for SUVs, so measure your rear seat first and check the board folds for storage behind the seat.

Is a hard bottom worth the extra money over a hammock?

For a large dog, yes. The rigid board gives a flat floor so the dog can lie down, and it spreads his weight instead of sagging. A soft hammock drops a big dog into the footwell and stresses him on every corner.

Which material is toughest for a working dog?

600D Oxford cloth with PP cotton fill and an EVA or rubber backing. It takes claws, grips the seat, and hoses clean. The PU leather versions look smart but mark easily under a wet, muddy working dog.

Should a big dog ride in the seat or the boot?

Either works. Use a hard-bottom extender for the seat, or a cargo liner like the Bulldogology or Kurgo with a bumper flap for the boot. The boot keeps the dog clear of the cabin but needs a liner that covers the walls and the bumper.
