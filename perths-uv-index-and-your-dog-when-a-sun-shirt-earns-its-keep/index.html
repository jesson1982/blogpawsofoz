Perth's UV Index and Your Dog: When a Sun Shirt Ears Its Keep

By Maddie SorensenSep 3, 2026Pet Apparel4 min read

Quick answer: in Perth, where summer UV regularly hits extreme, a sun shirt earns its keep for pale, thin-coated, or scarred dogs whenever the index is 3 or above and they'll be outside for more than a few minutes. As a mobile groomer and fitter here, I see sunburn on dog bellies and noses every summer, and the shirt is the easiest fix for the torso. Pair it with shade and early walks, and it does real work rather than sitting in the cupboard.

Perth's UV is a different beast

Our city sits under some of the highest UV readings in the world, and the index climbs fast by late morning even when the air feels mild. Dogs on a midday beach walk cop reflected UV off the sand, which is why I fit so many shirts in December and January. Locals underestimate it because the breeze off the coast feels cool.

The risk isn't only beaches. A dog dozing in a north-facing backyard from 11 am gets cumulative exposure that shows up as pink skin by evening.

When I actually reach for a shirt

For a staffy or a whippet with thin fur, I recommend a shirt any day the UV index hits 3 plus and they'll be out beyond a toilet break. For thick double coats it's rarely needed, but a shaved summer clip changes that, because the under-skin is suddenly exposed.

  • Index 3 and rising: shirt on for pale or thin-coated dogs.
  • Beach and river days: always, with reflection off sand and water.
  • After a clip or surgery shave: cover the bare patch.
  • Backyard lounging past 11 am: shade first, shirt second.
  • Early walks under index 2: usually no shirt needed.

Fitting it for a Perth summer

A shirt that's too heavy turns a hot dog hotter, so I fit light, breathable UPF fabric that still covers the back and sides. The belly often stays bare, which is fine if the dog is in shade, but for full-sun beach time I add a belly band style. A good local fit means the shirt stays put when he shakes off sand.

Being mobile, I measure on the spot and adjust straps, because a slipping shirt rubs and ends up rejected by the dog.

Local habits that beat the shirt alone

Walk before 9 am or after 6 pm in summer, stick to shaded paths like the river trails, and keep a pop-up shade for the dog at any outdoor gathering. The shirt is the backup for when you can't avoid the sun, not the excuse to be out at noon.

A fitter's quick UV checklist

Before I fit a shirt, I check three things: the dog's coat type, the time he'll be out, and whether he'll be on reflective ground like sand or concrete. A thin-coated dog at the beach at noon needs full coverage, while a thick-coated companion on a shaded verandah needs almost nothing. The shirt is matched to the plan, not bought on a hunch.

I also remind owners that the UV index is published daily for Perth, and a glance each morning beats guessing. Above 3, I cover the pale ones; above 6, I keep outings short even with a shirt; and at 9 plus, we stay in except for a quick, shaded toilet break. Simple rules, fewer pink bellies.

If a dog has already burned, skip the shirt until the skin settles and ring the vet, because fabric over raw skin stings and can slow healing. Prevention is the whole point, so get the shirt on before the damage, not after the fact.

Frequently Asked Questions

What UV index means a dog needs a shirt?

Around 3 and above is when I'd cover a pale or thin-coated dog for any real outdoor time. Below that, a short walk is usually fine. The index is published daily, so a quick check each morning guides the call.

Do Perth beaches need a sun shirt?

For a light-coated dog, yes, because sand and water bounce UV back at them. A shirt plus a shade spot and fresh water makes a beach day safer, and I fit most of my summer shirts for exactly this reason.

Will a shirt make my dog too hot?

A heavy one might, which is why I fit light UPF fabric for Perth summers. Watch for panting, and remove it during breaks in shade. The shirt should shield sun, not trap heat, so breathability is the key buy.

My dog has a thick coat, does he need one?

Usually not on the body, his fur does the job, but a shaved summer clip or scarred patch changes that. Noses and ear tips still burn, so a shirt won't cover those, and vet advice on sunscreen helps there.

When's the safest time to walk in Perth summer?

Before 9 am or after 6 pm, when the UV is lower and the pavements have cooled. Midday walks in December to February are best skipped, as the heat and UV together are rough even for hardy local breeds.
