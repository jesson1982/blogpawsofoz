Where to Buy Calming Gear in Perth: What's on the Shelf and at What Price

By Maddie SorensenSep 1, 2026Calming & Anxiety6 min read

If you're in Perth and your dog dreads storm season, you don't have to order blind from overseas. I'm Maddie, a Perth groomer, and the calming gear you need is on shelves right across the metro, from the big chains through to small specialty shops. Prices run from about twelve dollars for a basic spray to ninety-plus for a premium collar system. Here's what I've seen on the shelf and what each tier is genuinely worth.

The big chain stores

The national pet chains and the big discount stores stock the familiar collar and diffuser brands. You'll find them near the behaviour aisle, usually with a small selection of sprays alongside. Stock is reliable and prices sit in the middle of the road, which suits most shoppers who just want a known name.

The upside is convenience and a decent returns policy, which matters when you're not sure whether your dog will tolerate a collar. The downside is limited choice. If your dog needs a specific format, the shelf might only carry the two biggest names, and the staff are often busy enough that a real conversation about fit isn't on the cards.

Parking is the underrated factor. Half my clients shop wherever they can get in and out quickly, and making a careful decision with a dog waiting in a hot car never goes well. Buy calming gear on a quiet weekday morning if you can manage it, because a rushed choice in a warm car is how people end up with the wrong format.

The specialty and independent shops

Around Perth, the smaller pet boutiques and quite a few vet clinics carry the mid-tier generics I rate highly. These places let you feel the strap quality and ask an actual question, which the self-serve chains generally can't offer. The price is often a touch lower too, because there's no national marketing spend baked in.

  • Independent pet shops for honest, unhurried advice.
  • Vet clinics for options they're comfortable recommending.
  • Produce and hardware stores for budget sprays.
  • Grooming salons sometimes retail a collar they trust.
  • Local markets occasionally carry refills at a discount.

Vet clinics and grooming salons

Clinics tend to stock a narrower range, generally the brands their vets are happy to stand behind, sometimes at a small premium. What you trade in choice you gain in confidence, because nobody's guessing whether the product is sound. As a groomer I'll sometimes sell the collar I've seen actually settle a dog on my table, which is a different kind of recommendation.

Salons and clinics also tend to remember you, so a second visit gets a proper follow-up on whether the collar faded or the fit slipped. That continuity is worth more than the few dollars you might save online, especially for a first-time buyer who isn't sure what they're looking at yet.

What the price tiers actually buy

At the budget end, around twelve to twenty-eight dollars, you get a basic spray or a thin strap collar that does the job for a one-off event. The mid tier, roughly thirty to forty-five, is where I'd point most owners: a real strap, a steady month of effect, and a fair return if it doesn't suit. The premium end, forty-five to ninety, buys water resistance, refills and a strap that survives a swimming dog.

The honest line is that you rarely need the top tier unless your dog lives in the water or wears one all year. For a Perth storm week, the middle shelf is the sweet spot, and the difference between a twenty-eight-dollar collar and a twelve-dollar one is the difference between it still working in February and it falling apart in January.

Timing your Perth shop

The mistake I see is shopping in December, when the shelves thin out and everyone wants the same diffuser. Stock up in October or November, before storm season builds, and you'll have the pick of the formats and the time to try one without panic. Perth's noise doesn't wait for the shops to restock, so the early bird genuinely gets the better collar here.

Frequently Asked Questions

Where's the cheapest place to buy calming gear in Perth?

The budget sprays turn up at hardware and produce stores for around twelve to twenty-five dollars, and markets sometimes have refills at a discount, but cheap isn't always the saving it looks like. For a collar that lasts the season, the independent pet shops and vet clinics across Perth often beat the chains on the mid tier once you factor in a strap that doesn't fail. Cheapest upfront can cost more in returns by February.

Should I buy from a vet clinic or a pet shop?

If you want the widest choice and to feel the product, the independent shops are great, while clinics stock a narrower range they're willing to stand behind, sometimes a little dearer. For a first-time buyer unsure what fits, the clinic's confidence and follow-up are worth the few dollars. Either way you're buying local in Perth, which beats guessing from an overseas listing with no returns.

When should I shop before storm season?

October or November, before the shelves thin and the rush hits in December. Stocking up early means you get the format you want and the time to try it without a storm breathing down your neck. Perth's storms build through late spring, so the owner who buys in October is set, and the one who waits till New Year's Eve is usually empty-handed or overcharged.

Can I get a collar fitted in store?

At the smaller shops and salons, yes, and I'd encourage it, because a two-finger gap and a trimmed tail are the difference between a collar that works and one that rubs or gets chewed. The big chains leave you to it, which is fine if you know the fit, but for a first collar ask someone to check it. A good fit also tells you faster whether the product is actually doing anything.

Is online cheaper than Perth shops?

Sometimes, but the saving shrinks once you add postage and the risk of a format that doesn't suit and can't be returned easily. For a known product you've used before, online is fine; for a first buy, the local shelf lets you feel the strap and ask, which is worth more than a few dollars. And a local return is a counter visit, not a parcel sent back across the country.
