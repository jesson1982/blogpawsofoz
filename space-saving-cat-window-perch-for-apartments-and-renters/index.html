Space-Saving Cat Window Perch for Apartments & Renters

By Sophie BennettJul 6, 2026Cat Window Perches7 min read

Short answer: a window-mounted hammock frees every centimetre of floor while giving a cat a proper sunny perch, which is why it suits a small flat better than a bulky tree. In my one-bedroom place near the Perth coast I had more windows than walking room, so mounting Miso's lookout meant no screw in the wall and no lost space for the dog. You'll want to mount it high enough that Wally cannot bump it, which keeps both animals calm and gives the cat a retreat he cannot reach.

When I first brought Miso home, I was living in a one-bedroom flat near the Perth coast with more windows than floor space. Like a lot of renters, I could not put a single screw or nail into the wall without risking my bond, and the idea of a bulky cat tree seemed daft in a place where every square metre already had a job to do. What I wanted was something that gave my rescue kitten a proper sunny perch without taking up a single centimetre of walking room. That is exactly where a window-mounted hammock changed the game for us.

I am not a vet and I do not pretend to be one. I am just a budget-conscious first-time pet parent who needed a practical solution that my landlord would never notice and my kitten would actually use. The short version is this: a suction-cup window perch is the most space-efficient cat bed I have found for apartment life, because it lives on the glass rather than on the carpet.

Why renters need a no-drill option

Most rental agreements in Western Australia are clear that you cannot make structural changes without written permission, and even a small bracket can leave a mark that comes out of your bond at the end of a lease. I have moved twice in three years, and each time the thought of patching holes made me tired before I started. A no-drill perch sidesteps the whole problem because it attaches with suction cups to an existing window. When it is time to leave, you lift it off, wipe the glass, and the place looks exactly as it did when you arrived.

There is a second, quieter benefit for renters: flexibility. In a flat, the best sun patch moves across the day, and with a window perch you are not committing to one spot forever. I started with the lounge-room window, then shifted Miso's perch to the bedroom window when I realised the morning light there suited her naps better. No tools, no drama, no trip to Bunnings for wall plugs I would never use again.

How a window perch saves floor space

The maths of a small flat is brutal. A traditional cat tree can eat a corner you needed for a laundry basket or a guest chair. A window hammock, by contrast, uses vertical space that was doing nothing — the pane of glass above your heater or behind the couch. For Miso, the bed is about 30 by 45 centimetres of soft mesh that folds flat against the window when she is not using it, so the room reads as tidy even when her perch is mounted.

I also found that lifting her bed off the floor kept it away from Wally, my Cavoodle puppy, who at first treated the kitten's ground beds as an invitation to play. Now her perch is somewhere he simply cannot reach, which has made their slow friendship a lot calmer. He can see her, she can watch him, and neither of them is in the other's face on the rug.

Living with the Perth coastal light

One thing I did not expect was how much the sea breeze matters. Our windows catch a soft afternoon wind off the Indian Ocean, and on warm days Miso flops onto her perch with all four legs dangling, clearly loving the moving air. The mesh of the hammock breathes, so she does not get that sticky, overheated feeling a padded basket gives her. In winter the same window becomes a heat trap of gentle sun, and she will spend an hour there watching the curtain shift.

I keep the blinds mostly open through the day. Renting a coastal flat means I paid for the light, so I figure I should use it, and Miso agrees. The perch turned what was just a sunny pane into a proper little lounge for her, and it cost me nothing in floor space or bond risk.

Choosing a perch that suits a small home

Not every window perch is equal for apartment use. I look for a model with a low profile that does not stick out far from the glass, because in a narrow hallway even a ten-centimetre protrusion matters. Twin suction cups are plenty for a small or medium cat, and the bed should be removable for the washing machine. Our 1688-sourced hammock ticks those boxes and, honestly, the price was what let me try it without overthinking.

If you share your flat with a dog, as I do, mounting height is worth a thought. I put Miso's perch high enough that Wally cannot bump it by jumping, but low enough that I can lift her down easily. That balance took one move to get right, and now it is set.

A quick word on honesty: our standard hammock is suited to small and medium cats up to about 15 kg. If your cat is a big solid breed pushing past that, do not force it onto a twin-suction perch — a reinforced multi-suction or steel-cable model is the safer call, and I would rather tell you that than have you pick up a nervous cat off the floor.

Set-up tips for a tidy flat

I clean the window with a bit of rubbing alcohol before the first mount, then press each suction cup firmly and give it a gentle tug to be sure. The whole job takes two minutes and needs no measure or level. Once it is on, I check it weekly by pushing lightly on the frame, which takes about three seconds and has become part of my Saturday tidy-up.

For a small home, the win is not just space but calm. Miso has a spot that is hers, Wally has the floor, and I have a lounge room that still looks like a lounge room. That is the renter dream, really: a happy cat and a bond you get back in full.

Frequently asked questions

Frequently asked questions

Will a window perch damage my rental walls?

No. A suction-cup perch attaches to the glass, not the wall, so there are no holes, screws, or adhesive marks. When you move out, lift it off and wipe the window, and your bond should be fine.

Is a window hammock safe for a small apartment?

Yes, provided you mount it to clean, flat glass and check the suction weekly. It uses no floor space and sits flush to the window, which suits narrow rooms and hallways.

My flat is tiny — will the perch stick out too far?

Our hammock has a low profile and protrudes only slightly from the pane, so it rarely gets in the way. If your hallway is very narrow, choose a window that is not on the main walkway.

Can I use it if I have a dog as well as a cat?

I do. I mount the perch high enough that my Cavoodle cannot bump it, which keeps both animals calm and gives the cat a retreat the dog cannot reach.

What size cat is the standard hammock for?

It suits small and medium cats up to about 15 kg. For a large cat above that weight, choose a reinforced 4-suction or steel-cable model rather than our standard perch.

Does it work on double-glazed rental windows?

Generally yes, as long as the surface is smooth, clean, and flat. Avoid textured or frosted glass, which will not hold suction well.
