Two Dogs, One Back Seat: Setting Up for a Long Run

By Tom RileySep 1, 2026Dog Car Seat Covers8 min read

Short answer: Two dogs in one back seat need two separate footprints, not one shared hammock. I lay a divided cover across the bench, give each dog its own mat and its own anchor point, and put a firm centre line between them. That is what keeps them off each other for eight hours and still lets me clean the whole seat at a rest stop.

Scout is a Border Collie who has run about twenty thousand kilometres a year with me since he was a pup, most of it on Top End dirt. Last dry season I added a second dog: my brother's kelpie, Rusty, for a three-week loop from Darwin out past Katherine. Two working dogs, one bench, thirty-eight degrees in the shade. The first day was rubbish. Rusty stood on Scout's head for four hours, the cover slid into the seat gap, and both dogs finished up damp and filthy. So I rebuilt the back seat around the pair of them.

Two dogs need two footprints

A single back seat is one shape, and two dogs are not. My wagon's bench measures about 1.4 metres across the squab, which sounds generous until two 20 kg dogs, a cover and two harnesses are on it. Scout is long and low and likes to lie across the middle. Rusty is taller and wants his shoulder against something solid. Leave them on an open bench and they spend the day shifting instead of settling.

What fixed it was giving each dog a defined rectangle. Not one wide hammock with a vague middle, but two clear footprints with a physical edge between them. Scout settles the moment he can feel where his space ends. Rusty settles when he has a wall to lean on. If your pair are still shuffling after an hour on the road, the split is wrong, not the dogs.

Splitting the bench down the middle

Start with the cover, because it sets the floor. I lay a wide cover over the bench so a fold line falls between the two positions, then tuck the front edge into the seat gap so it cannot creep forward. The outer headrest straps take the load and the centre one stays free. The divider is a firm foam bolster rolled into the cover, about 15 cm high, enough to give each dog an edge without fencing anyone in. A rigid divider between the outer headrests works too, but you lose the middle for lying flat.

Then one mat each on top. Two cheap mats, one per dog, do more for settling than any single oversized bed. Each dog keeps a familiar texture underfoot, and when one mat gets wet or sandy I swap that mat instead of the cover.

Keeping them off each other in motion

Most of the standing-on-top behaviour I see is uncertainty rather than dominance. A dog that cannot predict where the other one will be keeps getting up to check.

The routine is boring and fixed. At every stop I take them out one at a time, on separate leads, in the same order. Scout out, Scout back in, then Rusty out, Rusty back in. Nobody meets anybody at an open door, and the dog already loaded gets his chew while the other one has a turn. I stop every two hours on a long day, and both dogs get water and a walk. A pair that has had a proper break will lie down for the next leg. A pair held in the car for five hours will not.

The cover has to do the hard work

Two dogs do roughly twice the damage of one. Nails, drool, dust, the odd accident, and a wet dog climbing back in after a swim. A cover for two needs four things: full coverage of the bench plus the backs of the front seats, a waterproof backing, a grippy base, and straps that do not stretch after a month.

I go through more covers than anyone I know, so I keep coming back to the shapes set out in our car seat covers guides. What I look for now is a cover wide enough to fold up at the sides, because those flaps are what stop the door cards getting scratched. Seams matter more than fabric. A seam down the middle will leak onto the seat under a wet dog every time.

  • Two defined footprints beat one wide shared hammock with a soft middle.
  • A firm centre bolster about 15 cm high gives each dog a settled edge.
  • One mat per dog, so a wet or sandy mat gets swapped on its own.
  • Load and unload one dog at a time, in the same order every stop.
  • Full side flaps stop door cards and seat edges taking nail damage.

Water, dirt and the six-minute reset

At the end of a driving day I want the back seat back to something I can sit on, and I want it done before dinner.

The reset runs in the same order every time. Both dogs out and tied off away from the car. Cover unclipped and shaken out downwind. Mats shaken and hung. Seats wiped under the cover with a damp cloth, because dust works through every seam. Cover back on, folded the same way, ready for the morning. Dust is the thing that beats you here, not mud. On a long trip I carry a spare cover folded flat, so if one dog rolls in something awful at four in the afternoon, the spare goes on and the dirty one waits in a bag until we hit a laundry.

What I would not do again

A few things went in the bin after the first two-dog trip. One shared tether across the middle was the worst of them. The dogs crossed over each other within ten minutes and spent the rest of the leg tangled, so it is two anchors now, one per dog, on separate points.

One giant shared bed went next. It bunched into the seat gap and both dogs tried to own the same square of it. Water bowls on the seat did not last either, because they tip on the first corner, so water happens at stops, on the ground, out of the esky. The last thing I stopped doing is unclipping both dogs at once at a busy roadhouse. Two dogs, one open door and a road full of caravans is how you lose one.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can two dogs share one hammock cover?

They can share the cover but not the space. I run one cover across the whole bench and then divide it, so both dogs stand on the same waterproof layer. What I would not do is leave them on one open hammock with no edge between them. Two dogs on an undivided surface drift into each other all day, and the dog that gives ground first never settles.

Do both dogs need their own anchor point?

Yes, and on separate points. One tether shared between two dogs crosses itself the moment one of them turns around. I use two harnesses and two anchor points, one on each outer side, with the tethers short enough that neither dog can reach the other's line. Ask your vet if your dog has a neck or back issue, because not every harness suits every dog.

How do I stop the cover sliding forward with two dogs on it?

Tuck the front edge deep into the seat gap rather than trusting the straps alone. Two dogs shifting their weight will walk a smooth cover forward within a couple of hours. I refit the cover at the midday stop, because a cover that has crept 10 cm is already letting dust in.

Is it better to fold the back seat down for two dogs?

For some wagons and some pairs, yes. A folded rear seat gives two large dogs far more room and a cargo barrier keeps them out of the front. The trade-offs are heat and dust, because the cargo area gets less cabin airflow, and the dogs have to climb in over the tailgate. Ask your vet about heat and older dogs before you decide.

How often do you stop with two dogs aboard?

Every two hours on a long day, and sooner in the build-up when it is hot and humid. Each stop is a proper one: out on separate leads, water, a walk, then back in one at a time. Three ten-minute stops do more for a pair of dogs than one forty-minute break.
