Our senior dog's bed rotation: indoors, caravan and tiled floors

By Sophie BennettSep 4, 2026Beds & Mats5 min read

Short answer: rotating our senior dog's bed across a few spots in the house, the caravan and the cool tiled floor has been one of the cheapest changes we made for her comfort. At twelve years old she stiffens up overnight, so giving her a warm, supportive bed in the lounge and a cooler spot on the tiles during the day means she settles instead of pacing. The trick is keeping the same familiar bed in each place rather than swapping textures on her, because the scent and feel of her own bed is what lets her switch off.

Why we started rotating instead of buying one big bed

Our rental in Perth has polished concrete through the living area and carpet in the bedrooms. In summer the tiles stay cool and our old cattle dog cross loves them; in winter she wants warmth and a wall at her back. One bed could not do both jobs, and a giant orthopaedic mattress would have cost more than we wanted to spend as renters who may move again.

So we bought two smaller beds and a lighter travel mat, then placed them where she already chose to sleep. Rotation meant meeting her where she was, not forcing her onto one expensive bed she might ignore. It also spread the cost across the year instead of one scary receipt, which suited our budget far better than a single premium purchase.

The three spots that actually get used

We keep a supportive foam bed in the lounge near the heater, a thin washable mat on the tiled kitchen floor for afternoon naps, and a fold-flat cot in the caravan for trips down south. Each spot serves a different need through the day and through the seasons, and none of them fights the others for space in a compact home.

  • Lounge bed: warm, raised edge, used at night and on cold mornings.
  • Tile mat: flat and cool, used after walks in the Perth heat.
  • Caravan cot: off the ground, used for overnight stays away from home.

Caravan and tiled floors need different support

On the tiles she stretches out flat, so a thin mat is fine because the floor does the cooling. In the caravan the floor can get cold and damp, so the raised cot keeps her off the ground and stops that morning stiffness we used to see after a night away. The lounge bed is the only one with real foam, because that is where she spends the longest stretch asleep.

The caravan also moves, so a bed that slides is a nuisance. The cot's feet grip the lino and it does not wander, which matters when you are parked on a slight slope at a coastal site. A flat mat would have slid into the door well by morning, so the shape earned its place on trips.

What we learned about our old girl's habits

Watching where she lies told us more than any label on a box. She picks the tiles when she is warm, the lounge when she is cold, and the caravan cot because it smells like her. Keeping her own bed in each place means the scent travels with her, which settled her quicker than a new bed ever did, and it cut the restless sighing we used to hear at night.

Keeping it simple as a renter

None of our beds are bolted down or permanent, which matters when the lease ends. They are all machine washable or have removable covers, and the cot folds flat behind the laundry door. If you are renting with a senior dog, start with the spot they already love and add from there, because a bed they ignore is just expensive floor clutter in a small place.

You can compare the shapes that suit different dogs in our Beds and Mats hub before you buy, and I would start with one good foam bed before adding the cooling options.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need a different bed for every room?

Not at all. Start with the one or two spots your dog already chooses, then add only where there is a clear gap, like a cold caravan floor. We use three because our old girl has very different needs in summer and winter, but a younger dog may be happy with one good bed and a cool tile option, so match the count to the dog.

Will rotating beds confuse a senior dog?

It did not confuse ours, because we kept the same bed in each place rather than moving a single bed around. The familiar scent and texture in each spot became a cue for that activity, so she learned the lounge bed means sleep and the tile mat means a cool rest after a walk, and the routine settled her rather than unsettling her.

How do I stop a bed sliding on polished concrete?

Look for a non slip base or place a rubber mat underneath. Our lounge bed came with a grippy bottom that held on the tiles, but the cheap one we tried first slid every time she stood up, which annoyed her and us. A liner from the discount shop fixed it for a few dollars and saved the floor from scratches at inspection.

Is a raised cot worth it for caravan trips?

For us it was. The caravan floor gets cold and a little damp near the coast, and lifting our dog off it stopped the stiff morning rise we used to see after a night away. It also folds flat, which matters in a small van. A thin mat would have left her on the cold ground and we would have been back to square one.

Can I do this on a tight budget?

Yes. Our tile mat was the cheapest item and gets the most summer use, and the lounge foam bed was the only real spend. Watch the op shops and end of season sales, and prioritise washable covers so the beds last. A renter does not need three premium beds to make rotation work, just the right ones in the right spots.
