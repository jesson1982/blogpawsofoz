Flooring Under a Pen: Slips, Joints and What to Use

By Hannah WrightSep 5, 2026Pet Playpens8 min read

Short answer: Put something with grip under the pen, not just under the pet. A rubber backed mat or a tight weave rug stops the panels walking across tiles, gives a puppy something to push against, and gives an older dog a surface to rise from. Loose towels and flattened cardboard are the two things I would not use.

A family brought a nine week old border collie in for his first vaccination and mentioned, almost as an aside, that the pen in the laundry kept moving. They had zip tied the panels together, weighted one corner with a toolbox and still found the run shifted against the wall each morning. I asked what was under it. Tiles, they said. Nothing else. They had bought a pen mat weeks earlier and left it in the boot of the car. That is the conversation I have most weeks, and it is nearly always about the floor rather than the pen.

Why the floor decides the setup

A pen is a frame, not a surface. The panels hold the shape and the floor does the work, and the floor is the part buyers think about last.

Two things happen on a hard, smooth floor. The pen moves, because the panels are light and have small feet, so a puppy pushing against the side shifts the whole run across a tiled room within a day. And the puppy's feet move differently, because there is nothing to grip, so every turn becomes a small slide.

What that means for a dog's joints over years is a vet's area, not mine. What I can say from handling dogs on clinic floors is that grip makes a puppy easier to move and an older dog easier to lift, and a pen that stays put is a pen you can leave the room from.

Tiles, timber and the sliding problem

Gloss tile is the worst surface in most Australian homes. It is hard, smooth and often slightly polished, and a pen with thin metal feet will skate on it.

Timber boards are better, but not by much once they are sealed and polished. Floating floors move as well, so a pen on top can slide as a unit and take a rug with it. Vinyl plank behaves like tile with a little more give.

Carpet is the easiest floor, because the pile holds the panels and absorbs some noise. It is also the hardest to clean, which matters in a pen where a puppy is still learning. Concrete is usually grippy enough unless it has been sealed, and sealed concrete behaves like tile. The fix is the same on all of them: something between the pen and the floor that grips both.

Mat movement and the pen's footprint

A mat only helps if it stays where you put it, and a light mat under a light pen will travel with the pen.

Rubber backed mats stay put because the underside grips the floor and the weight is spread across the footprint. A loose bath mat will bunch at the first corner a puppy digs into, and then you have a trip hazard inside the pen as well.

Size matters as much as material. The mat should be at least as big as the pen footprint, ideally a little bigger, so the panels sit on the mat rather than the join. A mat that covers only the middle leaves the corners on bare tile, which is exactly where a puppy turns. If the pen itself is light, the pet playpens range includes heavier panel sets. For a soft pen, weight the corners rather than the panels.

  • Put a rubber backed mat under the whole pen, corners included, not just the middle.
  • Buy or cut the mat slightly larger than the pen footprint.
  • Weight the corners of a soft pen instead of the panels.
  • Leave part of the pen off the mat so a warm puppy can choose a cool patch.
  • Lift the mat every couple of weeks and check what is underneath.

Grip for a puppy that is still growing

Puppies slip for reasons that are not about the floor, including loose joints and a body growing faster than their coordination. No mat fixes that. A mat gives them one less thing to manage and stops the pen becoming a skating rink.

What I look for is a tight weave or a rubber texture a claw can catch on without the foot sinking. Deep pile carpet is soft but moves under a foot, and a loose knit rug will ruck up. A short pile mat or a rubber backed runner is the better compromise.

Bare tile in a hot climate has one real advantage: it is cool, and an overheated puppy will choose it over a bed every time. That is why I cover part of the pen rather than all of it. If a puppy slips constantly on a surface that has grip, or is reluctant to walk on hard floors, raise it with your vet.

What to put down and what to avoid

Good options, roughly in order of how well they stay put: a rubber backed utility mat in a short pile, which is cheap, washable and stays flat; interlocking foam tiles, which give grip and cushion but are easy to chew; a tight weave cotton rug over a rubber anti slip underlay; and a purpose made pen mat with bound edges, which is the tidiest and the most expensive.

What I avoid is just as specific. Loose towels ruck and slide. Newspaper is slippery once it is damp. Cardboard goes soft, tears and gets eaten. Carpet offcuts with no backing move and fray, and the strands end up in a puppy's mouth.

Polished floor plus a loose rug is the combination most likely to end with a sliding pen and a puppy on their side.

Cleaning without wrecking the grip

A mat that has lost its grip is a mat that needs replacing, and how you clean it decides how long that takes.

Rubber backed mats can be hosed and hung, but not left in sun for days, because the backing goes brittle. Foam tiles wipe clean and should not be soaked, since water gets between the joins. Fabric mats go in the machine on a warm cycle with plain detergent.

For accidents, blot first, use an enzyme cleaner, then let the surface dry completely before the pen goes back. A damp mat under a pen is the fastest way to get a smell that will not leave the room. Lift the mat every couple of weeks and look underneath, because that is where the surprises are.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need a mat if the pen is on carpet?

Usually not for grip, since the pile holds the panels well enough. The reason to add one is cleaning, because a washable mat over carpet saves the carpet during toilet training. A cheap rubber backed mat does that job, and it is worth it if the carpet is new or the rental inspection is close.

Will a yoga mat work under a pen?

It grips well and it is cheap, so it is a reasonable temporary answer. The problems are thickness and edges. A thin foam mat compresses under panel feet, the rolled edge is a chew target, and a puppy claw goes through the surface. Expect to replace it within a month or two.

How do I stop the pen moving on tiles?

Start with a mat that covers the full footprint, because most sliding comes from bare panel feet on a polished surface. If it still creeps, put the pen against a wall on one side and weight the freestanding corners. Cable ties keep the shape but do not stop movement across the floor.

Is newspaper bad for a puppy in a pen?

It is not dangerous, but it is a poor floor. Newspaper slides on tile, tears when damp and soaks through quickly, so a puppy ends up standing on a wet surface. Puppy pads behave better because the backing grips. Neither replaces a surface with grip under the whole pen.

How do I clean up accidents without lifting the mat?

You cannot, really. Blot what is on top, lift the mat, clean the floor underneath and let both dry fully before the pen goes back. A mat that stays damp underneath will smell within a day, and a puppy will keep using the same spot. Two mats in rotation makes the drying time easier.
