Renters' Guide to Non-Permanent Pet Pens

By Sophie BennettJul 25, 2026Pet Playpens8 min read

Short answer: pick a foldable soft pen you can put up and take down with no drilling, because your bond depends on leaving the flat exactly as you found it. Mine runs one animal at a time, giving Miso and Wally their own bedding, and medium suits most apartments while large fits a bed and a litter tray together. The truth is the non-permanent style tucks behind a sofa or into a linen cupboard, so the inspection rolls around and the walls show no screw holes.

When I first moved into my little rental near the Perth coast, I had one rescue kitten in a carrier and a Cavoodle puppy on the way, and absolutely no idea how I was going to keep both of them safe without upsetting my property manager. The lease said no alterations, the bond was a scary chunk of money, and the last thing I wanted was a wall full of screw holes or a carpet ruined by a puppy who had not yet learned where the toilet was. That is when I started looking properly at a renters pet pen that I could put up, take down and tuck away without leaving a single mark behind.

Why renting changes the way you set up a pen

The biggest difference between owning a home and renting a flat is that you are always a guest in the place. Anything permanent you attach to the wall, the skirting board or the balcony becomes the landlord's problem the moment you leave, and in Western Australia that can come straight out of your bond. I learned this the hard way when a previous flatmate mounted a cat tree with eight heavy-duty brackets and we spent a tense afternoon patching plaster before the final inspection.

So when it came to Wally the puppy and Miso the kitten, I made a rule for myself: if a pen needs a drill, a screw or an adhesive that promises to be "removable" but probably is not, it does not come into my home. A good indoor dog pen for a rental should stand on its own, weigh enough to stay put, and fold flat enough to slide behind the sofa when visitors arrive. That single idea shaped every choice I made, and it is the heart of this guide.

What "non-permanent" actually means

Non-permanent does not mean flimsy. It means the structure holds itself up through its own weight and clever design rather than through fixing it to the building. The soft octagonal pens I use have a steel frame inside a padded fabric shell, with a zipped door and a mesh top that keeps Miso from treating the curtain rail as a climbing frame. Because it is freestanding, I can move it from the tiled kitchen area to the sunny lounge room depending on the time of day, then fold it down to a disc about the size of a camping chair when we head up to the caravan for the weekend.

For a puppy who is still learning the rules of the house, a freestanding indoor dog pen gives you a safe zone without committing to a permanent baby gate screwed across a doorway. I can set it up in the evening while I cook dinner, then pack it away before bed so the lounge room feels like a lounge room again. My favourite part is that there is nothing to undo when I move out, which in this rental market feels like a small miracle.

Setting up without losing your bond

If you do need a little extra grip on a polished floor, skip the tape and the screws. I use a few offcuts of non-slip rug underlay beneath the base of the pen, which stops it sliding on our tiles and peels up with no residue. For balconies and verandahs, a few small plant pots filled with sand sit against the outside of the panels as gentle weights, and they double as a herb garden in summer. None of these tricks attach to the building, so my property manager has never had a thing to complain about.

Another habit that saved me early on was taking a quick photo of each room the day I moved the pen in, and again the day before inspection. It sounds fussy, but having dated pictures of "no damage here" takes the stress out of the final walk-through. When you are a first-time pet parent and a first-time renter at the same time, you take your peace of mind where you can find it.

Living with two species in a small flat

Miso and Wally have very different needs, and a single pen does not solve everything. The kitten likes height and a quiet corner; the puppy wants space to flop and a clear view of me so he does not howl. I rotate the pen between them rather than trying to contain both at once, and I keep a cosy covered bed inside for Miso and a washable mat for Wally. Because the soft pen folds away, I am not sacrificing half my lounge room permanently, which keeps the flat feeling like a home rather than a kennel.

There is also the simple matter of guests. My mum visits from up north a few times a year, and she is not a dog person. Being able to fold the indoor dog pen down and tuck it behind the laundry door in thirty seconds means I can host without the place looking like a pet shop. That flexibility is, for me, the whole point of choosing non-permanent gear in the first place.

Taking the pen on the road

The reason I fell for the fold-flat design is that it goes where I go. On coastal camping weekends, the pen becomes Wally's safe spot outside the caravan, and inside it Miso has a familiar-smelling den that stops her bolting the moment a neighbouring campsite fires up the barbecue. It means I can relax by the water instead of spending the trip chasing two animals in opposite directions. For a budget-conscious renter, one item that does double duty at home and on holiday is worth far more than its price tag.

If you are staring at a lease and a new pet and wondering how to make it all work, my advice is simple: choose gear that leaves no trace. The right renters pet pen lets you be a responsible pet parent and a model tenant at the same time, and that is a combination worth getting right from day one.

Frequently asked questions

Can I use a soft pet pen in a rental without permission?

Freestanding, non-fixing pens are generally fine because they leave no alteration to the property, but it is always worth a quick word with your property manager so everyone is comfortable. Keep your bond protection photos just in case.

Will an indoor dog pen damage timber floors?

Not if you use non-slip underlay rather than tape or screws. The soft base spreads the weight and the underlay protects the finish, which is exactly what a rental-friendly setup should do.

Is a foldable pen sturdy enough for a puppy?

For a small breed like a Cavoodle, yes. Weight the base if you are on tiles, supervise at first, and choose a size that lets the puppy stand, turn and lie down comfortably.

How much space does a pen take when stored?

The soft octagonal style folds to a flat disc roughly the thickness of a folded doona, so it slides behind a sofa or into a linen cupboard without fuss.

Can I use the same pen for a cat and a dog?

You can rotate it between pets, but avoid housing them together unsupervised. I use mine for one animal at a time and give each their own bedding inside.

What size should a renter buy first?

Medium suits most apartments and a small dog or a cat with a bed, while large is better if you want both a bed and a litter tray or a puppy toilet inside. Start with the size your space allows.
