Where to buy a quality dog bed in Perth (and what to ask)

By Maddie SorensenSep 4, 2026Beds & Mats5 min read

Short answer: in Perth you can buy a decent dog bed from the big pet chains, a handful of independent pet stores, and weekend markets, but quality varies wildly, so ask before you pay. The questions that matter are about foam density, a washable cover, the weight rating, the return policy and who actually makes it. A shop that answers clearly is worth the extra few dollars over a mystery bed in a discount bin, and the five minutes of questions will save you the months of regret a flat bed brings.

Where Perth shoppers look

The major chains have the widest range and easy returns, which suits most people. Independent stores and local makers often stock denser foam and tougher covers, and the staff usually know the product. Markets and op shops are great for a cheap spare, but check the fill and the smell before you carry it home, because a bargain that smells of someone else's dog is no bargain at all once it is in your lounge.

The five questions to ask

Before money changes hands, ask what the foam density is, whether the cover comes off, what weight the bed is rated for, what the return policy is, and where the bed is made. A confident answer on all five signals a bed built to last; hedging on the foam is a red flag, and a shop that cannot tell you who made it is a shop I would walk out of with my wallet still shut.

  • Foam density: higher lasts longer under weight.
  • Cover: must zip off and wash.
  • Rating: match it to your dog's kg.
  • Returns: keep the receipt, know the window.
  • Maker: local or mystery import.

Reading the label like a local

Perth heat means washability matters more than elsewhere, so a fixed cover is a pass for me. Check the zip is real and the seam is tight, because our laundry gets a workout with beach sand and river mud. A bed that survives a Perth dog's life is the one you can hose or machine without fear, and the label should tell you which, not hide behind a photo of a sleeping labrador.

What I tell my grooming clients

I send people to a chain for the cot and a local maker for the orthopaedic bed, because the locals tend to use better foam. Either way, feel the bed in store and press the foam; if it stays dented, walk away. The floor of the shop tells you more than the front of the box, and a dent that holds under your thumb will hold under your dog within a month.

Avoiding the dud

Skip beds with no density, no zip and no returns, no matter the photo. The cheap bin specials look fine until they flatten in a month, and by then the receipt is gone. Spend where the dog sleeps most, and save on the spare, and you will rarely buy twice, which is the only real saving a dog owner ever gets from a bed.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which Perth store has the best beds?

There is no single winner. Chains give range and returns, local makers give better foam and covers, and markets give cheap spares. I tell clients to buy the main bed where the staff can answer the foam and cover questions, then grab the spare anywhere. Match the shop to the job, and you will leave with the right bed, not just a receipt.

What is the one question I must ask?

Ask whether the cover comes off and washes. In Perth heat and with our dogs' beach habits, a fixed cover is a bed that will smell and fail. If the shop cannot show you a zip, that bed is not for a real dog's life, no matter how soft it feels in the showroom, and the smell will find you by week two.

Are market dog beds safe to buy?

Often yes for a spare, but check them hard. Sniff for old accidents, feel for flat or clumped foam, and make sure the filling is not a choking risk for a chewer. A clean, firm market bed at a few dollars is a win; a mystery stained one is not worth the carry home, and a chewed filling is a vet trip waiting to happen.

Should I buy local or imported?

Local makers tend to use denser foam and stand behind it, which I like for a main bed. Imports are fine if the specs are clear and returns are possible, but some hide the density and the maker. Ask who built it; a real answer beats a pretty tag every time, and a maker who will talk to you is a maker who backs the bed.

How do I know the bed will last?

Press the foam and watch it spring back, check the zip and seam, and keep the receipt for the return window. A bed that dents and stays dented will flatten fast, so the in store squeeze is your best test. Pair that with a washable cover and it should see out the year, and the receipt is your insurance if it does not.

If you are shopping for an older dog, say so in the store, because the orthopaedic range is often at the back and the staff may not think to point to it. A quick sentence about your dog's age and weight gets you to the dense foam faster, and it tells the shop you are buying for comfort, not just for a photo on the wall that caught your eye.
