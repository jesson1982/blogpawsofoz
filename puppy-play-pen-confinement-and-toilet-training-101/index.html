Puppy Play Pen: Confinement & Toilet Training 101

By Sophie BennettJul 26, 2026Pet Playpens7 min read

Short answer: use the pen for safety and toilet training together, not as punishment, because a contained puppy learns the routine faster than one roaming free. Beside my bed he slept in his pen at ten weeks, which meant I heard him stir for late toilet trips and my carpet survived the chaos. In short, the same corner held a chew toy while I finished the washing, and slow meet-ups with Miso through the mesh let them learn each other at a calm pace.

Bringing Wally home was the loveliest chaos I have known. One minute he was a butterball of caramel fluff, the next he had wee on my rug and was chewing the leg of the couch. I had read that toilet training takes patience, but nobody told me how much of that patience is just about not letting the puppy roam free before he is ready. A puppy play pen became the tool that saved my carpet and my sanity, and in this post I want to show you the routine that worked for us in a small Perth flat.

I will be straight with you: I am a first-time dog mum, I rent, and I am on a budget. I could not build a fancy puppy room or lay down expensive flooring. What I needed was a safe, cosy space Wally could call his own, where accidents were contained and where I could teach him that outside is for toilets. That is exactly what a pen gave us, and it cost less than a single vet visit.

Confinement is safety, not punishment

The first mindset shift is the big one. When you shut a puppy in a pen, it is not a time-out for being bad. Dogs are den animals by instinct; a small enclosed space feels secure to them, the same way a crate or a bed tucked in a corner feels safe. Wally actually chooses to nap in his pen now, door open, because it smells like him and the world is quiet there.

Used as a toilet training pen, the enclosure simply stops the puppy from wandering off to a hidden corner of the flat to do his business. Puppies do not want to soil their bed, so if the pen is sized right, with a sleeping spot at one end and a wee mat at the other, he learns to hold on and signal when he wants out. That is the whole trick, and it is gentle rather than harsh.

Setting up Wally's corner

I put the soft octagonal pen in the living room against a wall, so two sides were already bordered and it felt snug. Inside I laid a washable mat, added his bed in the back corner, a water bowl clipped to the side, and a wee pad near the door end. I kept his favourite chew toy in there so the pen meant good things, not isolation.

For a renter, the best part is that nothing is fixed. When the real estate agent booked an inspection, I folded the whole thing flat, slid it behind the laundry door, and the flat looked showroom tidy in two minutes. A puppy toilet training pen that you can disappear on demand is worth its weight in bond money.

Our daily routine and schedule

Consistency is what turns a puppy into a housetrained dog. I took Wally out the moment he woke, straight after meals, after play, and every hour or so in between. The pen kept him contained between those trips so he could not sneak off. Here is the rough schedule we ran at ten to twelve weeks.

Time of dayPen timeToilet trip
6:30 am wakeOut of pen, straight outsideCarry to grass, praise when he goes
7:00 am breakfastBack in pen to digestOutside 10 min after eating
9:00 amFree play, then pen restOutside every hour
12:00 pm lunchPen with chew toyOutside 10 min after eating
3:00 pmShort free time, then penOutside every hour
6:00 pm dinnerPen to settleOutside 10 min after eating
9:30 pmLast toilet, then bed in penQuiet night, no play

The rhythm matters more than the clock. Puppies have tiny bladders, so the hourly trips outside are what build the habit. If he went inside the pen, I cleaned it without fuss and took him out sooner next time. Getting cross does not teach a puppy anything; a calm routine does.

Troubleshooting the accidents

Wally had maybe three real accidents in the first fortnight, all because I misjudged his signal. He would spin in a circle or sniff the mat edge, and if I missed it, we both paid. Once I learned his tells, the pen bought me the time to scoop him up and carry him to the grass. A puppy play pen is not a substitute for watching your dog, it is the safety net that makes learning slower and kinder.

If your puppy wees the moment you put him down inside, you may have the pen too big, or you are waiting too long between trips. Shrink the space, tighten the schedule, and reward the outside wins heavily. I kept a little jar of training treats by the door so praise was instant.

Adding the kitten into the mix

Miso the rescue kitten watched all of this from the top of the bookshelf, unimpressed. When Wally was in his pen, she had the run of the flat and her food stayed safe up high. Slowly, with the pen door open and me right there, they met nose to nose. The pen let me control the pace so neither felt trapped. If you have a cat and a puppy, I genuinely think a pen is more useful for the cat's peace of mind than the dog's.

What I would tell a new puppy parent

Buy the pen before the puppy arrives, set it up as a happy place, and use it as a gentle boundary rather than a lock-up. Pair it with a boring, repeatable toilet schedule and you will be surprised how fast things click. Wally was mostly clean by fourteen weeks, and my rental carpet survived intact. For the cost of a soft foldable pen, that is a trade I would make every single time.

Frequently Asked Questions

Frequently asked questions

Is keeping a puppy in a play pen cruel?

Not at all. A pen is a safe den, not a punishment. Dogs like enclosed cosy spaces, and used kindly it protects your puppy from household dangers while you teach him the rules.

How big should a puppy toilet training pen be?

Big enough for a bed, water and a wee mat with space between them, but not so large he can sleep far from where he toilets. For a Cavoodle, a medium soft octagonal size works well.

How often should I take a young puppy outside?

At ten to twelve weeks, aim for a toilet trip every hour while awake, plus straight after waking, eating and play. Tight, predictable timing is what builds the habit.

What if my puppy wees inside the pen?

Stay calm, clean it well so the smell does not draw him back, and take him out a little sooner next time. Accidents mean the schedule slipped, not that the puppy is naughty.

Can I use the pen at night?

Yes. Wally slept in his pen beside my bed, which kept him safe and meant I heard him stir when he needed a late toilet trip. Move the mat closer to the bed if he fusses.

Will a pen help with my cat as well?

It helps by giving the cat a puppy-free flat while the dog is contained. Slow, supervised meet-ups with the pen as a buffer let them learn each other at a calm pace.
