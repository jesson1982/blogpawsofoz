Fetch and Rope: A Heeler's Field Test

By Mick O'DonnellJun 15, 2026Cat & Dog Toys2 min read

Short answer: for a working dog like Banjo, the solid rubber fetch toy is the only thing back from paddock and beach with teeth marks but no chunks missing. You'll want a decent woven rope tug too; it shrugged off mud so you just hose it off, while frisbees stay gold on grass and turn to kindling on bitumen. A stick was never the answer — splinters and a sharp end at speed settled that.

Banjo reckons a stick is the perfect toy. He's wrong — splinters, and he brings the sharp end to you at speed. So I ran a proper field test on fetch and rope gear that might actually survive a working dog with opinions.

What came back in one piece

The solid rubber fetch toy won. It floats, it bounces weird, and after a month of paddock and beach it's got teeth marks but no chunks missing. The rope tug surprised me — I expected it to fall apart, but a decent woven one shrugs off mud, and you just hose it off. Frisbees are great until they hit bitumen; on grass they're gold, on the road they're kindling.

ToyBest useSurvives
Rubber fetch toyOpen ground, waterJaws, sun, salt
Rope tugPark, trainingMud, washing, tugging
FrisbeeGrass, open paddockGrass only — not asphalt
Tennis ballQuick throwLess so — wears fast, watch the cover

One rule I'll state plain: a toy won't teach recall. Banjo learnt to come back on a lead and a lot of patience before I trusted him near the kangaroos. Build the manners, then the beach is yours.

And feel for heat. A rubber toy left in the ute up here can fry a dog's mouth — back of the hand first, always.

Frequently Asked Questions

Frequently asked questions

What's the best fetch toy for a strong dog?

A solid rubber one that floats and shows up against grass or water. Skip anything that shatters.

Are rope toys safe?

For tugging and fetching, yes. If he starts eating the fibres, swap to rubber.

Why not just use a stick?

Splinters and the sharp end. A stick thrown at speed isn't worth the vet trip.

Can dog toys get too hot?

They can. Feel with the back of your hand before he picks it up in summer.

Will my dog bring it back?

That's recall, not the toy. Teach come-back first, then add the throw.
