Cat Toys in Australia: What I'd Buy for a First Cat (and the Kmart Question)

By Hannah WrightJul 2, 2026Cat & Dog Toys2 min read

A first cat changes the house in the best way. It also triggers a trolley-load of toy aisles, half of which your cat will ignore. After fostering more than a few shy rescues, I've got a short list of what's actually worth the money — and a calm take on the big-box-versus-boutique debate.

Start with enrichment, not quantity

A cat doesn't need twenty toys. It needs a few that tap into how cats think: hide, stalk, pounce, chew. A wand for interaction, a tunnel or box for hiding, and something edible-on-a-string covers most of it. The goal is a tired, content cat, not a full basket.

If your cat is…Reach forWhy
A confident kittenWand, small tunnel, kickerBurns energy, teaches bite control
A shy rescueTunnel, puzzle feederSelf-led play, less pressure
An indoor adultWindow perch + wand + feederStops boredom, supports waistline
A speed-eaterPuzzle or slow feederTurns dinner into a job

The Kmart question, answered honestly

Kmart sells decent basics at prices that suit a first cat. For a $10 crinkle tunnel or a pack of balls, it's a sensible start. The catch is consistency: check stitching, avoid anything with small glued-on parts a kitten could swallow, and retire worn items. Boutique and specialist brands tend to last longer and use clearer materials — worth it for anything your cat will chew or wombat into daily. Neither is "better"; they're different tiers for different jobs.

If you like a surprise, a cat toy subscription box shipped in Australia is a fun way to rotate novelty without overbuying. Just watch for overlap — three boxes of the same feather wand helps nobody.

Frequently Asked Questions

Frequently asked questions

How many toys does a first cat need?

Four or five well-chosen ones beat twenty ignored ones. Rotate them weekly so they feel new.

Are Kmart cat toys safe?

Basics are fine if inspected. Avoid small detachable parts for kittens, and bin anything frayed.

What's the one toy I shouldn't skip?

A wand. It's the only toy that builds a bond through play, which matters most for a new cat.

Are subscription boxes worth it?

For novelty and gifting, yes. For everyday value, a small curated set does the job cheaper.

My cat ignores toys — what then?

Try a different prey style (wand vs tunnel vs puzzle) and play at dawn/dusk when cats are naturally active.

Should toys be cat-specific?

Mostly, yes. Dog toys can be too tough or have parts unsafe for a cat's mouth.
