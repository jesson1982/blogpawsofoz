Wash, Dry, Repeat: Keeping Cat Beds in Rotation

By Ruth AldertonSep 3, 2026Beds & Mats8 min read

Short answer: A multi cat home runs best on a steady wash rotation rather than a single big clean, because cats settle on beds that still carry their own smell. Keep two beds per cat plus a spare, wash on a two week cycle, and dry without heat so the familiar scent comes back. Ask your vet if a skin condition changes the routine.

I still remember the Monday after a long weekend at the cattery. Thirty cats, every bed stripped. I learned fast that you never pull every bed at once. I run the same habit at home on a smaller scale. The trick is not a perfect clean, it is a rhythm the cats can trust. Each bed goes out smelling like its cat and comes back washed to the same spot. When I washed everything in one Sunday blitz the cats paced the hallway two nights. A slow rotation kept them calm and my weekends free. That is the system I use, and it costs nothing but planning.

Why a wash rotation beats a deep clean

Pulling every bed at once feels productive, but it unsettles cats. In the cattery I stripped all beds between boarders for hygiene, yet I left a worn blanket in each pen so the next cat stepped into a familiar smell. At home there is no changeover gap, so the answer is a rotation that keeps one bed per cat in service while another sits in the wash or airing. The cat never loses the spot they claimed. A single big clean also leaves you short on bedding for a day or two, which is when cats start sleeping on the couch. A rhythm of small, frequent washes spreads the work and keeps every cat on their own bed. The beds wear evenly too. I rotate by pen at the cattery and by cat at home.

How many beds a multi cat home needs

The maths is simpler than people think. Give each cat two beds and keep one spare for the house. Two lets you wash one while the other stays in service, so the cat is never without a spot. The spare covers a sick wash, a chewed seam, or a weekend guest. In a two cat home that is five beds in play. At the cattery I ran close to seventy cushions for thirty cats because pens needed a spare set for the wash window. You do not need that scale at home, but the ratio holds. Buy beds you can actually wash, not just the pretty ones. A cheaper washable cushion you rotate beats a plush favourite that lives on the floor because it never fits the machine. Count your cats, double it, add one, and you have a rotation that survives a fortnight. When choosing, our washable cat beds cover styles that fit a home machine.

Running a two week wash cycle

I label beds with a week number using a laundry pen on the tag, then wash one cat's set every few days so the load never piles up. A true two week cycle means each bed is washed roughly every fourteen days, enough for a healthy indoor cat and gentle on the fabric. I wash on a warm cycle around forty degrees with plain detergent and no softener, because softener coats the fibres and the cats notice the change in feel. Hang the washed bed back in its own room, not outside where the neighbour's dog scent drifts in. The point is rhythm, not perfection. If a week slips, the cat is fine, but the pile of unwashed beds grows fast. I keep a basket by the machine so a swapped bed goes straight into the cycle.

  • Keep two beds per cat plus one spare for the whole house.
  • Wash on a warm forty degree cycle with plain detergent, no softener.
  • Air dry in the same room so the familiar scent stays put.
  • Swap beds on a set day so the rhythm never slips.
  • Retire beds with broken zips or torn liners you cannot mend.

Drying without a clothes dryer

Not every home has a dryer, and most cat beds do not love the heat anyway. I air dry everything. After the wash I give the bed a hard spin, then reshape it while damp so the filling sits even. On a sunny Sydney morning I lay beds flat on the line, but bring them in before the afternoon heat bakes the foam. In wet weather I use an indoor airer near a window. A bed that dries slow and flat holds its shape far better than one tumbled hot, and the scent comes back stronger when it dries in its own room. If you must use a dryer, pick the lowest heat and pull the bed out while still a little damp, then finish on the line. Hot tumble cycles wrecked half my old cattery cushions.

Why scent continuity keeps cats settled

Cats read the world through smell before they trust it. A bed carrying a cat's own scent says safe, mine, stay. When I strip a pen at the cattery and return a bed smelling of industrial wash only, the cat paces and scratches the door. The fix is to keep one small blanket unwashed through the change so the new bed still smells like home. At home the same rule applies. Do not wash every bed a cat owns in the same week. Leave the favourite, wash the spare, and the cat never loses their anchor. Scent continuity is why a slow rotation beats a blitz, and you avoid the two night hallway patrol I learned the hard way.

What to keep and what to retire

After a fortnight of rotation you learn which beds earn their place. Keep the ones that survived the wash, still hold their shape, and went back to the same cat without a fuss. Mend the ones with a loose seam or popped button before they become a hazard. Bin the beds with split foam you cannot resew, a zip that will not close, or a liner that holds odour after a proper wash. In the cattery I retired cushions the moment the inner gave way, because a lumpy bed starts a fight over the good one. At home the call is softer but the rule holds. A bed that no longer supports or smells clean is finished. Rotate the keepers, fix the menders, let the rest go without guilt.

Frequently Asked Questions

How often should I wash my cat's bed?

For a healthy indoor cat a two week wash is plenty, with a quick shake and spot clean in between. If your cat sheds heavily or shares the bed with others, move to weekly. Use a warm wash with plain detergent and skip softener. Ask your vet if a skin condition means more frequent washing is needed for your cat.

Can I put cat beds in the dryer?

You can, but keep the heat low and pull the bed out while still a little damp. Hot tumble cycles break down foam and shrink the cover, which is what ruined plenty of my cattery stock. Air drying flat in the room the bed lives in keeps the shape and brings the cat's scent back. A gentle finish on the line is kinder to the bed.

Why does my cat avoid a freshly washed bed?

Because it no longer smells like home. Cats trust scent first, and an industrial clean strips the smell they knew. Leave one item unwashed through the change, or wash only the spare so the favourite stays put. Within a day the bed picks up the cat's smell again and they return. Scent continuity matters more than a deep clean every time.

How many beds do I need for two cats?

Five is a good number: two per cat and one spare for the house. That lets you wash one set while the other stays in service, so neither cat loses their spot. The spare covers a slow wash, a chewed seam, or a visitor. Buy beds you can actually fit in the machine, because a pretty bed that never gets washed just collects hair.

What should I do with a bed my cat has outgrown?

Check the structure first. If the cover is sound and the foam still holds, wash it and keep it as a spare or a car bed. If the zip is gone or the liner holds odour after a proper wash, retire it. There is no shame in letting a worn bed go. Ask your vet before reusing a bed from a cat with a contagious condition.
