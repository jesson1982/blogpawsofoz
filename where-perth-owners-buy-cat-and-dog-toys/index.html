Where Perth Owners Buy Cat and Dog Toys: The Shelves Compared

By Maddie SorensenSep 5, 2026Cat & Dog Toys5 min read

The short version: Perth owners split between the big pet chains for range and warranty, the supermarkets for cheap impulse buys, and the weekend markets for handmade and novelty toys. The chains win on returns and tough chews, the markets win on character and cat specific finds, and the supermarkets win on price for things pets outgrow fast. I drove the metro area asking owners, because where you shop in Perth shapes what your pet ends up with.

The big pet chains around Perth

PETstock and Petbarn stores across the metro carry the widest range and stand behind warranties, which matters for a sixty dollar chew. Staff knowledge varies by store, but the returns path is the real draw. For a power chewer, this is where I send owners first.

The downside is price and the fact that the cheap novelty end is thinner than the supermarkets.

Supermarkets and discount stores

Kmart and Coles tick the budget box for crinkle toys, plush and kitten basics. Great for things a pet destroys in a week, less great for anything needing to last. Owners on a tight week top up here between chain visits.

  • Chains: range, warranty, tough chews.
  • Supermarkets: cheap, impulse, fast turnover.
  • Markets: handmade, cat specific, character.
  • Vet clinics: a small safe toy shelf, pricier.

Perth markets and small shops

The dog beaches and weekend markets around Fremantle and the northern suburbs turn up handmade ropes, cat tunnels and locally sewn mice you will not see in a chain. Quality is hit and miss, so I check stitching before buying, but the cat finds are genuinely different.

For a rescue kitten, the market mouse is often the first real favourite in our house.

What Perth owners told me

Most run a mix: chains for the keeper items, supermarkets for the passing fancies, markets for gifts. The heat matters too, owners near the coast buy more salt tolerant outdoor toys, while hills folk want shade friendly options. Local climate quietly drives the basket.

If you want to pair buys with the right toy type, our toy category breaks it down.

Climate quietly shapes the basket too. Coastal owners buy more salt tolerant rubber, hills owners want shade friendly gear, and city apartment folk lean to quiet and small. The same toy sells differently three suburbs apart, which is why a single national list never tells the whole story.

I also ask owners what they regret buying. The common answer is a bulk set of untested shapes that clogged the cupboard. Most wish they had bought two good things and watched, which is the same advice I give at the basin during a groom. Local knowledge beats a catalogue.

Markets suit gifts and first toys, while chains suit the items you want to last and return. Keeping both in mind saves a drive and a dud. The best shoppers I meet mix all three without loyalty to any one shelf.

If you are new to Perth, ask at the off leash beach which store locals trust. The answer is usually practical, not promotional, and it will save you the trial and error we all went through first.

The takeaway for a new Perth owner is boring but true, buy the keeper items where you can return them, and treat the markets and supermarkets as the fun fringe. Your pet will not remember the shop, only the toy, so spend the energy on the match, not the mileage.

If you are on a budget, the supermarkets and markets cover the fun, and one good chew from a chain covers the serious wear. Spreading the spend like that got most of the owners I spoke to through a year without a single toy regret. The shelf you choose matters less than buying the right toy for the right pet at the right time.

Frequently Asked Questions

Where is the best place to buy dog toys in Perth?

For tough chews and anything you want warranted, the big chains like PETstock and Petbarn. For cheap toys a dog outgrows fast, the supermarkets. Markets are best for character pieces. Most Perth owners mix all three depending on the toy and the week.

Are Perth markets good for cat toys?

Surprisingly yes for novelty and handmade finds, cat tunnels, sewn mice and small wand toys you will not see in chains. Check the stitching and avoid glued on bits. They make great first toys for a rescue kitten without spending much.

Is it worth driving to a chain for one toy?

If it is a keeper chew or a warranty item, yes, the returns path saves money long term. For a plush a cat bats once, the local supermarket is fine. Match the trip to the toy's lifespan, not the habit.

Do coastal and hills owners buy differently?

They do. Coastal owners lean to salt tolerant outdoor toys and rinse friendly rubber, while hills and hotter inland owners want shade friendly and heat stable options. The Perth climate quietly shapes the basket more than people expect.

Can I trust supermarket pet toys?

For gentle play and short term use, yes. Check for glue on small parts and a chemical smell, and retire anything that splits. They are fine for a kitten or a bat and release cat, less so for a power chewer who needs a warranted toy.
