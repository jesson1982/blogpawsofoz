Weight Limits and Cat Size: Matching a Perch to Your Cat

By Tessa NgataSep 2, 2026Cat Window Perches8 min read

Short answer: a perch weight limit is a static load figure, not a promise about how a perch behaves in a real lounge room. Body weight is only part of the load, because landing force, build and daily habits all add to it. Match the rating to your cat with a sensible margin, then judge the perch on how it actually performs.

Last month a bloke brought in a solid grey cat for a routine weigh-in and mentioned that his window hammock had started to sag. The perch box had promised a generous load figure and the owner had trusted it. Nobody had weighed the cat against that figure. On the scale he came in at just over seven kilograms. The perch he had been sold was rated for six. That gap is common and rarely dishonest. Ratings get skimmed, cats get weighed at clinics, and the two numbers almost never meet in the same room. Weight and load sit close to my day job, and this is the conversation I wish happened before the first hole gets drilled.

What a weight rating actually assumes

A load rating describes a condition that almost never occurs in a house: a static weight, spread evenly across the platform, on a mount fitted exactly as instructed, on day one. That is what the figure was measured against, and it is a fair thing to measure. Your cat delivers something different. She lands on one edge, she lounges with her back half hanging off, she launches sideways off the far end, and she does all of it on a mount you fitted yourself. None of that shows up in the rating, which says nothing about time either. Fabric stretches, suction rubber hardens, and screws loosen a quarter turn at a time, so the perch that held in autumn can feel different by the end of a Hobart summer.

How a cat's build changes the load

Two cats can weigh the same and load a perch very differently. A tall, leggy cat spreads her weight along a long body and tends to step on. A short, dense cat drops the same kilograms into a smaller patch of platform and often lands with a thump. A cat launching from the sill next door arrives with the platform already moving. A cat that drops from a bookshelf above lands from higher again, and the mount takes that arrival all at once. In rehab I watch cats move between surfaces, and the ones with stiff joints or cautious natures are usually the heaviest landers, because they step and drop rather than flow. The rating on the box cannot see any of this. You can.

What an overloaded perch looks like

Overload rarely announces itself with a crash, only small changes that are easy to explain away. A suction pad develops a pale ring at one edge and lifts there first. A fabric hammock sits a little lower each month until the cat is practically on the glass. Bracket screws develop a visible angle where they used to sit square. The frame creaks under one particular cat but not the other. Any one of these is worth acting on. The useful habit is a monthly hand check: press down with the flat of your palm, put most of your weight through it, and watch what moves. A perch that shifts under a careful human hand is not ready for a flying seven-kilo cat.

  • Weigh your cat before you shop, not after the perch is up.
  • A rating describes a static load, not a landing, a pounce or a launch.
  • Choose a figure comfortably above your cat's weight, not one that just clears it.
  • Two cats sharing a perch share one rating, not two.
  • A sagging hammock or lifting suction edge is a warning, not a quirk.

Matching the rating to your cat

Start with a real weight, not a guess. Step on the bathroom scale holding the cat, step on alone, and subtract. Then add a margin, because the rating must cover landings and lounging, not just standing still. A cat that sits close to the limit on paper is over it in practice most days. Breed matters too. Maine coons, ragdolls and solid moggies routinely outgrow ratings written for an average house cat, and a kitten outgrows her figures within months, so buy for the adult she will become. In a two-cat house, rate the perch for both cats plus a margin, because one rating carries everyone who uses it. Checking the mount without special tools

None of this needs a toolbox. Once a month, look at the fixings before you clean the window: screws should sit square and tight, suction cups should show a full even ring with no pale edges, and brackets should not rock under hand pressure. Clean glass matters more for suction than any brand claim, so wipe it properly and refit the cup from the centre outward. Temperature is the quiet variable. A cold Hobart morning followed by a warm afternoon moves glass and frames a fraction, and that is often the day a sound cup lets go, so re-check after sharp swings and after any house move. When the perch is new, photograph the mount with the cat on it. Months later that photo will tell you whether the perch has started to sit lower, tilt or drift.

When the numbers change under you

Both figures in this equation drift. Cats gain weight quietly through adulthood, and some slim down on a plan your vet has set, so a rating that suited last winter may be tight now. Fixings loosen over the years no matter how well they were fitted. If you move house, treat the perch as new: different glass, different wall, different framing, and the old assumptions do not carry over. The habit that keeps all of this honest is simple. Weigh the cat whenever she is in for anything else anyway, glance at the mount monthly, and re-run the comparison once or twice a year. It takes minutes, and it is the difference between a perch rated for your cat and one that merely has a rating.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the weight limit on a cat perch an official standard?

No. It is the manufacturer's own figure, and there is no single independent standard across brands. Some makers test carefully and print conservatively; others print an optimistic number. Treat the rating as one useful data point rather than a guarantee, and let the perch's real behaviour carry more weight than the box.

My cat sits right at the stated limit. Is that actually fine?

On paper it is within spec, in practice it is tight. The rating assumes a still, evenly spread cat, and yours will spend part of every day landing, shifting or springing away. If the perch is already up, check it weekly at first and watch for sag. If you have not bought yet, pick a figure with room above her weight.

Can two cats share a perch rated for one?

Only if their combined weight sits well inside the rating. Two cats together load a small platform in ways no rating measured: two bodies landing, shifting and launching in different directions. If your cats genuinely share, choose a wide perch rated for their combined weight with a margin, and check the fixings more often.

How do I weigh my cat at home without clinic scales?

Use the bathroom scale method. Step on alone, note the figure, then step on again holding the cat and subtract. It is rough, but it is honest enough for choosing a perch, and better than guessing. If you want a precise number, most vet clinics will weigh a cat quickly while you are there anyway.

Are suction mounts ever suitable for a heavier cat?

Sometimes, but they ask the most of the rating, since the whole load hangs on adhesion. Fresh clean glass, a full even seal and regular checks matter more than any printed figure. For a big cat I would rather see the load carried by screws into solid framing, with suction left to lighter duties.
