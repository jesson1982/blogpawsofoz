Multi-Cat Window Perch: Two Cats, One View

By Sophie BennettJul 8, 2026Cat Window Perches6 min read

Short answer: our standard twin-suction hammock is built for one small or medium cat at a time, so two cats need two perches spaced apart on the same window. When I mind my neighbour's tabby Biscuit, I stick a second one beside Miso's and they share the light a paw's width apart without a squabble. You should step up to a reinforced four-suction model for any cat above about 15 kg, because the standard one is not rated for heavy breeds.

I only have one cat, Miso, but plenty of friends in my Perth apartment block have two, and the question I get asked most is whether a single window hammock can cope with more than one feline. The short answer is that our standard twin-suction hammock is made for one small or medium cat at a time, but a window can absolutely host more than one cat if you plan the space. When I look after my neighbour's tabby, Biscuit, for a weekend, I borrow a second hammock and stick it beside Miso's. Watching them both soak up the same patch of light, a paw's width apart but never squabbling, is honestly the cosiest thing in my flat.

Why two cats need two spaces

Cats are territorial in a quiet way. They like having a spot that is clearly theirs, even when they are happy to be near another cat. Squeezing two cats onto one small hammock may look cute in a photo, but it can spark a struggle over warmth and position, especially at a window where the best sun moves through the day. Giving each cat its own perch respects that instinct and keeps the peace. Miso guards her bed with a slow blink, not a hiss, but I would never test her patience by crowding her.

In a rental, the bonus is that suction perches leave no mark, so you can run two or three across a wide window and still get your bond back. I have seen a clever setup in a friend's lounge with three hammocks stacked at different heights on one sliding door, each cat claiming a level like tenants in a tiny feline apartment block.

Spacing the perches on one window

The trick is to spread the beds so the cats are not forced to touch if they do not want to. On a standard 90 cm wide window I can fit two of our 30×45 cm hammocks side by side with a small gap, and both stay on the glass because each has its own twin-suction mount. For a bigger picture window you might place one high and one low, which also suits cats with different mobility or confidence levels. A kitten like Miso prefers the lower, easier hop, while an older cat may like the higher lookout.

I also think about the view. Birds gather at the same feeder outside my kitchen window, so both perches face that spot. When the cats are oriented the same way, they tend to watch together rather than watch each other, which lowers the chance of tension. It is a small thing, but it works.

Weight and the honest limit

Our 1688-sourced twin-suction hammock is suited to small and medium cats up to about 15 kg each. That is per hammock, not combined. If you have a large cat of 15 kg or more, our standard model is not the right call for them; I would steer you to a reinforced four-suction or steel-cable design that spreads the load. I would never claim our everyday hammock holds a heavy large breed, because saying so would put a cat at risk and a renter's glass at risk too.

Introducing a second cat to the window

If you already have a perch and add a second cat, let the newcomer explore the empty spare hammock while your resident cat is in another room. Swap a blanket between the two beds so they share scent before they share a window. When both are calm, let them use the perches together with you nearby. I did this with Biscuit and Miso and within an hour they were both asleep, which is about as relaxed as two cats from different homes ever get.

Wally, my Cavoodle, is fascinated by the cats at the window and would press his nose to the glass if I let him. I keep him on the floor and a step back, because a puppy lunging at the window can startle a cat mid-hop. The perch is a calm zone, and the dog learns that the glass is not his to patrol.

Keeping it clean and checked

Two cats means twice the fur and twice the chance of a knocked cup. I wipe the glass and re-press each suction cup every week, and I wash the fabric beds on a gentle cycle. In the warm Perth months I crack the window for a sea breeze, which the cats adore, but I make sure the latch is secure so the pane cannot shift under them. Little habits like these keep a multi-cat window setup safe without any drama.

Frequently asked questions

Can two cats share one window hammock?

Our standard twin-suction hammock is built for one small or medium cat at a time. Two cats are happier and safer on separate perches placed side by side or at different heights on the same window.

How many hammocks fit on one window?

On a 90 cm window you can fit two of our 30×45 cm beds with a gap; a wider picture window can take three. Each hammock has its own suction mount, so spacing is about glass width and your cats' comfort, not a shared frame.

What weight can a multi-cat setup handle?

Each standard hammock suits a cat up to about 15 kg. The limit is per hammock, not combined. For a cat of 15 kg or more, choose a reinforced four-suction or steel-cable model rather than our standard one.

Will my cats fight over the best sun spot?

They might jostle at first. Give each cat its own perch and orient both toward the same view. Most cats settle quickly when they each have a clear, equal claim to the light.

Is a suction setup safe for a rental with two cats?

Yes, as long as the glass is clean and dry and you check the cups weekly. No drilling means no bond risk, and you can remove or reposition the perches when you move out.

My second cat is a large breed. What then?

Skip our standard hammock for that cat and use a reinforced four-suction or steel-cable perch. We do not claim the standard model holds heavy large breeds, and your cat deserves gear rated for its size.
